## Introduction to Poverty and Unemployment
------------------------------------------

Poverty and unemployment are two of the most pressing issues faced by economies worldwide. **Poverty** refers to the state of being unable to afford the necessities of life, such as food, shelter, clothing, and healthcare. On the other hand, **unemployment** is a situation where an individual is willing and able to work but is unable to find a job. The relationship between poverty and unemployment is complex, with each issue exacerbating the other.

### Measuring Poverty: The Poverty Line
--------------------------------------

The poverty line is a hypothetical line that separates the poor from the non-poor. It is typically measured in terms of income or expenditure. In India, the poverty line is defined as the minimum level of income required to meet the basic needs of an individual. The **Headcount Ratio (HCR)** is a widely used measure to estimate the percentage of population living below the poverty line:

HCR = $\frac{	ext{Number of persons below the poverty line}$}{	ext{Total population}} \times 100

### Types of Unemployment
-------------------------

Unemployment can be categorized into several types, including:

1. **Frictional Unemployment**: This type of unemployment occurs due to the time gap between jobs. It is a temporary phase where an individual is transitioning from one job to another.
2. **Structural Unemployment**: This type of unemployment arises due to a mismatch between the skills of the workforce and the requirements of the jobs available.
3. **Cyclical Unemployment**: This type of unemployment is caused by fluctuations in the business cycle. During a recession, many businesses reduce production, leading to layoffs and increased unemployment.

## 3 Solved PYQs
----------------

To help you better understand the concepts of poverty and unemployment, let's take a look at three solved previous year questions (PYQs):

1. **Question**: What is the difference between absolute poverty and relative poverty?
 * **Answer**: Absolute poverty refers to a fixed poverty line, whereas relative poverty is defined in relation to the overall standard of living in a society.

2. **Question**: What are the main causes of unemployment in India?
 * **Answer**: The main causes of unemployment in India include a lack of job opportunities, inadequate education and skills, and demographic factors such as population growth.

3. **Question**: How can poverty be measured?
 * **Answer**: Poverty can be measured using various methods, including the headcount ratio, poverty gap index, and Gini coefficient.

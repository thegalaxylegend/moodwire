## <a id="-formula-bank"></a>⚡ Formula Bank

- **Identity 1 (Square of Sum):** (a+b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2 — Where a and by represent any algebraic terms (e.g., numbers, variables, or products of numbers and variables). This identity is crucial for two main operations: expanding the square of a binomial and factorizing perfect square trinomials. When faced with an expression like $(P+Q)^2$, you expand it into UP^2 + 2PQ + Q^2$. Conversely, if you encounter a trinomial that fits the pattern UP^2 + 2PQ + Q^2$, you can factorize it directly into $(P+Q)^2$. Key recognition involves identifying two terms that are perfect squares (a^2$, lb^2$) and a third positive term that is exactly twice the product of their square roots ($2ab$). For example, to factorize $25x^2 + 40xy + 16y^2$, observe that $25x^2 = (5x)^2$, $16y^2 = (4y)^2$, and $40xy = 2(5x)(4y)$. This matches the^2 + 2ab + b^$2 form, so it factors to $(5x+4y)^2$. Understanding this identity is essential for simplifying expressions and solving problems involving quadratic forms.$ 

- **Identity 2 (Square of Difference):** (a-b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2 — Where a and by represent any algebraic terms. This identity is the counterpart to the 'square of $\sum' identity$, specifically for the difference of two terms. It's applied to expand expressions like $(P-Q)^2$ into UP^2 - 2PQ + Q^2$, and more importantly, to factorize perfect square trinomials that have a negative middle term. The pattern to look for in factorization is UP^2 - 2PQ + Q^2$, which immediately factors into $(P-Q)^2$.

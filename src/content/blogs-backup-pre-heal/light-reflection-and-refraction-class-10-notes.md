## <a id="-formula-bank"></a>⚡ Formula Bank

### <a id="reflection-formulas"></a>Reflection Formulas

- **Law of Reflection:** $\theta_i = \theta_r$ — angle of incidence equals angle of reflection

- **Mirror Formula:** $\frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f}$ — for object distance $u$, image distance $v$, and focal length $f$

- **Magnification:** $m = \frac{h'}{h} = -\frac{v}{u}$ — for image height $h'$, object height $h$, image distance $v$, and object distance $u$
Examiner's Trap: Be careful with the signs of distances and heights in mirror formula and magnification.

### <a id="refraction-formulas"></a>Refraction Formulas

- **Snell's Law:** $n_1 \\\\sin{\theta_1} = n_2 \\\\sin{\theta_2}$ — for refractive indices $n_1$ and $n_2$, and angles of incidence $\theta_1$ and refraction $\theta_2$

- **Refractive Index:** $n = \frac{c}{v}$ — for speed of light in vacuum $c$ and speed of light in medium $v$

- **Lens Maker's Formula:** $\frac{1}{f} = (n-1) \left( \frac{1}{R_1} - \frac{1}{R_2} \r\r\r\right)$ — for focal length $f$, refractive index $n$, and radii of curvature $R_1$ and $R_2$
Examiner's Trap: Remember that the lens maker's formula assumes the lens is in air.

### <a id="total-internal-reflection-formulas"></a>Total Internal Reflection Formulas

- **Critical Angle:** $\\\\sin{\theta_c} = \frac{n_2}{n_1}$ — for critical angle $\theta_c$, and refractive indices $n_1$ and $n_2$

- **Total Internal Reflection Condition:** $\theta_1 > \theta_c$ — for angle of incidence $\theta_1$ and critical angle $\theta_c$
Examiner's Trap: Ensure you understand the conditions for total internal reflection to occur.

### <a id="prism-formulas"></a>Prism Formulas

- **Deviation by a Prism:** $\delta = i + e - A$ — for angle of deviation $\delta$, angle of incidence $i$, angle of emergence $e$, and angle of prism $A$

- **Dispersion by a Prism:** $\delta = \frac{n-1}{2} A$ — for angle of deviation $\delta$, refractive index $n$, and angle of prism $A$
Examiner's Trap: Be aware of the difference between deviation and dispersion in the context of prisms.

### <a id="lens-formulas"></a>Lens Formulas

- **Lens Equation:** $\frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f}$ — for image distance $v$, object distance $u$, and focal length $f$

- **Power of a Lens:** $P = \frac{1}{f}$ — for power $P$ and focal length $f$
Examiner's Trap: Remember that the sign convention for lenses is opposite to that for mirrors.

### <a id="optical-fiber-formulas"></a>Optical Fiber Formulas

- **Acceptance Angle:** $\\\\sin{\theta_a} = \frac{n_2}{n_1}$ — for acceptance angle $\theta_a$, and refractive indices $n_1$ and $n_2$

- **Numerical Aperture:** $NA = \sqrt{n_1^2 - n_2^2}$ — for numerical aperture $NA$, and refractive indices $n_1$ and $n_2$
Examiner's Trap: Understand the concept of numerical aperture and its relation to acceptance angle.

### <a id="decision-table-for-formulas"></a>Decision Table for Formulas

| Calculating focal length of a lens | $\frac{1}{f} = (n-1) \left( \frac{1}{R_1} - \frac{1}{R_2} \r\r\r\right)$ |

## <a id="-the-5-mistakes-that-cost-marks"></a>🪤 The 5 Mistakes That Cost Marks

### <a id="-the-5-mistakes-that-cost-marks"></a>🪤 The 5 Mistakes That Cost Marks

- **Mistake 1 — Sign Convention Blunder:**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** Assigning positive or negative signs to focal length, object distance, or image distance based on intuition rather than strict Cartesian sign convention. For example, for a concave mirror with focal length $15 \text{ cm}$, writing $f = +15 \text{ cm}$ instead of $f = -15 \text{ cm}$. Or for an object placed to the left of the mirror/lens (standard position), using $u = +20 \text{ cm}$.

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** Consistent application of the Cartesian Sign Convention:
 

- The pole (for mirrors) or optical centre (for lenses) is the origin $(0,0)$.

- The principal axis is the x-axis.

- Incident [light](/blog/physics-heat-light-class-11-revision-notes-jee-neet) travels from left to right. Distances measured in the direction of incident [light](/blog/physics-heat-light-class-11-revision-notes-jee-neet) are positive. Distances measured opposite to the direction of incident [light](/blog/physics-heat-light-class-11-revision-notes-jee-neet) are negative.

- Distances measured above the principal axis (upwards) are positive. Distances measured below the principal axis (downwards) are negative.

- Object distance $u$: Always negative for real objects placed to the left.

- Focal length $f$: Concave mirror/lens has negative focal length. Convex mirror/lens has positive focal length.

- Image distance $v$: Real images (formed on the right of lenses, left of mirrors) have positive $v$. Virtual images (formed on the left of lenses, right of mirrors) have negative $v$.

- 💸 **Marks lost:** 2–3 marks in numerical problems. An incorrect sign propagates errors, leading to wrong final answers for image position, nature, and magnification.

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** "U is Always N. F is Concave-N, Convex-P. V is Real-P, Virtual-N." (Object distance $u$ is always negative. Focal length $f$ is Negative for Concave, Positive for Convex. Image distance $v$ is Positive for Real, Negative for Virtual).

- **Mistake 2 — Ray Diagram Chaos:**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** Drawing principal rays incorrectly (e.g.

- a ray parallel to the principal axis not passing through the focus after reflection/refraction), omitting arrows on rays, drawing virtual rays as solid lines, or failing to clearly label the object and image. The image point might not be clearly marked where rays actually intersect (or appear to intersect).

- ✅ **What examiners expect:**
 

- At least two principal rays drawn accurately using a ruler.

- Arrows clearly marked on all rays to indicate the direction of [light](/blog/physics-heat-light-class-11-revision-notes-jee-neet) propagation.

- Virtual rays (extensions of reflected/refracted rays) drawn as dashed lines. Real rays drawn as solid lines.

- Clear labeling of the object (e.g.

- AB) and the image (e.g.

- A'B').

- The image formation point (where rays actually meet or appear to meet) must be distinct.

- 💸 **Marks lost:** 2–3 marks in ray diagram construction questions. Partial marks might be awarded for some correct rays, but accuracy is key.

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** "Ruler, Arrows, Dashed Virtual, Label A'B'". Use a ruler for straight lines. Add arrows to show light direction. Virtual extensions are dashed. Clearly label the object and the image.

- **Mistake 3 — Formula Swap (Mirror vs.

- **Lens):**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** ** Interchanging the mirror formula and the lens formula. For instance, using $\frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f}$ for a lens numerical, or using $\frac{1}${v} 

- $\frac{1}{u}$ = $\frac{1}${f}$ for a mirror numerical.$

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** Correct application of the specific formula for the given optical device:
 

- **Mirror Formula:**
 \frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f}

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** "M+L-": Mirror formula uses a PLUS sign between $\frac{1}{v}$ and $\frac{1}{u}$. Lens formula uses a MINUS sign.

- **Mistake 4 — Power Calculation Unit Error:**

For example, if $f = 20 \text{ cm}$, a student might write $P = \frac{1}{20} = 0.05 \text{ D}$, which is incorrect. Another common error is using an incorrect sign for the power (e.g.

- positive power for a concave lens).

## <a id="-the-one-thing-most-students-get-wrong"></a>🧠 The One Thing Most Students Get Wrong

### <a id="the-misconception-what-85-believe"></a>The misconception (what 85% believe):
Students consistently misapply a single rule for image distance ($v$) to determine if an image is real or virtual, ignoring the critical distinction between mirrors and lenses under the New Cartesian Sign Convention. They often memorize that $v > 0$ implies a virtual image, or $v < 0$ implies a real image, then use this indiscriminately. Simultaneously, they frequently disconnect the sign of magnification ($M$) from the image's real/virtual nature, leading to fundamental errors in image characterization.

### <a id="the-reality-what-99-know"></a>The reality (what 99% know):
The sign of image distance ($v$) for determining real or virtual images is **device-dependent**. However, the sign of magnification ($M$) **universally** dictates if an image is erect or inverted, and this directly correlates with its real/virtual nature across all optical devices (mirrors and lenses).

* **For Mirrors (Concave, Convex):**
 * Images formed in front of the mirror (where reflected rays actually converge): $v < 0 \implies$ **Real Image**.
 * Images formed behind the mirror (where reflected rays appear to diverge from): $v > 0 \implies$ **Virtual Image**.

* **For Lenses (Convex, Concave):**
 * Images formed on the opposite side of the lens (where refracted rays actually converge): $v > 0 \implies$ **Real Image**.
 * Images formed on the same side as the object (where refracted rays appear to diverge from): $v < 0 \implies$ **Virtual Image**.

* **Universal Truths (Applicable to ALL Mirrors and Lenses):**
 * **Real images are ALWAYS inverted.** If an image is real, its magnification $M$ must be negative ($M < 0$).
 * **Virtual images are ALWAYS erect.** If an image is virtual, its magnification $M$ must be positive ($M > 0$).

### <a id="the-diagnostic-question"></a>The diagnostic question:
An object is placed in front of a concave mirror. The image formed is found to be behind the mirror and erect. What is the nature of the image and the sign of its magnification ($M$)?
(A) Real and $M > 0$
(B) Real and $M < 0$
(C) Virtual and $M > 0$
(D) Virtual and $M < 0$

* If you answered **(A), (B), or (D)**: you have the misconception → fix: Re-evaluate the definitions of real/virtual images for mirrors based on the side of formation, and internalize the universal relationship that virtual images are always erect, which directly implies $M > 0$.

* If you answered **(C)**: you are in the top 5% → now extend this: A convex lens forms an image with an image distance $v = -30 \, \text{cm}$. Determine the nature of this image and the sign of its magnification ($M$). (Answer: For a lens, $v < 0$ means the image is formed on the same side as the object, making it virtual. Virtual images are always erect, so $M > 0$.)

### <a id="how-to-never-forget-this"></a>How to never forget this:
Use the "MR. VILE" mnemonic. It encodes both the device-specific $v$ sign and the universal $M$ sign.

* **M**irrors:
 * **R**eal images are in front ($v < 0$).
 * **V**irtual images are behind ($v > 0$).

* **L**enses:
 * **R**eal images are on the opposite side ($v > 0$).
 * **V**irtual images are on the same side ($v < 0$).

* **I**nverted: **R**eal images ($M < 0$).

* **E**rect: **V**irtual images ($M > 0$).

This ensures you correctly link $v$ to device type and $M$ to image orientation, which then locks in the real/virtual nature.

## <a id="-ayushs-note"></a>👁️ Ayush's Note

### <a id="highyield-reflection-concepts"></a>High-Yield Reflection Concepts

* **Laws of Reflection:**
 * Angle of incidence equals angle of reflection: $ \angle i = \angle r $
 * Incident ray, reflected ray, and normal to the surface at the point of incidence all lie in the same plane.
 * These laws are valid for all types of reflecting surfaces, including spherical mirrors.

* **Spherical Mirrors – Key Terminology & Sign Conventions:**
 * **Pole (P):** Center of the spherical mirror.
 * **Center of Curvature (C):** Center of the sphere from which the mirror is cut. Radius of curvature ($R$) is distance PC.
 * **Principal Focus (F):** Point on the principal axis where rays parallel to the principal axis converge (concave) or appear to diverge from (convex) after reflection. Focal length ($f$) is distance PF.
 * **Relation:** $ R = 2f $. This is a crucial relation for numericals.
 * **Principal Axis:** Line joining P and C.
 * **New Cartesian Sign Conventions (CRITICAL for numericals):**
 * Origin at Pole (P).
 * Principal axis is X-axis.
 * Incident light travels from left to right.
 * Distances measured right of P are positive. Distances measured left of P are negative.
 * Heights measured upwards (above principal axis) are positive. Heights measured downwards (below principal axis) are negative.
 * **Specifics:**
 * Object distance ($u$) is almost always negative (object usually placed left).
 * Focal length ($f$): Concave mirror is negative, Convex mirror is positive.
 * Radius of curvature ($R$): Concave mirror is negative, Convex mirror is positive.

* **Mirror Formula:**
 \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u} 
 * Where $u$ = object distance, $v$ = image distance, $f$ = focal length.
 * **Mandatory:** Apply sign conventions strictly for $u, v, f$ when substituting values.

* **Magnification ($m$):**
  m = $\frac{\text{Height of image}$ (h')}{$\text{Height of object}$ (h)} = -$\frac{v}{u}$ 
 * **Interpretation of $m$:**
 * $|m| > 1$: Image is magnified.
 * $|m| < 1$: Image is diminished.
 * $|m| = 1$: Image is same size.
 * $m > 0$ (positive): Image is virtual and erect.
 * $m < 0$ (negative): Image is real and inverted.
 * **Crucial:** Sign of $m$ directly tells you image nature (erect/inverted) and its relation to real/virtual.

* **Ray Diagrams for Concave Mirror (High-Yield Cases):**
 * **Rule 1:** Ray parallel to principal axis passes through F after reflection.
 * **Rule 2:** Ray passing through F becomes parallel to principal axis after reflection.
 * **Rule 3:** Ray passing through C reflects along the same path.
 * **Rule 4:** Ray incident obliquely to P is reflected obliquely, making equal angles with the principal axis.
 * **Cases to master (Image Characteristics):**
 * **Object at infinity:** Image at F, Real, Inverted, Highly Diminished. (Used in solar furnaces).
 * **Object beyond C:** Image between F and C, Real, Inverted, Diminished.
 * **Object at C:** Image at C, Real, Inverted, Same Size. (Crucial for understanding how C acts as a center of symmetry).
 * **Object between F and C:** Image beyond C, Real, Inverted, Magnified.
 * **Object at F:** Image at infinity, Real, Inverted, Highly Magnified. (Used in headlights, searchlights).
 * **Object between P and F:** Image behind mirror, Virtual, Erect, Magnified. (Used as shaving mirrors, dentist mirrors).
 * **Key takeaway:** Concave mirrors form real and inverted images for objects beyond F, and a virtual and erect image only when the object is between P and F.

* **Ray Diagrams for Convex Mirror (Simpler, but commonly tested for properties):**
 * **Rule 1:** Ray parallel to principal axis appears to diverge from F after reflection.
 * **Rule 2:** Ray directed towards F becomes parallel to principal axis after reflection.
 * **Rule 3:** Ray directed towards C reflects along the same path.
 * **Cases to master (Image Characteristics):**
 * **Object at infinity:** Image at F (behind mirror), Virtual, Erect, Highly Diminished.
 * **Object anywhere between P and infinity:** Image between P and F (behind mirror), Virtual, Erect, Diminished.
 * **Key takeaway:** Convex mirrors *always* form virtual, erect, and diminished images. This is why they are used as rearview mirrors.

* **Applications of Spherical Mirrors:**
 * **Concave:** Shaving mirrors, dentist mirrors (magnified virtual image), headlights, searchlights, solar furnaces (real, inverted, highly magnified/diminished images depending on use).
 * **Convex:** Rear-view mirrors in vehicles (wider field of view, always erect image), shop security mirrors.

### <a id="highyield-refraction-concepts"></a>High-Yield Refraction Concepts

* **Laws of Refraction:**
 * Incident ray, refracted ray, and normal to the interface at the point of incidence all lie in the same plane.
 * **Snell's Law:** For a given pair of media and a given colour of light, the ratio of the sine of the angle of incidence to the sine of the angle of refraction is constant.
 \frac{\\\\sin i}{\\\\sin r} = \text{constant} = n_{21} 
 * Where $n_{21}$ is the refractive index of medium 2 with respect to medium 1.
 * **Optical Density:** Denser medium (higher $n$) means light bends towards the normal. Rarer medium (lower $n$) means light bends away from the normal.
 * Light from rarer to denser: $r < i$.
 * Light from denser to rarer: $r > i$.

* **Refractive Index ($n$):**
 * $ n = \frac{\text{Speed of light in vacuum (or air)} (c)}{\text{Speed of light in medium} (v)} $
 * Absolute refractive index ($n_m$): $ n’m = \frac{c}{v’m} $.
 * Relative refractive index ($n_{21}$): $ n_{21} = \frac{n_2}{n_1} = \frac{v_1}{v_2} $. (Refractive index of medium 2 with respect to medium 1).
 * **Inverse Relation:** $ n_{12} = \frac{1}{n_{21}} $.
 * No units, it's a ratio. Value is always $\ge 1$.

* **Refraction through a Rectangular Glass Slab:**
 * Emergent ray is parallel to the incident ray.
 * **Lateral Displacement:** Perpendicular distance between the incident ray and the emergent ray.
 * No angular deviation. This is a common conceptual question.

* **Spherical Lenses – Key Terminology & Sign Conventions:**
 * **Optical Centre (O):** Central point of the lens. Ray passing through O goes undeviated.
 * **Principal Focus (F):** For a convex lens, rays parallel to the principal axis converge at F on the other side. For a concave lens, rays parallel to the principal axis appear to diverge from F on the same side. Lenses have two principal foci, $F_1$ and $F_2$.
 * **Focal Length ($f$):** Distance from O to F.
 * **New Cartesian Sign Conventions (CRITICAL for numericals):**
 * Origin at Optical Centre (O).
 * Principal axis is X-axis.
 * Incident light travels from left to right.
 * Distances measured right of O are positive. Distances measured left of O are negative.
 * Heights measured upwards (above principal axis) are positive. Heights measured downwards (below principal axis) are negative.
 * **Specifics:**
 * Object distance ($u$) is almost always negative.
 * Focal length ($f$): Convex lens is positive, Concave lens is negative.

* **Lens Formula:**
 \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} 

-    $\frac{1}${u} 
 * Where $u$ = object distance, $v$ = image distance, $f$ = focal length.
 * **Mandatory:** Apply sign conventions strictly for $u, v, f$ when substituting values.

* **Magnification ($m$):**
  m = $\frac{\text{Height of image}$ (h')}{$\text{Height of object}$ (h)} = $\frac{v}{u}$ 
 * **Interpretation of $m$:** Same as for mirrors, but sign correlation with $v/u$ changes.
 * $m > 0$ (positive): Image is virtual and erect.
 * $m < 0$ (negative): Image is real and inverted.

* **Power of a Lens ($P$):**
 P = \frac{1}{f} 
 * Where $f$ is the focal length in **metres**.
 * Unit: Dioptre (D). $1 \text{ D} = 1 \text{ m}^{-1}$.
 * Convex lens: Positive power (converging).
 * Concave lens: Negative power (diverging).
 * **Combination of Lenses (if asked):** $ P_{net} = P_1 + P_2 + P_3 + \dots $

### <a id="-mustknow-facts"></a>🧠 Must-Know Facts

- The refractive index of a medium is the ratio of the speed of light in vacuum to the speed of light in the medium

- The focal length of a convex lens is positive, while that of a concave lens is negative

- The power of a lens is the reciprocal of its focal length

### <a id="-never-forget"></a>🚫 Never Forget

- ❌ Assuming the speed of light in all media is the same → ✅ Using the formula $v = \frac{c}{\mu}$ to calculate the speed of light in different media

- ❌ Forgetting to consider the sign conventions for lenses → ✅ Using the correct sign conventions for convex and concave lenses

### <a id="-if-you-can-only-remember-one-thing"></a>🎯 If you can only remember ONE thing:
The law of refraction, $n_1 \\\\sin \theta_1 = n_2 \\\\sin \theta_2$, is fundamental to understanding how light behaves when passing from one medium to another.

**2. A ray of light is incident on a plane mirror at an angle of $30^circ$. If the reflected ray makes an angle of $60^circ$ with the normal, then the angle of incidence is $ ? $**
**A)**      30
**B)**      45
**C)**      60
**D)**      90

**Answer:** B) From the laws of reflection, we know that the angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection. Since the angle of incidence is given as $30^circ$, the angle of reflection is also $30^circ$. The angle between the reflected ray and the normal is given as $60^circ$. Since the angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection, the angle between the incident ray and the normal is $30^circ$. Therefore, options C and D can be eliminated, as they are greater than $30^circ$. Option A is also incorrect, as it is less than $30^circ$. Therefore, the correct answer is option B.

**3. A point source of light $S$ is placed at a distance of $15 , 	ext{cm}$ from a convex lens of focal length $10 , 	ext{cm}$. The lens forms an image of $S$ on a screen placed $20 , 	ext{cm}$ away from the lens. If the image is real and inverted, then the magnification of the lens is $ ? $**
**A)**      -0.5
**B)**      -1
**C)**      -1.5
**D)**      1

**Answer:** B) From the lens formula, we know that $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u}$. Rearranging the formula to find the magnification, we get $m = \frac{v}{u} = \frac{-v}{u} = \frac{-(-20)}{-15} = -\frac{4}{3}$. Therefore, option B is the correct answer, as it is the only option that equals $-\frac{4}{3}$.

**4. A ray of light is incident on a prism at an angle of $30^circ$. If the angle of deviation is $40^circ$, then the angle of the prism is $ ? $**
**A)**      20
**B)**      30
**C)**      40
**D)**      50

**Answer:** C) From the prism formula, we know that $delta = (i - e) + A$. Rearranging the formula to find the angle of the prism, we get $A = delta - (i - e)$. We know that $delta = 40^circ$ and $i = 30^circ$. The angle of emergence $e$ is equal to the angle of incidence $i$. Therefore, $e = 30^circ$. Substituting these values, we get $A = 40 - (30 - 30) = 40$. Therefore, option C is the correct answer, as it is the only option that equals $40^circ$.

**5. A concave mirror has a focal length of $-15 , 	ext{cm}$. If an object is placed $20 , 	ext{cm}$ away from the mirror, then the image distance is $ ? $**
**A)**      -30
**B)**      -15
**C)**      15
**D)**      30

**Answer:** C) From the mirror formula, we know that $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u}$. Rearranging the formula to find the image distance, we get $\frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{f} - \frac{1}{u}$. We know that $f = -15$ and $u = -20$. Substituting these values, we get $\frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{-15} - \frac{1}{-20} = -\frac{1}{15} + \frac{1}{20} = -\frac{4}{60} + \frac{3}{60} = -\frac{1}{60}$. Therefore, $v = -60$. Therefore, option C is the correct answer, as it is the only option that equals $-60$.

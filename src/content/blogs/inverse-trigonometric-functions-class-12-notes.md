## 🪤 The 5 Mistakes That Cost Marks

Mistake: Using $\sin^{-1}(\frac{1}{2})$ without considering the principal value range, which is $\left[ -\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2} \right]$.
Costs: Full 4 marks
Fix: Always ensure that the inverse trigonometric function's range is considered, e.g., $\sin^{-1}(\frac{1}{2}) = \frac{\pi}{6}$ because $\frac{\pi}{6}$ lies within the principal value range.

Mistake: Forgetting to apply the correct identity for $\tan^{-1}(x)$ when $x < 0$, which is $\tan^{-1}(x) = -\tan^{-1}(|x|)$.
Costs: 2-3 marks depending on the question
Fix: Remember the properties of inverse trigonometric functions, specifically for $\tan^{-1}(x)$ when $x$ is negative, use $-\tan^{-1}(|x|)$ to maintain the correct quadrant.

Mistake: Not simplifying expressions involving inverse trigonometric functions, such as $\cos^{-1}(\cos(\frac{5\pi}{4}))$ without considering the period of $\cos(x)$.
Costs: 3-4 marks
Fix: Simplify using the knowledge that $\cos(x) = \cos(2\pi - x)$ for $x$ in the 3rd and 4th quadrants, thus $\cos^{-1}(\cos(\frac{5\pi}{4})) = \frac{3\pi}{4}$ because $\frac{5\pi}{4}$ is in the 3rd quadrant but its reference angle is in the 1st quadrant where $\cos^{-1}$ is defined.

Mistake: Incorrectly applying the addition formula for $\sin^{-1}$ or $\cos^{-1}$, e.g., $\sin^{-1}(x) + \sin^{-1}(y)$ without using $\cos^{-1}(x)$ and $\cos^{-1}(y)$ relationship.
Costs: Full 4 marks
Fix: Use the formula $\sin^{-1}(x) + \sin^{-1}(y) = \sin^{-1}(x\sqrt{1-y^2} + y\sqrt{1-x^2})$ for $x, y \geq 0$, and apply similar logic for $\cos^{-1}$, ensuring the correct range is considered.

Mistake: Not checking the domain of the function before applying inverse trigonometric identities, e.g., $\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right)$ without confirming $\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$ is within the domain of $\tan^{-1}$.
Costs: 2 marks
Fix: Always verify the input of the inverse trigonometric function is within its defined domain, for $\tan^{-1}$ this means any real number, but for $\sin^{-1}$ and $\cos^{-1}$, the input must be in $[-1,1]$.

Q: If $\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{x+1}{x-1}\right) + \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{x-1}{x+1}\right) = \frac{\pi}{4}$, then $x$ is equal to: 
 Trap in this question: Most students get confused in applying the identity $\tan^{-1}x + \tan^{-1}y = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{x+y}{1-xy}\right)$ and also in handling the equation to get the value of $x$.
 Solution: 
 $\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{x+1}{x-1}\right) + \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{x-1}{x+1}\right) = \frac{\pi}{4}$. Applying the identity, we get 
 $\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\frac{x+1}{x-1} + \frac{x-1}{x+1}}{1 - \frac{x+1}{x-1} \cdot \frac{x-1}{x+1}}\right) = \frac{\pi}{4}$. Simplifying, 
 $\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{(x+1)^2 + (x-1)^2}{(x-1)(x+1) - (x-1)(x+1)}\right) = \frac{\pi}{4}$. This leads to 
 $\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{2x^2 + 2}{-2}\right) = \frac{\pi}{4}$, which further simplifies to 
 $\tan^{-1}(-x^2 - 1) = \frac{\pi}{4}$. Using the fact that $\tan(\frac{\pi}{4}) = 1$, we equate the argument of $\tan^{-1}$ to $1$, resulting in 
 $-x^2 - 1 = 1$. Solving for $x$, $-x^2 = 2$ or $x^2 = -2$. Since $x^2$ cannot be negative, the initial equation has no real solutions for $x$. However, this approach leads to a misunderstanding. The correct path involves recognizing that $\tan^{-1}x + \tan^{-1}\frac{1}{x} = \frac{\pi}{2}$ for $x > 0$, which isn't directly applicable here. Revisiting the equation, 
 $\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{x+1}{x-1}\right) + \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{x-1}{x+1}\right) = \frac{\pi}{4}$ suggests using the tangent addition formula correctly and considering the range of $\tan^{-1}x$. The mistake was in misapplying the formula and not considering the properties of inverse trigonometric functions properly. The question actually simplifies by recognizing the identity $\tan^{-1}x + \tan^{-1}\frac{1}{x} = \frac{\pi}{2}$ does not directly apply but instead realizing the sum leads to an equation where the terms cancel out or simplify under correct manipulation, leading to a specific $x$ that satisfies the given condition, which might involve complex numbers or a special case that fulfills the equation given.
 Answer: The question setup given does not straightforwardly resolve through standard trigonometric identities without considering the specifics of the inverse tangent addition formula and its limitations. The error in the initial analysis was in mishandling the formula and not pursuing the correct algebraic manipulation that directly addresses the given equation's unique aspects.

 Q: The equation $\sin^{-1}x = \tan^{-1}\frac{2x}{1-x^2}$ holds if:
 Trap in this question: Students often get confused about the domains and ranges of inverse trigonometric functions and may incorrectly apply the identities.
 Solution: 
 Given $\sin^{-1}x = \tan^{-1}\frac{2x}{1-x^2}$, recall that $\sin^{-1}x$ implies $-1 \leq x \leq 1$ and $-\frac{\pi}{2} \leq \sin^{-1}x \leq \frac{\pi}{2}$. Also, $\tan^{-1}x$ implies $-\frac{\pi}{2} < \tan^{-1}x < \frac{\pi}{2}$. Recognize the right side can be simplified using the identity $\tan(2\theta) = \frac{2\tan\theta}{1 - \tan^2\theta}$, letting $\tan\theta = x$, thus $\tan(2\theta) = \frac{2x}{1-x^2}$. This connects to the double angle identity for sine, $\sin(2\theta) = \frac{2\tan\theta}{1 + \tan^2\theta}$, but the equation given directly correlates with the tangent double angle formula, suggesting a path through trigonometric identities that relate $\sin^{-1}x$ and $\tan^{-1}\frac{2x}{1-x^2}$. The equation essentially reflects a relationship between the inverse functions that can be explored by considering the ranges and domains of the functions involved and applying trigonometric identities to simplify and understand the conditions under which the equation holds.
 Answer: The given equation relates to specific values of $x$ where both sides of the equation are defined and equal, which typically involves $x$ in the domain of both $\sin^{-1}x$ and $\tan^{-1}\frac{2x}{1-x^2}$, implying $-1 \leq x \leq 1$ and the argument of $\tan^{-1}$ must be defined.

 Q: The principal value of $\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\cos x - \sin x}{\cos x + \sin x}\right)$ is:
 Trap in this question: The trap here is not recognizing the need to simplify the expression inside the inverse tangent using trigonometric identities before applying the inverse tangent function.
 Solution: 
 We start with $\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\cos x - \sin x}{\cos x + \sin x}\right)$. Dividing both numerator and denominator by $\cos x$, we get $\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{1 - \tan x}{1 + \tan x}\right)$. Recall the identity for $\tan(A - B) = \frac{\tan A - \tan B}{1 + \tan A \tan B}$, letting $\tan A = 1$ and $\tan B = \tan x$, the expression inside $\tan^{-1}$ simplifies to $\tan(\frac{\pi}{4} - x)$. Therefore, $\tan^{-1}\left(\tan(\frac{\pi}{4} - x)\right) = \frac{\pi}{4} - x$ for $x$ in the appropriate range. This simplification uses the fact that $\tan^{-1}(\tan x) = x$ for $x \in (-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2})$.
 Answer: $\frac{\pi}{4} - x$

## 🧠 The One Thing Most Students Get Wrong

The key concept that differentiates 85% scorers from 95% scorers in Inverse Trigonometric Functions is the understanding of the range of inverse trigonometric functions and the ability to apply it to solve equations involving composite functions. Most students struggle with the fact that $\sin^{-1}(\sin x) = x$ is not true for all $x$. The principal value of $\sin^{-1}$ is defined as $\left[-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2}\right]$. Therefore, for $x$ outside this range, $\sin^{-1}(\sin x) \neq x$. For instance, if $x = \frac{5\pi}{6}$, then $\sin^{-1}\left(\sin \frac{5\pi}{6}\right) = \frac{\pi}{6}$, not $\frac{5\pi}{6}$. This concept is crucial in solving equations like $\sin^{-1}(\sin x) = \frac{\pi}{3}$, where one must consider the range of $\sin^{-1}$ to find all possible solutions for $x$. The same applies to other inverse trigonometric functions like $\cos^{-1}$ and $\tan^{-1}$, each having its defined range of $[0, \pi]$ and $\left(-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2}\right)$ respectively. Understanding and applying these ranges is critical for accurately solving problems, especially those involving the composition of trigonometric and inverse trigonometric functions.

This restriction is crucial in solving problems. Additionally, the following identities are extremely useful: $\sin^{-1}x + \cos^{-1}x = \frac{\pi}{2}$, $\tan^{-1}x + \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) = \frac{\pi}{2}$ for $x > 0$, and $\tan^{-1}x - \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) = -\frac{\pi}{2}$ for $x < 0$. These identities can be used to simplify complex expressions and solve equations involving inverse trigonometric functions. Moreover, it's important to remember that $\sin^{-1}\left(\sin x\right) = x$ if $x \in \left[ -\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2} \right]$, $\cos^{-1}\left(\cos x\right) = x$ if $x \in \left[ 0, \pi \right]$, and $\tan^{-1}\left(\tan x\right) = x$ if $x \in \left[ -\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2} \right]$. These properties can be used to find the principal values of inverse trigonometric functions and solve equations. By focusing on these key concepts and patterns, students can improve their chances of solving problems related to inverse trigonometric functions correctly.

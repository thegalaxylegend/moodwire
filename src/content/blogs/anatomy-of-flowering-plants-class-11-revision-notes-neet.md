## Core Concepts: Vegetative Anatomy

Vegetative anatomy deals with the structure of roots, stems, and leaves. The root system consists of the primary root, secondary roots, and root hairs. The stem consists of the epicotyl, hypocotyl, and nodes. Leaves are the primary organs of photosynthesis and consist of the lamina, petiole, and leaf base.

The primary tissues in plants include:
- Epidermis: the outermost layer of cells
- Cortex: the layer of cells between the epidermis and the stele
- Stele: the central cylinder of cells that includes the xylem and phloem
- Xylem: the tissue responsible for water and mineral transport
- Phloem: the tissue responsible for food transport

```{.latex}
The equation for photosynthesis is: 6CO_2 + 6H_2O \rightarrow C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2
```




## Core Concepts: Reproductive Anatomy

Reproductive anatomy deals with the structure of flowers, fruits, and seeds. A flower consists of the sepals, petals, stamens, and carpels. The stamens produce pollen, while the carpels produce ovules. Fruits are the mature ovary of a flower, while seeds are the mature ovules.

The process of reproduction in plants involves:
- Pollination: the transfer of pollen from the anther to the stigma
- Fertilization: the fusion of the male and female gametes
- Seed formation: the development of the embryo and the seed coat




## Shortcut Formula/Trick: Identifying Plant Tissues

To identify plant tissues, remember the following formula:
- Epidermis: outermost layer
- Cortex: layer between epidermis and stele
- Stele: central cylinder of cells
- Xylem: tissue with tracheids and vessels
- Phloem: tissue with sieve tubes and companion cells

## Derivation of Plant Body Formulas in Dicots and Monocots

Understanding the formulas of plant body in dicots and monocots is crucial to grasp their anatomy. In dicots, plant body can be represented as: Dicot ≈ Xy - Phe - Phloem

Xy : Xylem

Phe : Phloem

In monocots, plant body can be represented as: Monocot ≈ Xy - Phloem - Vascular Bundle

These formulas help in differentiating between dicot and monocot stem structures and their respective tissues.


| Dicot Body Formula | Monocot Body Formula |
| --- | --- |
| Xy - Phe - Phloem | Xy - Phloem - Vascular Bundle |


## Trap Questions & Exceptions to Watch Out For

Be careful when identifying plant tissues. Remember that parenchyma cells are present in all parts of the plant, sclerenchyma cells provide mechanical support, collenchyma cells are associated with the growth zone, and xylem and phloem are transport tissues. Additionally, do not confuse xylem ray cells with sclereids.




## Derivation of Vascular Bundle in Dicots

In dicots, the vascular bundle is in the form of a ring, but in monocots, it's in the form of a strand. To derive the cross-sectional view of a dicot stem, you need to know the following:


| Component | Arrangement |
| --- | --- |
| Pith | Absent |
| Xylem | Outer ring |
| Cortex | Middle ring |
| Phloem | Inner ring |
| Pericycle | Outer wall of the cambium layer |

## Comparative Study of Plant and Animal Tissues

Plant and animal tissues share many similarities in their structure and function, but there are significant differences between them as well.
The table below provides a comparative study of the main differences between plant and animal tissues:

| Tissue Type | Plant Tissue Characteristics | Animal Tissue Characteristics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Parenchyma | Parenchyma cells are thin-walled with many small vacuoles; storage of food and other substances | Parenchyma cells are thick-walled with few small vacuoles; involved in detoxification and storage of waste products |
| Collenchyma | Collenchyma cells are irregular in shape with abundant cellulose; provide mechanical support | Collenchyma cells are not present in animals |
| Sclerenchyma | Sclerenchyma cells are dead with secondary walls; provide mechanical support | Sclerenchyma cells are not present in animals |
| Xylem | Xylem cells are dead with lignin; involved in water and mineral transport | Xylem cells are not present in animals; blood vessels and nerves provide similar functions |
| Phloem | Phloem cells are alive with sieve tubes and companion cells | Phloem cells are not present in animals |
| Epidermis | Epidermal cells are thin-walled with stomatal apparatus; regulate gas exchange and transpiration | Epidermal cells are thick-walled with tight junctions; regulate gas exchange and secretion of mucus and sweat |

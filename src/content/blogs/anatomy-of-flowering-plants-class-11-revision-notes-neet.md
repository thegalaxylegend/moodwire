## ⚡ Formula Bank
- The anatomy of flowering plants can be understood by studying the structure of roots, stems, and leaves.
- The primary root is the first root to emerge from a seed, and it grows downward into the soil, anchoring the plant and absorbing water and minerals.
- The root system can be categorized into two main types: taproot system and fibrous root system.
- The taproot system consists of a single primary root that grows straight down into the soil, while the fibrous root system consists of many small roots that branch out in all directions.
- The stem is the above-ground part of the plant that supports the leaves and flowers, and it can be categorized into two main types: herbaceous and woody.
- Herbaceous stems are non-woody and flexible, while woody stems are rigid and have a hard outer layer.
- The leaf is a vital organ of the plant that performs photosynthesis, and it consists of three main parts: petiole, lamina, and leaf base.
- The petiole is the stalk that attaches the leaf to the stem, the lamina is the flat part of the leaf that contains the chloroplasts, and the leaf base is the part of the leaf that attaches to the petiole.
- The arrangement of leaves on the stem can be categorized into two main types: alternate and opposite.
- The alternate arrangement refers to the arrangement of leaves in an alternating pattern on the stem, while the opposite arrangement refers to the arrangement of leaves in pairs on the stem.

## ✏️ 3 Solved PYQs
- **Question 1:** Describe the structure of a root.
- The root is a underground part of the plant that anchors the plant and absorbs water and minerals from the soil.
- The root consists of three main parts: root cap, root hair, and root meristem.
- The root cap is a protective layer of cells that covers the root tip, the root hair is a thin hair-like structure that increases the surface area of the root for absorption, and the root meristem is a region of actively dividing cells that produces new root tissue.
- **Question 2:** Explain the difference between a taproot system and a fibrous root system.
- The taproot system consists of a single primary root that grows straight down into the soil, while the fibrous root system consists of many small roots that branch out in all directions.
- The taproot system is typically found in plants that have a deep root system, such as carrots and beets, while the fibrous root system is typically found in plants that have a shallow root system, such as grasses and wheat.
- **Question 3:** Describe the structure of a leaf.
- The leaf is a vital organ of the plant that performs photosynthesis, and it consists of three main parts: petiole, lamina, and leaf base.
- The petiole is the stalk that attaches the leaf to the stem, the lamina is the flat part of the leaf that contains the chloroplasts, and the leaf base is the part of the leaf that attaches to the petiole.
- The leaf also has other structures such as veins, midrib, and margin.

## 🧠 The One Thing Most Students Get Wrong
- The one thing that most students get wrong is the concept of root and shoot apical meristem.
- The root apical meristem is a region of actively dividing cells at the root tip that produces new root tissue, while the shoot apical meristem is a region of actively dividing cells at the shoot tip that produces new shoot tissue.
- Many students confuse the two and think that they are the same thing, but they are actually two separate regions of the plant that have different functions.

## <a id="-3-solved-pyqs"></a>✏️ 3 Solved Yes

- **Q1:** Find the cube root of 17576 by the prime factorization method.
 - **Trap:** Students often make calculation errors during prime factorization or forget to group the factors in triplets, which is crucial for finding the cube root.
 - **Solution:**
 - First, we prime factorize 17576:
 17576 = 2 \times 87888788 = 2 \times 43944394 = 2 \times 2197
 - Now, we need to factorize 2197. A quick check for prime numbers reveals that 2197 is divisible by 13.
 2197 = 13 \times 169169 = 13 \times 13
 - So, the prime factorization of 17576 is:
 17576 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 13 \times 13 \times 13
 - Next, we group the factors in triplets:
 17576 = (2 \times 2 \times 2) \times (13 \times 13 \times 13)17576 = 2^3 \times 13^3
 - To find the cube root, we take one factor from each triplet:
 \sort[3]{17576} = \sort[3]{2^3 \times 13^3}\sort[3]{17576} = 2 \times 13\sort[3]{17576} = 26
 - **Answer:** The cube root of 17576 is 26.

- **Q2:** What is the smallest number by which 392 must be multiplied so that the product is a perfect cube?
 - **Trap:** Students might incorrectly identify the prime factors or fail to see which factors are not part of a triplet, leading to choosing the wrong multiplier.
 - **Solution:**
 - We start by finding the prime factorization of 392:
 392 = 2 \times 196196 = 2 \times 9898 = 2 \times 4949 = 7 \times 7
 - So, the prime factorization of 392 is:
 392 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 7 \times 7
 - Now, we group the factors in triplets:
 392 = (2 \times 2 \times 2) \times (7 \times 7)
 - We can see that the factor 2 forms a triplet ($2^3$). However, the factor 7 only appears twice ($7^2$). To make it a perfect cube, we need one more factor of 7 to complete its triplet.
 - Therefore, to make 392 a perfect cube, we must multiply it by 7.
 - The new number would be:
 392 \times 7 = (2^3 \times 7^2) \times 7 = 2^3 \times 7^3 = (2 \times 7)^3 = 14^3 = 2744
 - And $\sort[3]{2744} = 14$.
 - **Answer:** The smallest number by which 392 must be multiplied is 7.

## <a id="-formula-bank"></a>⚡ Formula Bank

### <a id="-formula-bank"></a>⚡ Formula Bank
#### **Literary Devices Formulas**

- **Metaphor:** A comparison between two unlike things without using like or as — used to create vivid descriptions

- **Simile:** A comparison between two unlike things using like or as — used to create vivid descriptions

- **Personification:** Attributing human qualities to non-human entities — used to create imaginative descriptions

- **Alliteration:** Repetition of initial consonant sounds in words — used to create musical effects

- **Assonance:** Repetition of vowel sounds in words — used to create musical effects
Examiner's Trap: Be careful not to confuse literary devices with each other, as they have distinct uses and effects.

#### **Poetic Devices Formulas**

- **Imagery:** Language that appeals to the senses — used to create vivid descriptions

- **Symbolism:** Using objects or colors to represent abstract ideas — used to create deeper meanings

- **Irony:** A contrast between what is expected and what actually happens — used to create surprise or humor

- **Hyperbole:** An exaggeration used to emphasize a point — used to create strong emotions

- **Onomatopoeia:** Words that imitate the sounds they describe — used to create sensory experiences
Examiner's Trap: Make sure to identify and analyze poetic devices correctly, as they can greatly impact the overall meaning of a poem.

#### **Grammar Formulas**

- **Clause:** A group of words containing a subject and a predicate — used to create sentences

- **Phrase:** A group of words without a subject or predicate — used to create sentence components

- **[Active](/blog/active-and-passive-voice-class-10-notes) Voice:** The subject performs the action — used to create clear and direct sentences

- **[Passive](/blog/active-and-passive-voice-class-10-notes) Voice:** The subject receives the action — used to create emphasis on the action rather than the doer

- **Tense:** A verb form that indicates time — used to create coherence and consistency in writing
Examiner's Trap: Be careful with verb tenses, as inconsistent use can disrupt the flow of a passage.

#### **Figurative Language Formulas**

- **Idiom:** A phrase with a non-literal meaning — used to create colorful and engaging language

- **Colloquialism:** A word or phrase used in informal conversation — used to create a sense of familiarity

- **Jargon:** Specialized language used in a particular field — used to create precision and expertise

- **Euphemism:** A mild or indirect word or phrase used to avoid offense — used to create tact and sensitivity

- **Oxymoron:** A combination of two words that seem to be contradictory — used to create interesting and thought-provoking descriptions
Examiner's Trap: Be aware of the context in which figurative language is used, as it can greatly impact the meaning and tone of a passage.

#### **Rhetorical Devices Formulas**

- **Allusion:** A reference to a person, place, or event outside the text — used to create deeper understanding and connections

- **Amplification:** The extension or expansion of a detail — used to create emphasis and importance

- **Anaphora:** The repetition of a word or phrase at the beginning of successive clauses — used to create rhythm and emphasis

- **Anastrophe:** The reversal of the usual order of words — used to create surprise and interest

- **Aporia:** A expression of doubt or uncertainty — used to create a sense of questioning and inquiry
Examiner's Trap: Be careful not to overuse rhetorical devices, as they can become overwhelming and detract [from](/blog/from-the-diary-of-anne-frank-class-10-notes) the main message.

## <a id="-the-5-mistakes-that-cost-marks"></a>🪤 The 5 Mistakes That Cost Marks

### <a id="the-5-mistakes-that-cost-marks"></a>The 5 Mistakes That Cost Marks

- **Mistake 1 — Inconsistent Verb Tense:**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** "The writer describes the beauty of [India](/blog/nationalism-in-india-class-10-notes) and then went on to explain its cultural diversity."
 

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** Consistent verb tense, e.g.

- "The writer describes the beauty of [India](/blog/nationalism-in-india-class-10-notes) and then explains its cultural diversity."
 

- 💸 **Marks lost:** 1 mark
 

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** Check verb tense consistency by re-reading your work.

- **Mistake 2 — Misinterpretation of Poetic Devices:**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** "The poet uses simile in 'like a tiger's eyes'."
 

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** Accurate identification, e.g.

- "The poet uses a simile in 'like a tiger's eyes' to compare the eyes to a tiger's."
 

- 💸 **Marks lost:** 2 marks
 

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** Recall common poetic devices and their examples.

- **Mistake 3 — Incorrect Use of Articles:**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** "The writer talks about an importance of preserving [India](/blog/nationalism-in-india-class-10-notes)'s natural beauty."
 

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** Correct article usage, e.g.

- "The writer talks about the importance of preserving [India](/blog/nationalism-in-india-class-10-notes)'s natural beauty."
 

- 💸 **Marks lost:** 1 mark
 

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** Remember 'a' for general sounds, 'an' for vowel sounds, and 'the' for specific references.

- **Mistake 4 — Failure to Provide Context:**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** "The writer says that [India](/blog/nationalism-in-india-class-10-notes) is a diverse country."
 

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** Contextualized answer, e.g.

- "According to the passage, the writer highlights [India](/blog/nationalism-in-india-class-10-notes)'s diversity by mentioning its various cultures and traditions."
 

- 💸 **Marks lost:** 2–3 marks
 

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** Read the question carefully and provide relevant context.

- **Mistake 5 — Grammatical Errors:**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** "The writer describe the beauty of [India](/blog/nationalism-in-india-class-10-notes) and its cultural diversity."
 

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** Grammatically correct sentence, e.g.

- "The writer describes the beauty of [India](/blog/nationalism-in-india-class-10-notes) and its cultural diversity."
 

- 💸 **Marks lost:** 1–2 marks
 

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** Proofread your work to catch grammatical errors.

**1. What was the main reason for the British to introduce the English language in India?**
**A)**           To promote Indian culture
**B)**           To establish their rule and control
**C)**           To improve education
**D)**           To increase trade

**Answer:** B) The British introduced the English language in India primarily to establish their rule and control. This helped them to communicate effectively with Indians and to spread Western ideas and values. Option A is incorrect because the British were not interested in promoting Indian culture. Option C is also incorrect because while education was a byproduct, it was not the main reason. Option D is incorrect because trade was not directly related to the introduction of the English language.

**2. Who was the author of the novel 'Indianness'?**
**A)**           Salman Rushdie
**B)**           K. Narayan
**C)**           Jawaharlal Nehru
**D)**           Khushwant Singh

**Answer:** B) R.K. Narayan was a renowned Indian author who wrote several novels and short stories. While he did write about Indian themes, 'Indianness' is not a widely known novel, making it a medium-level question. Option A is incorrect because Salman Rushdie is known for 'Midnight's Children'. Option C is incorrect because Jawaharlal Nehru was a politician and writer, but not known for this novel. Option D is incorrect because Khushwant Singh was a poet and writer, but not associated with this novel.

**A)**           Around 100
**B)**           Around 500
**C)**           Around 1000
**D)**           Over 2000

**Answer:** C) At the time of India's independence, it is estimated that there were around 1000 languages spoken across the country. This reflects the linguistic diversity of India. Option A is incorrect because 100 languages would underestimate the diversity. Option B is incorrect because 500 languages is still a low estimate. Option D is incorrect because while India is diverse, over 2000 languages might be an overestimate.

**5. A book costs ₹250. If it is sold at a 20% profit, what is the selling price?**
**A)**           ₹200
**B)**           ₹250
**C)**           ₹300
**D)**           ₹350

**Answer:** C) To find the selling price with a 20% profit, calculate 20% of ₹250, which is 0.20 × ₹250 = ₹50. Add this to the cost price: ₹250 + ₹50 = ₹300. Option A is incorrect because it is less than the cost price. Option B is incorrect because it does not account for the profit. Option D is incorrect because it overestimates the profit.

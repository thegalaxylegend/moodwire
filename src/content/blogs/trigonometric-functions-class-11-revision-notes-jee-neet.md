## <a id="-formula-bank"></a>⚡ Formula Bank

### <a id="-formula-bank"></a>⚡ Formula Bank
#### **Trigonometric Identities**

- **Pythagorean Identity:** $\sin^2 \theta + \cos^2 \theta = 1$ — relates sine and cosine of an angle

- **Sum and Difference Formulas:** $\sin (a + b) = \sin a \cos b + \cos a \sin b$ — sine of sum of angles

- **Sum and Difference Formulas:** $\sin (a - b) = \sin a \cos b - \cos a \sin b$ — sine of difference of angles

- **Sum and Difference Formulas:** $\cos (a + b) = \cos a \cos b - \sin a \sin b$ — cosine of sum of angles

- **Sum and Difference Formulas:** $\cos (a - b) = \cos a \cos b + \sin a \sin b$ — cosine of difference of angles
Examiner's Trap: Be careful with the signs in sum and difference formulas.

#### **Trigonometric Ratios**

- **Sine:** $\sin \theta = \frac{opposite}{hypotenuse}$ — ratio of opposite side to hypotenuse

- **Cosine:** $\cos \theta = \frac{adjacent}{hypotenuse}$ — ratio of adjacent side to hypotenuse

- **Tangent:** $\tan \theta = \frac{opposite}{adjacent}$ — ratio of opposite side to adjacent side

- **Cotangent:** $\cot \theta = \frac{adjacent}{opposite}$ — ratio of adjacent side to opposite side

- **Secant:** $\sec \theta = \frac{hypotenuse}{adjacent}$ — ratio of hypotenuse to adjacent side

- **Cosecant:** $\csc \theta = \frac{hypotenuse}{opposite}$ — ratio of hypotenuse to opposite side
Examiner's Trap: Make sure to remember the definitions of all six trigonometric ratios.

#### **Inverse Trigonometric Functions**

- **Inverse Sine:** $\sin^{-1} x = \theta$ — angle whose sine is x

- **Inverse Cosine:** $\cos^{-1} x = \theta$ — angle whose cosine is x

- **Inverse Tangent:** $\tan^{-1} x = \theta$ — angle whose tangent is x

- **Inverse Cotangent:** $\cot^{-1} x = \theta$ — angle whose cotangent is x

- **Inverse Secant:** $\sec^{-1} x = \theta$ — angle whose secant is x

- **Inverse Cosecant:** $\csc^{-1} x = \theta$ — angle whose cosecant is x
Examiner's Trap: Be aware of the range of inverse trigonometric functions.

#### **Double Angle and Half Angle Formulas**

- **Double Angle Formula for Sine:** $\sin 2\theta = 2\sin \theta \cos \theta$ — sine of double the angle

- **Double Angle Formula for Cosine:** $\cos 2\theta = \cos^2 \theta - \sin^2 \theta$ — cosine of double the angle

- **Half Angle Formula for Sine:** $\sin \frac{\theta}{2} = \pm \sqrt{\frac{1 - \cos \theta}{2}}$ — sine of half the angle

- **Half Angle Formula for Cosine:** $\cos \frac{\theta}{2} = \pm \sqrt{\frac{1 + \cos \theta}{2}}$ — cosine of half the angle
Examiner's Trap: Double angle and half angle formulas can be tricky to apply.

#### **Product to Sum Formulas**

- **Product to Sum Formula for Sine:** $\sin a \cos b = \frac{1}{2}[\sin (a + b) + \sin (a - b)]$ — product of sine and cosine

- **Product to Sum Formula for Cosine:** $\cos a \cos b = \frac{1}{2}[\cos (a + b) + \cos (a - b)]$ — product of two cosines

- **Product to Sum Formula for Sine:** $\sin a \sin b = \frac{1}{2}[\cos (a - b) - \cos (a + b)]$ — product of two sines
Examiner's Trap: These formulas can help simplify complex trigonometric expressions.

#### **Sum to Product Formulas**

- **Sum to Product Formula for Sine:** $\sin a + \sin b = 2\sin \left(\frac{a + b}{2}\right) \cos \left(\frac{a - b}{2}\right)$ — sum of two sines

- **Sum to Product Formula for Cosine:** $\cos a + \cos b = 2\cos \left(\frac{a + b}{2}\right) \cos \left(\frac{a - b}{2}\right)$ — sum of two cosines

- **Sum to Product Formula for Sine:** $\sin a - \sin b = 2\cos \left(\frac{a + b}{2}\right) \sin \left(\frac{a - b}{2}\right)$ — difference of two sines

- **Sum to Product Formula for Cosine:** $\cos a - \cos b = -2\sin \left(\frac{a + b}{2}\right) \sin \left(\frac{a - b}{2}\right)$ — difference of two cosines
Examiner's Trap: These formulas can help in simplifying sums and differences of trigonometric functions.

## <a id="-the-5-mistakes-that-cost-marks"></a>🪤 The 5 Mistakes That Cost Marks

### <a id="-the-5-mistakes-that-cost-marks"></a>🪤 The 5 Mistakes That Cost Marks

- **Mistake 1 — Sine Rule Slip-Up:**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** $sin(A) = \frac{a}{2R}$ 
 

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** $sin(A) = \frac{a}{2R}$ is incorrect, the correct relation is $\frac{a}{sin(A)} = \frac{b}{sin(B)} = \frac{c}{sin(C)} = 2R$
 

- 💸 **Marks lost:** 2 marks
 

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** Remember the sine rule as $\frac{a}{sin(A)} = 2R$, where $R$ is the circumradius of the triangle

- **Mistake 2 — Cosine Rule Confusion:**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** $cos(A) = \frac{b^2 + c^2 

- a^2}{2bc}$ is the formula for $a^2$, but they forget to square the sides
 

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** $a^2 = b^2 + c^2 

- 2bc \cdot cos(A)$, where $a$, $b$, and $c$ are the sides of the triangle
 

- 💸 **Marks lost:** 1 mark
 

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** Always remember to square the sides when applying the cosine rule, and the correct formula is $a^2 = b^2 + c^2 

- 2bc \cdot cos(A)$

- **Mistake 3 — Tangent Rule Mistake:**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** $tan(A) = \frac{sin(A)}{cos(A)}$ is correct, but they often forget that $tan(A) = \frac{opposite}{adjacent}$
 

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** $tan(A) = \frac{sin(A)}{cos(A)} = \frac{opposite}{adjacent}$, where $A$ is the angle in a right-angled triangle
 

- 💸 **Marks lost:** 1 mark
 

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** Recall that $tan(A)$ can be expressed as both a ratio of trigonometric functions and a ratio of the sides of a right-angled triangle

- **Mistake 4 — Trigonometric Identity Error:**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** $sin^2(A) + cos^2(A) = 1$ is correct, but they often forget the Pythagorean identity for $1 + tan^2(A)$
 

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** $sin^2(A) + cos^2(A) = 1$ and $1 + tan^2(A) = \frac{1}{cos^2(A)} = sec^2(A)$
 

- 💸 **Marks lost:** 2 marks
 

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** Remember the Pythagorean identity $1 + tan^2(A) = sec^2(A)$ to avoid confusion with the fundamental trigonometric identity $sin^2(A) + cos^2(A) = 1$

- **Mistake 5 — Inverse Trigonometric Function Mix-Up:**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** $sin^{-1}(\frac{1}{2}) = \frac{\pi}{4}$, which is incorrect because they confuse $sin^{-1}$ with $cos^{-1}$
 

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** $sin^{-1}(\frac{1}{2}) = \frac{\pi}{6}$ and $cos^{-1}(\frac{1}{2}) = \frac{\pi}{3}$
 

- 💸 **Marks lost:** 3 marks
 

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** Be careful with the inverse trigonometric functions and remember that $sin^{-1}(\frac{1}{2}) = \frac{\pi}{6}$ and $cos^{-1}(\frac{1}{2}) = \frac{\pi}{3}$ to avoid mix-ups

 - 🪤 **Trap:** Most students incorrectly simplify the equation and fail to recognize the need to express $a$ and $b$ in terms of $\alpha$.
 - 🧮 **Solution (Step-by-step):**
 Step 1: Express the given equation using the sum and difference formula for cosine: $a\cos\theta - b\sin\theta = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}(\cos\theta\cos\alpha - \sin\theta\sin\alpha)$
 Step 2: Compare the coefficients of $\cos\theta$ and $\sin\theta$ on both sides: $a = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}\cos\alpha$ and $-b = -\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}\sin\alpha$
 Step 3: Divide the two equations to eliminate the square root: $\frac{a}{-b} = \frac{\cos\alpha}{\sin\alpha}$
 Step 4: Recognize that $\frac{\cos\alpha}{\sin\alpha}$ is equal to $\cot\alpha$, so $\frac{a}{-b} = \cot\alpha$
 Step 5: Recall that $\cot\alpha$ is the reciprocal of $\tan\alpha$, so $\tan\alpha = \frac{-b}{a}$
 **Final Answer:** \tan\alpha = \frac{-b}{a}
 - ⚡ **Speed trick:** Use the sum and difference formula for cosine and compare coefficients to quickly derive the relationship between $a$, $b$, and $\alpha$.

## <a id="-the-one-thing-most-students-get-wrong"></a>🧠 The One Thing Most Students Get Wrong

### <a id="the-one-thing-most-students-get-wrong"></a>The One Thing Most Students Get Wrong
#### Trigonometric Functions Class 11 Revision Notes JEE NEET

- **The misconception (what 85% believe):** Most students believe that the range of $\sin^{-1}x$ is $[-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2}]$ and the range of $\cos^{-1}x$ is $[0, \pi]$, but they often get confused when applying these ranges to solve equations involving inverse trigonometric functions.

- **The reality (what 99% know):** The correct approach is to understand that the range of $\sin^{-1}x$ is indeed $[-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2}]$, but when solving equations like $\sin^{-1}x = \sin^{-1}y$, it implies $x = y$ because $\sin^{-1}$ is a one-to-one function within its defined range. Similarly, for $\cos^{-1}x$, the range is $[0, \pi]$, and $\cos^{-1}x = \cos^{-1}y$ implies $x = y$ due to the one-to-one nature of $\cos^{-1}$ within its range. The key insight is recognizing that these inverse functions are restricted to specific intervals to ensure they are one-to-one, which is crucial for solving equations and inequalities involving them.

- **The diagnostic question:** What is the solution to the equation $\sin^{-1}(\frac{1}{2}) + \cos^{-1}(\frac{1}{2})$?

- If you answered $\frac{\pi}{2}$: you have the misconception → fix: Recall that $\sin^{-1}(\frac{1}{2}) = \frac{\pi}{6}$ and $\cos^{-1}(\frac{1}{2}) = \frac{\pi}{3}$, so the sum is $\frac{\pi}{6} + \frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{\pi}{2}$, which actually confirms the calculation is correct but understanding the one-to-one nature of these functions within their defined ranges is key.

- If you answered $\frac{\pi}{2}$ but understood the reasoning: you are in the top 5% → now extend this: Recognize that for any $x$ where $-1 \leq x \leq 1$, $\sin^{-1}x + \cos^{-1}x = \frac{\pi}{2}$ because of the complementary nature of sine and cosine functions within the specified ranges of their inverse functions.

- **How to never forget this:** To remember that $\sin^{-1}x + \cos^{-1}x = \frac{\pi}{2}$ for $-1 \leq x \leq 1$, use the mnemonic "Sine and Cosine Inverses Complement to $\frac{\pi}{2}$" and visualize the unit circle where the angles of sine and cosine functions are complementary, meaning they add up to $\frac{\pi}{2}$ (or 90 degrees), reinforcing the concept that the sum of an angle and its complement is a right angle. Additionally, remembering that the range of $\sin^{-1}$ is $[-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2}]$ and the range of $\cos^{-1}$ is $[0, \pi]$ helps in quickly identifying the correct approach to solving problems involving these inverse trigonometric functions. 

### <a id="key-formulas-and-identities"></a>Key Formulas and Identities

- The Pythagorean identity: $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$

- The definition of $\sin^{-1}x$: $\sin^{-1}x = \theta$ if and only if $\sin\theta = x$ and $-\frac{\pi}{2} \leq \theta \leq \frac{\pi}{2}$

- The definition of $\cos^{-1}x$: $\cos^{-1}x = \theta$ if and only if $\cos\theta = x$ and $0 \leq \theta \leq \pi$

- The complementary identity: $\sin^{-1}x + \cos^{-1}x = \frac{\pi}{2}$ for $-1 \leq x \leq 1$

- The derivatives of $\sin^{-1}x$ and $\cos^{-1}x$ are $\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}$ and $-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}$, respectively, which can be used to find the derivatives of more complex functions involving inverse trigonometric functions.

## <a id="-ayushs-note"></a>👁️ Ayush's Note

### <a id="ayushs-note"></a>Ayush's Note

- **🔮 The Hidden Pattern:** Trigonometric functions are often linked with coordinate geometry, as seen in 30%+ of papers, where students are required to find the equation of a line or circle using $sin(\theta)$ and $cos(\theta)$, and then apply it to solve problems involving $tan(\theta) = \frac{y}{x}$, where $\theta$ is the angle between the line and the x-axis, and $x$ and $y$ are the coordinates of a point on the line.

- **🎯 The "Always Check" Rule:** Always check for the boundary condition where $cos(\theta) = 0$, because this often leads to $tan(\theta)$ being undefined, and examiners love to test students on their understanding of this edge case, especially when solving equations involving $\frac{sin(\theta)}{cos(\theta)}$.

- **📊 PYQ Frequency Intel:** In 2019, questions were asked on the general solution of $sin(\theta) = 0$ [1], in 2021, on the relationship between $cos(\theta)$ and $sin(\theta)$ using the identity $cos^2(\theta) + sin^2(\theta) = 1$ [2], and in 2023, on the use of $tan(\theta)$ to solve a right-angled triangle problem involving $\frac{a}{b}$, where $a$ and $b$ are the lengths of the sides of the triangle [3].

- **⚡ The 30-Second Shortcut:** To solve problems involving $sin(A+B)$, use the formula $sin(A+B) = sin(A)cos(B) + cos(A)sin(B)$, and if $A = B$, it simplifies to $sin(2A) = 2sin(A)cos(A)$, allowing you to quickly find the value of $sin(2A)$ given the values of $sin(A)$ and $cos(A)$, and this can be done in under 30 seconds by simply plugging in the given values into the formula.

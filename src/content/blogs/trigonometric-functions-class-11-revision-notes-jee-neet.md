## <a id="-formula-bank"></a>⚡ Formula Bank

### <a id="-formula-bank"></a>⚡ Formula Bank
#### **Trigonometric Identities**

- **Pythagorean Identity:** \sin^2{\theta} + \cos^2{\theta} = 1 — relates sine and cosine of an angle

- **Sum and Difference Formula for Sine:** \sin{(a \pm b)} = \sin{a}\cos{b} \pm \cos{a}\sin{b} — angle addition and subtraction for sine

- **Sum and Difference Formula for Cosine:** \cos{(a \pm b)} = \cos{a}\cos{b} \mp \sin{a}\sin{b} — angle addition and subtraction for cosine

- **Double Angle Formula for Sine:** \sin{2a} = 2\sin{a}\cos{a} — relates sine of double angle to sine and cosine of single angle

- **Double Angle Formula for Cosine:** \cos{2a} = \cos^2{a} - \sin^2{a} — relates cosine of double angle to sine and cosine of single angle
Examiner's Trap: Be careful with the signs in the \sum and difference formulas.

#### **Trigonometric Ratios**

- **Sine Ratio:** \sin{\theta} = $\frac{\text{opposite side}$}{$\text{hypotenuse}$} — defines sine in a right-angled triangle

- **Cosine Ratio:** \cos{\theta} = $\frac{\text{adjacent side}$}{$\text{hypotenuse}$} — defines cosine in a right-angled triangle

- **Tangent Ratio:** \tan{\theta} = $\frac{\text{opposite side}$}{$\text{adjacent side}$} — defines tangent in a right-angled triangle

- **Cotangent Ratio:** \cot{\theta} = $\frac{\text{adjacent side}$}{$\text{opposite side}$} — defines cotangent in a right-angled triangle

- **Secant Ratio:** \sec{\theta} = $\frac{\text{hypotenuse}$}{$\text{adjacent side}$} — defines secant in a right-angled triangle

- **Cosecant Ratio:** \csc{\theta} = $\frac{\text{hypotenuse}$}{$\text{opposite side}$} — defines cosecant in a right-angled triangle
Examiner's Trap: Remember that the reciprocal identities are crucial for simplifying expressions.

#### **Inverse Trigonometric Functions**

- **Inverse Sine Function:** \sin^{-1}{x} = \theta \iff \sin{\theta} = x — defines the inverse sine function

- **Inverse Cosine Function:** \cos^{-1}{x} = \theta \iff \cos{\theta} = x — defines the inverse cosine function

- **Inverse Tangent Function:** \tan^{-1}{x} = \theta \iff \tan{\theta} = x — defines the inverse tangent function
Examiner's Trap: Be aware of the range of inverse trigonometric functions.

#### **Product to Sum Formulas**

- **Product to Sum Formula for Sine:** \sin{a}\sin{b} = $\frac{1}{2}$[\cos{(a - b)} - \cos{(a + b)}] — converts product of sines to \sum

- **Product to Sum Formula for Cosine:** \cos{a}\cos{b} = $\frac{1}{2}$[\cos{(a + b)} + \cos{(a - b)}] — converts product of cosines to \sum

- **Product to Sum Formula for Sine and Cosine:** \sin{a}\cos{b} = $\frac{1}{2}$[\sin{(a + b)} + \sin{(a - b)}] — converts product of sine and cosine to \sum
Examiner's Trap: These formulas can simplify complex expressions significantly.

#### **Sum to Product Formulas**

- **Sum to Product Formula for Sine:** \sin{a} + \sin{b} = 2\sin{\left($\frac{a + b}{2}$\right)}\cos{\left($\frac{a - b}{2}$\right)} — converts \sum of sines to product

- **Sum to Product Formula for Cosine:** \cos{a} + \cos{b} = 2\cos{\left($\frac{a + b}{2}$\right)}\cos{\left($\frac{a - b}{2}$\right)} — converts \sum of cosines to product
Examiner's Trap: These formulas are useful for expressing sums as products.

### <a id="-ayushs-note"></a>👁️ Ayush's Note

- **🔮 The Hidden Pattern:** Trigonometric expressions frequently appear as quadratic forms, often disguised, requiring you to find their range or conditions for real solutions. This is a recurring pattern in 30%+ of advanced papers (JEE Adv/NEET AIIMS level). For instance, problems like finding the minimum value of $f(x) = A \sin^2 x + B \cos x + C$ for $x \in \mathbb{R}$ are solved by substituting $\cos x = t$ where $t \in [-1, 1]$, transforming the problem into finding the range of a quadratic $At^2 

- Bt + C'$ over a restricted domain. Similarly, equations like $a \cos x + b \sin x = c$ can be solved by converting to $R \cos(x-\alpha) = c$, or by using the $\tan(x/2)$ substitution, which leads to a quadratic equation in $\tan(x/2)$. The crucial step is always to verify the validity of the roots within the domain of the trigonometric function. For example, if $t = \sin x$, any derived $t$ value outside $[-1, 1]$ is extraneous.

- **🎯 The "Always Check" Rule:** When solving trigonometric equations, particularly those involving squares or products, *always* verify the domain and range constraints of the original functions for your solutions. Examiners love to introduce solutions that are algebraically correct for the transformed equation but violate the fundamental range of $\sin x$ or $\cos x$, which is $[-1, 1]$, or the domain restrictions of $\tan x$, $\cot x$, $\sec x$, $\csc x$. For instance, if you solve for $\sin x$ and get $\sin x = 2$, this solution must be rejected. Similarly, if you divide by a trigonometric expression, say $\cos x$, ensure that $\cos x 
eq $0 for the valid solutions; if $\cos x = 0$ is a possible solution, it must be handled separately.

- **This extends to general solutions:** ensure that the principal values used for inverse functions align with their defined ranges.

- **A common pitfall:** $\sqrt{\sin^2 x} = |\sin x|$, not just $\sin x$. Ignoring this sign can lead to incorrect solution sets.

- Sum of sines:
  \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \sin(\alpha + k\beta) = \frac{\sin(n\beta/2)}{\sin(\beta/2)} \sin\left(\alpha + \frac{(n-1)\beta}{2}\right) 
 

- Sum of cosines:
  \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \cos(\alpha + k\beta) = \frac{\sin(n\beta/2)}{\sin(\beta/2)} \cos\left(\alpha + \frac{(n-1)\beta}{2}\right) 
 This instantly simplifies expressions that would otherwise involve tedious \sum-to-product conversions. For instance, if you need $\sin \theta + \sin(\theta + 2\pi/n) + \dots + \sin(\theta + 2(n-1)\pi/n)$, applying the formula with $\beta = 2\pi/n$ yields $\sin(n \cdot \pi/n) = \sin(\pi) = 0$ in the numerator, directly giving $0$ (provided $\sin(\beta/2) 
eq $0). This is a critical time-saver for series-based questions.

## <a id="-last-5-minutes-box"></a>🔁 Last 5 Minutes Box

### <a id="-core-formulas"></a>⚡ Core Formulas

- $sin(\theta) = \frac{opposite}{hypotenuse}$ — gives you the sign of an angle in a right-angled triangle

- $cos(\theta) = \frac{adjacent}{hypotenuse}$ — gives you the cosine of an angle in a right-angled triangle

- $tan(\theta) = \frac{opposite}{adjacent}$ — gives you the tangent of an angle in a right-angled triangle

- $sin^2(\theta) + cos^2(\theta) = 1$ — gives you the Pythagorean identity for sine and cosine

- $tan(\theta) = \frac{sin(\theta)}{cos(\theta)}$ — gives you the relationship between tangent, sine, and cosine

### <a id="-mustknow-facts"></a>🧠 Must-Know Facts

- The range of $sin(\theta)$ and $cos(\theta)$ is $[-1, 1]$

- The range of $tan(\theta)$ is all real numbers

- The period of $sin(\theta)$, $cos(\theta)$, and $tan(\theta)$ is $2\pi$, $\frac{\pi}{2}$ for $tan(\theta)$, but $sin(\theta)$ and $cos(\theta)$ have a period of $2\pi$

### <a id="-never-forget"></a>🚫 Never Forget

- ❌ Assuming $tan(\theta) = \frac{1}{tan(\theta)}$ → ✅ $tan(\theta) = \frac{1}{cot(\theta)}$ and $cot(\theta) = \frac{1}{tan(\theta)}$

- ❌ Forgetting that $sec(\theta) = \frac{1}{cos(\theta)}$ and $csc(\theta) = \frac{1}{sin(\theta)}$ → ✅ using these identities to simplify trigonometric expressions

**1. The value of $\sin 75^\circ$ is most nearly equal to which of the following?**
**A)**   $\sqrt{3}/2$
**B)**   \frac{1}{2}
**C)**   \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
**D)**   \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}

**Answer:** A) Option B and C can be eliminated because $\sin 75^\circ$ is greater than $\frac{1}{2}$ and option D is less than $\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$. We can simplify $\sin 75^\circ$ using the angle addition formula: $\sin 75^\circ = \sin (45^\circ + 30^\circ) = \sin 45^\circ \cos 30^\circ + \cos 45^\circ \sin 30^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{2} = \frac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{4}$. This is close to $\sqrt{3}/2$.

**2. In triangle ABC, $\angle C = 90^\circ$, $\tan A = 3$, and $\cos B = 1/\sqrt{10}$. What is the value of $\sin B$?**
**A)**   \frac{3}{\sqrt{10}}
**B)**   \frac{1}{\sqrt{10}}
**C)**   \frac{3}{\sqrt{13}}
**D)**   \frac{1}{\sqrt{13}}

**Answer:** B) We can use the trigonometric identity $\tan A = \frac{\sin A}{\cos A}$ to find $\sin A$. Then, we can use the identity $\sin B = \cos (90^\circ - B)$ to find $\sin B$. Since $\tan A = 3$, we have $\sin A = 3 \cos A$. Since $\cos B = 1/\sqrt{10}$, we have $\sin B = \cos (90^\circ - B) = \sin B = \frac{\sqrt{10}}{\sqrt{10}} = 1$. Therefore, $\sin B = \boxed{\frac{1}{\sqrt{10}}}$.

**3. A ray of light is incident on a glass slab at an angle of incidence $\theta = 30^\circ$. If the angle of refraction is $\phi = 20^\circ$, what is the value of the refractive index $\mu$?**
**A)**   \frac{4}{5}
**B)**   \frac{5}{4}
**C)**   \frac{3}{4}
**D)**   \frac{4}{3}

**Answer:** A) We can use Snell's law to find the refractive index $\mu$. Snell's law states that $\mu = \frac{\sin \theta}{\sin \phi}$, so we have $\mu = \frac{\sin 30^\circ}{\sin 20^\circ}$.

**4. What is the value of $\sin (\arcsin x + \arccos x)$?**
**A)**   $x$
**B)**   $x^2$
**C)**   $x - x^3$
**D)**   \frac{x^2}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}}

**Answer:** D) We can use the \sum and difference formulas for sine to expand $\sin (\arcsin x + \arccos x)$. This yields $\sin (\arcsin x + \arccos x) = \sin (\arcsin x) \cos (\arccos x) + \cos (\arcsin x) \sin (\arccos x) = x \cdot \sqrt{1 - x^2} + \sqrt{1 - x^2} \cdot x$. We can simplify further to obtain $\sin (\arcsin x + \arccos x) = \frac{x^2}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}}$.

**5. A water tank is in the shape of a cone with base radius $r = 2$ m and height $h = 3$ m. Water is poured into the tank until the depth of water is $d = 1$ m. What is the value of the angle of elevation $\theta$?**
**A)**   \tan^{-1} (\frac{1}{2})
**B)**   \tan^{-1} (\frac{1}{3})
**C)**   \tan^{-1} (\frac{2}{1})
**D)**   \tan^{-1} (\frac{3}{2})

**Answer:** C) We can use the properties of similar triangles to find the angle of elevation $\theta$. Let $\theta$ be the angle of elevation from the vertex of the cone to the point where the water surface intersects the side of the cone. We can draw the diagram and use the properties of similar triangles to obtain $\tan \theta = \frac{h - d}{r} = \frac{3 - 1}{2} = \frac{1}{2}$. Therefore, $\theta = \boxed{\tan^{-1} (\frac{1}{2})}$.

## <a id="-formula-bank"></a>⚡ Formula Bank

### <a id="chemical-reaction-formulas"></a>Chemical Reaction Formulas

- **Law of Conservation of Mass:** $\text{Mass of reactants}$ = $\text{Mass of products}$ — states that matter cannot be created or destroyed in a chemical reaction

- **Law of Definite Proportions:** $\franc{\text{Mass of element in compound}$}{$\text{Mass of compound}$} = $\text{Constant}$ — describes the fixed ratio of elements in a compound

- **Chemical Equation:** $\text{Reactants}$ \right arrow $\text{Products}$ — represents a chemical reaction

- **Balancing Chemical Equations:** NCAA + BB \right arrow cc + DDR — coefficients a, b, c, and d are used to balance the equation
Examiner's Trap: Make sure to balance the chemical equation by using the smallest whole-number coefficients.

### <a id="stoichiometry-formulas"></a>Stoichiometry Formulas

- **Mole Ratio:** $\franc{\text{Number of moles of reactant}$}{$\text{Number of moles of product}$} — used to determine the limiting reactant

- **Molar Mass:** $\text{Molar mass}$ = $\franc{\text{Mass of substance}$}{$\text{Number of moles}$} — relates the mass of a substance to its number of moles

- **Number of Moles:** in = $\franc{m}{M}$ — calculates the number of moles of a substance

- **Empirical Formula:** $\text{Empirical formula}$ = $\franc{\text{Number of moles of each element}$}{$\text{Smallest number of moles}$} — gives the simplest whole-number ratio of atoms in a compound
Examiner's Trap: Be careful when calculating the number of moles, as it is a common source of error.

### <a id="types-of-chemical-reactions-formulas"></a>Types of Chemical Reactions Formulas

- **Combination Reaction:** A + B \right arrow ABLE — two or more substances combine to form a new substance

- **Decomposition Reaction:** ARAB \right arrow A + BY — a single substance breaks down into two or more simpler substances

- **Displacement Reaction:** A + BC \right arrow AC + BY — one element displaces another element from a compound

- **Double Displacement Reaction:** ARAB + CD \right arrow AD + CBC — two [compounds](/blog/carbon-and-its-compounds-class-10-notes) exchange partners
Examiner's Trap: Identify the type of chemical reaction to determine the correct equation.

### <a id="oxidation-and-reduction-formulas"></a>Oxidation and Reduction Formulas

- **Oxidation:** A \right arrow A^{+} + e^{-} — loss of electrons

- **Reduction:** JOB^{+} + e^{-} \right arrow BY — gain of electrons

- **Redox Reaction:** A + B^{+} \right arrow A^{+} + BY — combination of oxidation and reduction
Examiner's Trap: Recognize that oxidation and reduction occur simultaneously in a redox reaction.

### <a id="catalysts-and-reactants-formulas"></a>Catalysts and Reactants Formulas

- **Catalyst:** $\text{Catalyst}$ = $\text{Substance that speeds up the reaction without being consumed}$ — does not affect the equilibrium constant

- **Reactant:** $\text{Reactant}$ = $\text{Substance that is consumed during the reaction}$ — affects the equilibrium constant
Examiner's Trap: Distinguish between catalysts and reactants, as they have different roles in the reaction.

### <a id="equilibrium-constant-formulas"></a>Equilibrium Constant Formulas

- **Equilibrium Constant (K):** OK = $\franc{[\text{Products}$]}{[$\text{Reactants}$]} — measures the ratio of product concentrations to reactant concentrations at equilibrium

- **Equilibrium Constant Expression:** OK = $\franc{[\text{C}$]^c [$\text{D}$]^d}{[$\text{A}$]^a [$\text{B}$]^b} — relates the equilibrium constant to the concentrations of reactants and products
Examiner's Trap: Use the correct equilibrium constant expression for the given reaction.

### <a id="which-formula-when"></a>Which Formula When?
| Formula | Description | When to Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \text{Mass of reactants} = \text{Mass of products} | Law of Conservation of Mass | Always true for a closed system |
| \franc{\text{Number of moles of reactant}}{\text{Number of moles of product}} | Mole Ratio | To determine the limiting reactant |
| in = \franc{m}{M} | Number of Moles | To calculate the number of moles of a substance |
| OK = \franc{[\text{Products}]}{[\text{Reactants}]} | Equilibrium Constant | To measure the ratio of product concentrations to reactant concentrations at equilibrium |

## <a id="-the-5-mistakes-that-cost-marks"></a>🪤 The 5 Mistakes That Cost Marks

### <a id="-the-5-mistakes-that-cost-marks"></a>🪤 The 5 Mistakes That Cost Marks

- **Mistake 1 — Balancing Act:**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** $2Na + Cl_2 \right arrow 2NaCl_2$
 

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** $2Na + Cl_2 \right arrow 2NaCl$
 

- 💸 **Marks lost:** 1 mark
 

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** Use the law of conservation of mass to balance equations, ensuring the number of atoms for each element is the same on both the reactant and product sides.

- **Mistake 2 — Formula Frenzy:**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** $\franc{m}{M} = \franc{g}{cm^3}$
 

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** $\franc{m}{M} = \franc{g}{\text{mol}}$ and density CD = \franc{m}{V} = \franc{g}{cm^3}$
 

- 💸 **Marks lost:** 2 marks
 

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** Distinguish between mass (me), molar mass (ME), and density (do), using the correct units: me in grams (g), ME in g/MOL, and do in g/cm^$3.

- **Mistake 3 — Reaction Types:**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** All chemical reactions are reversible.

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** Identify the types of reactions (combination, decomposition, displacement, double displacement) and understand that not all reactions are reversible.

- 💸 **Marks lost:** 2 marks
 

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** Classify reactions into types and recall that reversible reactions have a $\rightleftharpoons$ symbol, indicating equilibrium.

- **Mistake 4 — Equation Errors:**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** Zn + CUSO_4 \right arrow ENSO_4 + Cut
 

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** Zn + CUSO_4 \right arrow ENSO_4 + Cut
 

- 💸 **Marks lost:** 1 mark
 

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** Verify the correctness of chemical equations by ensuring the reactants yield the correct products and that the equation is balanced.

- **Mistake 5 — Stoichiometry Slip-Ups:**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** in = \franc{m}{$\text{volume}$}$
 

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** in = \franc{m}{M}$, where and is the number of moles, me is the mass in grams, and ME is the molar mass.

- 💸 **Marks lost:** 3 marks
 

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** Recall the formula in = \franc{m}{M}$ and ensure to use the correct units for calculating the number of moles of a substance.

 **Final Answer:** Monaco_3 + 2HCl \right arrow Call_2 + H_2O + CO_2
 - ⚡ **Speed trick:** Use the "hit and trial" method to balance the equation by trial and error.

## <a id="-the-one-thing-most-students-get-wrong"></a>🧠 The One Thing Most Students Get Wrong

### <a id="the-one-thing-most-students-get-wrong"></a>The One Thing Most Students Get Wrong
The majority of students, approximately 85%, believe that a chemical equation is balanced when the number of atoms of each element is equal on both the reactant and product sides.

- This misconception arises from a lack of understanding of the concept of chemical reactions and equations.

- Students often think that balancing an equation is just about making the number of atoms of each element equal, without considering the actual chemical reaction.

### <a id="the-reality"></a>The Reality is that a chemical equation is balanced when the number of atoms of each element is equal on both the reactant and product sides, and the equation follows the law of conservation of mass.

- This means that the total mass of the reactants must be equal to the total mass of the products.

- The law of conservation of mass can be expressed using the formula: km_{$\text{reactants}$} = m_{$\text{products}$}$

### <a id="the-diagnostic-question"></a>The Diagnostic Question
What is the correct way to balance a chemical equation?

- A) By making the number of atoms of each element equal on both sides

- B) By making the number of atoms of each element equal on both sides and following the law of conservation of mass

- C) By changing the formula of the reactants or products

- D) By ignoring the law of conservation of mass

If you answered A: you have the misconception → fix: remember that balancing an equation involves not just making the number of atoms equal, but also following the law of conservation of mass, which can be expressed as $\franc{m_{\text{reactants}}}{m_{\text{products}}} = 1$.

- **If you answered B: you are in the top 5% → now extend this:** consider the equation $2Na + Cl_{2} \right arrow 2NaCl$, where the number of atoms of each element is equal on both sides, and the law of conservation of mass is followed, as the total mass of the reactants ($2 \times 23 + 2 \times 35.5$) is equal to the total mass of the products ($2 \times 58.5$).

### <a id="how-to-never-forget-this"></a>How to Never Forget This
To never forget this, use the mnemonic "CARE": 

- C: Conservation of mass

- A: Atoms of each element must be equal

- R: Reactants and products must be balanced

- E: Equation must follow the law of conservation of mass
Alternatively, visualize a balance scale with reactants on one side and products on the other, where the total mass of the reactants is equal to the total mass of the products, and the number of atoms of each element is equal on both sides, as expressed by the equation: $\franc{\text{mass of reactants}$}{$\text{mass of products}$} = $1

**1. The balanced chemical equation for the combustion of methane is $\text{CH}_4 + 2\text{O}_2 \right arrow \text{CO}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$. If 1 mole of methane is burned, how many moles of water are produced?**
**A)**     1 mole
**B)**     2 moles
**C)**     4 moles
**D)**     6 moles

**Answer:** B) Options A and D are incorrect because the balanced equation shows that 1 mole of methane produces 2 moles of water (not 1 or 6 moles). Option C is incorrect because the balanced equation shows that 1 mole of methane produces 2 moles of water (not 4 moles).

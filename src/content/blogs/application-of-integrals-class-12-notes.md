## 🎯 What WILL Come in Your Exam

* **Area between curves**: 1-2 numericals on finding the area between $y = f(x)$ and $y = g(x)$, always using the formula $A = \int_{a}^{b} |f(x) - g(x)| \, dx$.
* **Area under curves**: 1 numerical on finding the area under a curve $y = f(x)$ between the limits $a$ and $b$, using the formula $A = \int_{a}^{b} f(x) \, dx$.
* **Volume of solids**: 1 numerical on finding the volume of a solid formed by revolving a region about the x-axis, using the formula $V = \pi \int_{a}^{b} [f(x)]^2 \, dx$.
* **Surface area of solids**: 1 numerical on finding the surface area of a solid formed by revolving a region about the x-axis, using the formula $S = 2\pi \int_{a}^{b} f(x) \sqrt{1 + [f'(x)]^2} \, dx$.
* **Centre of mass**: 1 numerical on finding the centre of mass of a lamina, using the formulas $\bar{x} = \frac{\int_{a}^{b} x \cdot f(x) \, dx}{\int_{a}^{b} f(x) \, dx}$ and $\bar{y} = \frac{\int_{a}^{b} \frac{1}{2} [f(x)]^2 \, dx}{\int_{a}^{b} f(x) \, dx}$.
* **Moment of inertia**: 1 numerical on finding the moment of inertia of a lamina about the x-axis, using the formula $I_x = \int_{a}^{b} \frac{1}{3} [f(x)]^3 \, dx$.
* **Graphical questions**: 1-2 questions on graphing $y = \int_{a}^{x} f(t) \, dt$, including finding the area under the curve and the volume of the solid formed by revolving the region about the x-axis.
* **Definite integral properties**: 1-2 numericals on using the properties of definite integrals, such as $\int_{a}^{b} f(x) \, dx = \int_{a}^{c} f(x) \, dx + \int_{c}^{b} f(x) \, dx$ and $\int_{a}^{b} f(x) \, dx = -\int_{b}^{a} f(x) \, dx$.
* **Improper integrals**: 1 numerical on evaluating an improper integral, such as $\int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{x^2 + 1} \, dx$ or $\int_{-\infty}^{0} \frac{1}{x^2} \, dx$.
* **Applications of integrals in physics**: 1-2 numericals on using integrals to solve physics problems, such as finding the work done by a variable force or the centre of mass of a system of particles.


## ⚡ Formula Bank

- **Area Between Two Curves:** $$\int_{a}^{b} [f(x) - g(x)] \,dx$$ — $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ are the two curves, $a$ and $b$ are the limits of integration.

 - **Area Under a Curve:** $$\int_{a}^{b} f(x) \,dx$$ — $f(x)$ is the function, $a$ and $b$ are the limits of integration.

 - **Volume of Solid by Disk Method:** $$V = \pi \int_{a}^{b} [f(x)]^2 \,dx$$ — $f(x)$ is the radius of the disk, $a$ and $b$ are the limits of integration.

 - **Volume of Solid by Washer Method:** $$V = \pi \int_{a}^{b} [f(x)]^2 - [g(x)]^2 \,dx$$ — $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ are the outer and inner radii, $a$ and $b$ are the limits of integration.

 - **Volume of Solid by Shell Method:** $$V = 2\pi \int_{a}^{b} x[f(x) - g(x)] \,dx$$ — $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ are the heights of the shell, $a$ and $b$ are the limits of integration.

 - **Surface Area of Solid by Disk Method:** $$S = 2\pi \int_{a}^{b} f(x)\sqrt{1 + [f'(x)]^2} \,dx$$ — $f(x)$ is the radius of the disk, $f'(x)$ is the derivative, $a$ and $b$ are the limits of integration.

 - **Surface Area of Solid by Washer Method:** $$S = 2\pi \int_{a}^{b} [f(x) + g(x)]\sqrt{1 + [f'(x)]^2} \,dx$$ — $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ are the outer and inner radii, $f'(x)$ is the derivative, $a$ and $b$ are the limits of integration.

 - **Centroid of Region:** $$\bar{x} = \frac{1}{A} \int_{a}^{b} x[f(x) - g(x)] \,dx$$ — $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ are the boundaries, $A$ is the area, $a$ and $b$ are the limits of integration.

 - **Moments of Inertia:** $$I_x = \int_{a}^{b} y^2 \,dm$$, $$I_y = \int_{a}^{b} x^2 \,dm$$ — $x$ and $y$ are the distances from the axis, $dm$ is the mass element.

 - **Work Done by a Variable Force:** $$W = \int_{a}^{b} F(x) \,dx$$ — $F(x)$ is the force, $a$ and $b$ are the limits of integration.

 - **Hydrostatic Force:** $$F = \int_{a}^{b} p(x) \,dx$$ — $p(x)$ is the pressure, $a$ and $b$ are the limits of integration.

 - **Center of Mass:** $$\bar{x} = \frac{1}{M} \int_{a}^{b} x \rho(x) \,dx$$ — $\rho(x)$ is the density, $M$ is the total mass, $a$ and $b$ are the limits of integration.



## 🪤 The 5 Mistakes That Cost Marks

- **Mistake 1:** Incorrectly applying the formula for the area between two curves, $A = \int_{a}^{b} (f(x) - g(x)) \, dx$, where $f(x) \geq g(x)$ in $[a, b]$.

 - *Costs:* 4-6 marks per question

 - *Fix:* Ensure $f(x) \geq g(x)$ and the correct limits of integration are used. Double-check the functions and the interval.

 - **Mistake 2:** Forgetting to consider the orientation of the curve when calculating the area using $A = \int_{a}^{b} y \, dx$ or $A = \int_{c}^{d} x \, dy$ for regions bounded by functions of $x$ or $y$.

 - *Costs:* 3-5 marks per question

 - *Fix:* Always sketch the region to understand the orientation and choose the appropriate formula.

 - **Mistake 3:** Not accounting for the absolute value when finding the area between curves that intersect, leading to incorrect application of $A = \int_{a}^{b} |f(x) - g(x)| \, dx$.

 - *Costs:* 5-7 marks per question

 - *Fix:* Identify intersection points and split the integral into parts where one function is above the other, or use the absolute value to ensure positive area.

 - **Mistake 4:** Incorrect application of the disk/washer method for volumes of solids, specifically misusing the formula $V = \pi \int_{a}^{b} (f(x))^2 - (g(x))^2 \, dx$ for washers.

 - *Costs:* 6-8 marks per question

 - *Fix:* Verify that the outer radius is $f(x)$ and the inner radius is $g(x)$, and ensure the limits of integration are correct.

 - **Mistake 5:** Failing to evaluate the integral correctly when finding volumes using the shell method, $V = 2\pi \int_{a}^{b} x(f(x) - g(x)) \, dx$, particularly with respect to the height of the shell.

 - *Costs:* 5-7 marks per question

 - *Fix:* Ensure the height of the shell, $f(x) - g(x)$, is correctly calculated and that the integral is evaluated with respect to $x$.



## ✏️ 3 Solved PYQs

- **Q1:** Find the area of the region bounded by the curve $y = \frac{1}{x}$, the x-axis, and the lines $x = 1$ and $x = 4$.

 - **Trap:** Forgetting to consider the limits of integration and the nature of the function.

 - **Solution:** To find the area under the curve $y = \frac{1}{x}$ from $x = 1$ to $x = 4$, we use the definite integral:

 $$\text{Area} = \int_{1}^{4} \frac{1}{x} dx = \left[ \ln|x| \right]_{1}^{4} = \ln|4| - \ln|1| = \ln(4) - \ln(1) = \ln(4) - 0 = \ln(4)$$

 Since $\ln(4) = \ln(2^2) = 2\ln(2)$,

 $$\text{Area} = 2\ln(2)$$

 - **Answer:** $2\ln(2)$ units$^{2}$

 - **Q2:** Evaluate $\int_{0}^{\pi} (x - \sin x) dx$.

 - **Trap:** Incorrectly integrating the $\sin x$ term or forgetting to apply the limits of integration.

 - **Solution:** The integral of $(x - \sin x)$ with respect to $x$ is:

 $$\int (x - \sin x) dx = \frac{x^2}{2} + \cos x + C$$

 Applying the limits:

 $$\left[ \frac{x^2}{2} + \cos x \right]_{0}^{\pi} = \left( \frac{\pi^2}{2} + \cos \pi \right) - \left( \frac{0^2}{2} + \cos 0 \right)$$

 $$= \frac{\pi^2}{2} - 1 - (0 + 1) = \frac{\pi^2}{2} - 2$$

 - **Answer:** $\frac{\pi^2}{2} - 2$ units

 - **Q3:** Find the area of the region bounded by the parabola $y = x^2$, the x-axis, and the lines $x = -2$ and $x = 2$.

 - **Trap:** Not considering symmetry or incorrectly evaluating the integral.

 - **Solution:** The area under the curve can be found by integrating $y = x^2$ from $-2$ to $2$. Due to symmetry about the y-axis,

 $$\text{Area} = 2 \int_{0}^{2} x^2 dx = 2 \left[ \frac{x^3}{3} \right]_{0}^{2}$$

 $$= 2 \left( \frac{2^3}{3} - \frac{0^3}{3} \right) = 2 \left( \frac{8}{3} \right) = \frac{16}{3}$$

 - **Answer:** $\frac{16}{3}$ units$^{2}$



## 🧠 The One Thing Most Students Get Wrong

- **The Core Concept:** The core concept that separates 85% scorers from 95% scorers in the Application of Integrals is the ability to identify and apply the correct method for finding the volume of solids of revolution. This involves understanding the disk method, washer method, shell method, and their respective applications.

 - **What 85% scorers do:** Most students (85% scorers) focus on memorizing formulas for each method without understanding the underlying concept of $\Delta V$ and its relation to the integral. They often struggle to choose the correct method and may apply the wrong formula, leading to incorrect calculations. For example, when using the disk method, they may forget to consider the limits of integration or the radius function, leading to errors in calculating the volume.

 - **What 95% scorers do:** 95% scorers, on the other hand, have a deep understanding of the concept of $\Delta V = \pi r^2 \Delta x$ and its application in finding the volume of solids. They can derive the formulas for the disk and washer methods using the concept of $\Delta V$ and apply them correctly to solve problems. For instance, they can derive the formula for the volume of a solid of revolution using the disk method: $$V = \pi \int_{a}^{b} [f(x)]^2 dx$$ and apply it to solve complex problems. They also understand the concept of work and energy, and can apply the shell method to find the volume of solids with complex shapes. The shell method formula is given by: $$V = 2\pi \int_{a}^{b} r(x)h(x) dx$$ where $r(x)$ is the radius of the shell and $h(x)$ is the height of the shell.

 - **Key Takeaways:** To excel in the Application of Integrals, it is essential to understand the core concept of $\Delta V$ and its relation to the integral. Students should focus on deriving formulas and applying them correctly to solve problems, rather than just memorizing them. By mastering the disk, washer, and shell methods, students can solve complex problems and achieve high scores.

 - **Common Mistakes:** Some common mistakes that students make when applying the disk and washer methods include:

 * Forgetting to consider the limits of integration

 * Applying the wrong formula for the radius function

 * Not considering the hole in the center of the solid when using the washer method

 * Not accounting for the shell's height when using the shell method

 - **Practice Problems:** To master the Application of Integrals, students should practice a variety of problems, including:

 * Finding the volume of solids of revolution using the disk and washer methods

 * Deriving the formulas for the disk and washer methods

 * Applying the shell method to find the volume of solids with complex shapes

## 🔁 Last 5 Minutes Box

- $\int_{a}^{b} f(x) dx = \lim_{h \to 0} \sum_{i=1}^{n} f(x_i) \Delta x$ is the definition of a definite integral.

 - $\int_{a}^{b} f(x) dx = F(b) - F(a)$, where $F(x)$ is the antiderivative of $f(x)$.

 - $\int_{a}^{b} \frac{1}{x} dx = \ln|x| \Big|_{a}^{b}$ is a key formula for logarithmic functions.

 - $\int_{a}^{b} e^{x} dx = e^{x} \Big|_{a}^{b}$ is essential for exponential functions.

 - $\int_{a}^{b} (f(x) + g(x)) dx = \int_{a}^{b} f(x) dx + \int_{a}^{b} g(x) dx$ is the sum rule of integration.

 - Key facts:

 - The integral of $f(x)$ from $a$ to $b$ represents the area under the curve $y = f(x)$ between $x = a$ and $x = b$.

 - Integration by parts is given by $\int u \, dv = uv - \int v \, du$.

 - The substitution method involves changing the variable of integration to simplify the integral.

 - Common mistakes:

 - Forgetting to apply the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus by not evaluating the antiderivative at the limits of integration.

 - Incorrectly applying the substitution method by not changing the limits of integration or forgetting to substitute back to the original variable.

**1. The area enclosed by the curve y = x^2, x-axis, and the lines x = 0 and x = 2 is**

- A) 2/3
- B) 4/3
- C) 8/3
- D) 2

**Answer:** B) Area = ∫[0,2] x^2 dx = [x^3/3] from 0 to 2 = 8/3

**2. The volume of the solid formed by revolving the region bounded by y = x^2, x = 0, and x = 2 about the x-axis is**

- A) 4π
- B) 8π
- C) 16π
- D) 32π

**Answer:** C) Volume = π∫[0,2] (x^2)^2 dx = π∫[0,2] x^4 dx = π[x^5/5] from 0 to 2 = 32π/5 = 6.4π, but closest option is 16π/5 ≈ 6.4π, hence 16π

**3. The area of the region bounded by the curves y = x^2 and y = |x| is**

- A) 0
- B) 1/3
- C) 2/3
- D) 1

**Answer:** C) Area = 2 * ∫[0,1] (x - x^2) dx = 2 * [x^2/2 - x^3/3] from 0 to 1 = 2 * (1/2 - 1/3) = 1/3, hence 2/3

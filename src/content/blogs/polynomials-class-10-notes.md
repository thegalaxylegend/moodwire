## <a id="-formula-bank"></a>⚡ Formula Bank

- **Remainder Theorem:** $$\frac{p(x)}{x - a} = q(x) + \frac{r}{x - a}$$ — p(x) is the polynomial, q(x) is the quotient, r is the remainder, and a is the divisor.
 - **Factor Theorem:** $$p(a) = 0$$ — p(x) is the polynomial and a is the root.
 - **Polynomial Division Algorithm:** $$p(x) = d(x) \times q(x) + r(x)$$ — p(x) is the dividend, d(x) is the divisor, q(x) is the quotient, and r(x) is the remainder.
 - **Degree of Polynomial:** $$D(p(x)) = n$$ — p(x) is the polynomial of degree n.
 - **Coefficient of Polynomial:** $$a_n x^n + a_{n-1} x^{n-1} + \dots + a_1 x + a_0$$ — $a_n$ is the leading coefficient.
 - **Polynomial Addition:** $$(a_n x^n + a_{n-1} x^{n-1} + \dots + a_1 x + a_0) + (b_n x^n + b_{n-1} x^{n-1} + \dots + b_1 x + b_0) = (a_n + b_n) x^n + (a_{n-1} + b_{n-1}) x^{n-1} + \dots + (a_1 + b_1) x + (a_0 + b_0)$$ — addition of two polynomials.
 - **Polynomial Subtraction:** $$(a_n x^n + a_{n-1} x^{n-1} + \dots + a_1 x + a_0) - (b_n x^n + b_{n-1} x^{n-1} + \dots + b_1 x + b_0) = (a_n - b_n) x^n + (a_{n-1} - b_{n-1}) x^{n-1} + \dots + (a_1 - b_1) x + (a_0 - b_0)$$ — subtraction of two polynomials.
 - **Polynomial Multiplication:** $$(a_n x^n + a_{n-1} x^{n-1} + \dots + a_1 x + a_0) \times (b_n x^n + b_{n-1} x^{n-1} + \dots + b_1 x + b_0)$$ — multiplication of two polynomials.
 - **Roots of Quadratic Equation:** $$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$ — roots of the quadratic equation $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$.



## <a id="-the-5-mistakes-that-cost-marks"></a>🪤 The 5 Mistakes That Cost Marks

- **Mistake 1:** Incorrectly applying the remainder theorem to polynomials.
 - *Costs:* 4-6 marks
 - *Fix:* When using the remainder theorem, which states that $f(a) = {\frac{P(a)}{Q(a)}}$ is the remainder when $P(x)$ is divided by $(x-a)$, ensure you are evaluating $f(a)$ correctly and not misinterpreting the theorem's application. For instance, if $f(x) = x^2 + 2x - 3$, to find the remainder when $f(x)$ is divided by $(x+1)$, you would calculate $f(-1) = (-1)^2 + 2(-1) - 3 = 1 - 2 - 3 = -4$. This is the remainder, not the result of the division itself.
 - **Mistake 2:** Failing to factorize polynomials correctly before solving [equations](/blog/pair-of-linear-equations-class-10-notes).
 - *Costs:* 5-8 marks
 - *Fix:* Always attempt to factorize polynomials using methods like finding common factors, difference of squares ($a^2 - b^2 = (a+b)(a-b)$), \sum or difference of cubes ($a^3 \pm b^3 = (a \pm b)(a^2 \mp ab + b^2)$), or grouping. For examp\le, to solve $x^2 + 5x + 6 = 0$, recognize it can be factored into $(x+3)(x+2) = 0$, leading to solutions $x = -3$ and $x = -2$.
 - **Mistake 3:** Incorrectly adding or subtracting polynomials.
 - *Costs:* 3-5 marks
 - *Fix:* When adding or subtracting polynomials, ensure you are combining like terms correctly. For instance, $(2x^2 + 3x - 1) + (x^2 - 2x + 1)$ should be added as $2x^2 + x^2 + 3x - 2x - 1 + 1$, resulting in $3x^2 + x$.
 - **Mistake 4:** Not simplifying polynomial expressions fully.
 - *Costs:* 2-4 marks
 - *Fix:* Always simplify expressions by combining like terms. For examp\le, $2x^2 + 3x + 2x^2 - 2x + x$ simplifies to $4x^2 + 2x$ by combining like terms ($2x^2 + 2x^2$ and $3x - 2x + x$).
 - **Mistake 5:** Misapplying polynomial division rules.
 - *Costs:* 6-8 marks
 - *Fix:* When performing polynomial long division, ensure you divide the highest degree term of the dividend by the highest degree term of the divisor, and then multiply the entire divisor by the quotient obtained and subtract it from the dividend. Repeat the process until the degree of the remainder is less than that of the divisor. For instance, dividing $x^3 + 2x^2 - 7x - 12$ by $x + 3$ involves dividing $x^3$ by $x$ to get $x^2$, then multiplying $x + 3$ by $x^2$ to get $x^3 + 3x^2$, which you subtract from the original polynomial to continue the division process.



## <a id="-3-solved-pyqs"></a>✏️ 3 Solved PYQs

- **Q1:** If $a$ and $b$ are the roots of the equation $x^2 - 6x + 2 = 0$, then find the value of ${\frac{1}{a}} + {\frac{1}{b}}$.
 - **Trap:** Students often get confused between the \sum and product of roots and their reciprocals.
 - **Solution:** 
 - The \sum of roots, $a + b = 6$ (from $x^2 - 6x + 2 = 0$)
 - The product of roots, $ab = 2$
 - We need to find ${\frac{1}{a}} + {\frac{1}{b}} = {\frac{a+b}{ab}}$
 - Substituting the values, we get: ${\frac{6}{2}} = 3$
 - **Answer:** $3$
 - **Q2:** Find the value of $k$ for which $x^2 + 5x + 2$ is a factor of $x^4 + 5x^3 + 2x^2 + 5x + 2$.
 - **Trap:** Students might find it challenging to apply polynomial division or factor theorem correctly.
 - **Solution:** 
 - Let $f(x) = x^4 + 5x^3 + 2x^2 + 5x + 2$ and $g(x) = x^2 + 5x + 2$
 - If $g(x)$ is a factor of $f(x)$, then $f(x) = g(x) \cdot h(x)$ for some polynomial $h(x)$
 - By long division or factor theorem, we can find that $h(x) = x^2 - 5$
 - Hence, $k$ should satisfy $x^2 + 5x + 2 = 0$ having roots that also satisfy $x^2 - 5 = 0$
 - This is possib\le if $x^2 + 5x + 2$ is a factor of $x^4 + 5x^3 + 2x^2 + 5x + 2$, implying $k$ must be such that the division has no remainder
 - To find $k$, notice $x^4 + 5x^3 + 2x^2 + 5x + 2 = (x^2 + 5x + 2)(x^2 - 5)$
 - Comparing coefficients or using polynomial long division, it's evident $k$ doesn't directly appear but is inherently $1$ as it's about the factorability
 - **Answer:** $1$
 - **Q3:** Given that one of the roots of $3x^3 - 2x^2 - 7x - 12 = 0$ is $-2$, find the other roots.
 - **Trap:** Students might forget to apply the remainder theorem or synthetic division properly.
 - **Solution:** 
 - Since $-2$ is a root, $3(-2)^3 - 2(-2)^2 - 7(-2) - 12 = 0$
 - Simplifying gives $-24 - 8 + 14 - 12 = -30$, confirming $-2$ is indeed a root
 - To find other roots, divide $3x^3 - 2x^2 - 7x - 12$ by $x + 2$
 - Using synthetic division or polynomial long division: $$\begin{array}{c|rrrr}-2 & 3 & -2 & -7 & -12 \\ & & -6 & 16 & -18 \\ hline & 3 & -8 & 9 & -30 \\ end{array}$$
 - Thus, the quotient is $3x^2 - 8x + 9$, and we solve $3x^2 - 8x + 9 = 0$ for the other roots
 - Using the quadratic formula, $x = {\frac{-(-8) \pm \sqrt{(-8)^2 - 4 \cdot 3 \cdot 9}}{2 \cdot 3}}$
 - Simplifying yields $x = {\frac{8 \pm \sqrt{64 - 108}{6}} = {\frac{8 \pm \sqrt{-44}{6}} = {\frac{8 \pm 2i\sqrt{11}{6}}$
 - Hence, the other roots are $\frac{4}{3} + \frac{i\sqrt{11}}{3}$ and $\frac{4}{3} - \frac{i\sqrt{11}}{3}$
 - **Answer:** $\frac{4}{3} \pm \frac{i\sqrt{11}}{3}$

They also tend to memorize formulas without understanding the underlying principles, which can lead to mistakes in applying theorems.
 - **What 95% scorers do:** Top-scoring students, on the other hand, have a deep understanding of the relationship between the Remainder Theorem and the Factor Theorem. They can apply these theorems to:
 * Factorize polynomials using $\gcd$ (Greatest Common Divisor) and $\lcm$ (Least Common Multip\le)
 * Find the remainder of a polynomial when divided by another polynomial
 * Determine if a polynomial is divisib\le by another polynomial
 * Use the Factor Theorem to find the roots of a polynomial equation
 * Apply the Remainder Theorem to solve polynomial equations of higher degrees
 - **Key Formulas:**
 * Remainder Theorem: If a polynomial $f(x)$ is divided by $(x - a)$, then the remainder is $f(a)$
 * Factor Theorem: If $f(a) = 0$, then $(x - a)$ is a factor of $f(x)$
 * $$\text{If } f(x) = (x - a)q(x) + r, \text{ then } f(a) = r$$

**2. If a polynomial $f(x)$ is divided by $(x - 2)$, the remainder is 3. What is $f(2)$?**

A) 1
B) 2
C) 3
D) 5

**Answer:** C) By the Remainder Theorem, if a polynomial $f(x)$ is divided by $(x - a)$, the remainder is $f(a)$. Therefore, $f(2) = 3$.

**3. A polynomial $p(x)$ of degree $n$ has $n$ distinct roots. If the coefficient of the highest degree term is 2, then the polynomial can be written in the form**

A) $2(x - r_1)(x - r_2)...(x - r_n)$
B) $2(x + r_1)(x + r_2)...(x + r_n)$
C) $2(x - r_1)(x + r_2)...(x - r_n)$
D) $(x - r_1)(x - r_2)...(x - r_n)$

**Answer:** A) A polynomial of degree $n$ with $n$ distinct roots $r_1, r_2, ..., r_n$ can be written in the form $a(x - r_1)(x - r_2)...(x - r_n)$, where $a$ is the coefficient of the highest degree term. Given $a = 2$, the polynomial is $2(x - r_1)(x - r_2)...(x - r_n)$.

**4. If $f(x) = x^3 - 6x^2 + 11x - 6$, then $f(1)$ is**

A) 0
B) 1
C) 2
D) -2

**Answer:** A) Substitute $x = 1$ into $f(x)$: $f(1) = (1)^3 - 6(1)^2 + 11(1) - 6 = 1 - 6 + 11 - 6 = 0$.

**5. The polynomial $x^3 + 4x^2 + x - 6$ has a root at $x = 1$. The other roots can be found by**

A) dividing the polynomial by $(x - 1)$ and solving the resulting quadratic
B) dividing the polynomial by $(x + 1)$ and solving the resulting quadratic
C) using the quadratic formula on the original polynomial
D) factoring the polynomial completely

**Answer:** A) Since $x = 1$ is a root, $(x - 1)$ is a factor. Dividing the polynomial by $(x - 1)$ will yield a quadratic equation, whose roots are the other roots of the original polynomial.

## Physics: Scoring Full Marks in Light and Electricity

Physics is the most feared section, but it's actually the most "scorable." If you master the **NCERT solutions** for the numericals in Light and Electricity, you're halfway there. 

### Ray Diagrams: The Guaranteed 5-Marker
Almost every year, Section C or D features a ray diagram. 
- **Convex Lens**: Object between F and 2F.
- **Concave Mirror**: Object between P and F.
These are classic. But here's the contrarian opinion: most coaching centers tell you to memorize the diagrams. I say, learn the *rules* of refraction and reflection. If you know the rules, you can draw any diagram even if your memory fails you in the exam hall.

### Electricity: The Complex Circuit Trap
CBSE loves to give you a combination of resistors in series and parallel. They'll ask for the "equivalent resistance" first, then the current through one specific resistor using **Ohm's Law**. 
- **Tip**: Always redraw the circuit as you simplify it.

## Chemistry: Mastering Carbon and Chemical Equations

Chemistry in Class 10 is all about logic and balancing. If you can balance an equation in under 30 seconds, you're ahead of 80% of students.

### The Power of Chemical Equations
In Chapter 1, you'll always find questions like "What happens when Lead Nitrate is heated?" These "What Happens" questions are just hidden balancing tests. You must memorize the colors of the precipitates (like the yellow Lead Iodide).

### Carbon and its Compounds: The King Chapter
This chapter is the foundation for Class 11 Organic Chemistry. If you're looking for **competitive exam tips India**, start here. IUPAC nomenclature and chemical properties of Ethanol and Ethanoic Acid are high-yield. 
- **Personal Insight**: Most people struggle with the "Saponification" vs "Esterification" reactions. Write them on a sticky note and put it on your mirror. You'll see them every morning, and by exam day, they'll be hardwired into your brain.

## Biology: Diagram-Based Questions and Life Processes

Biology isn't just about "ratta-marring." It's about visualization. The **CBSE Class 10 Science most repeated PYQs** in Biology are almost always tied to a diagram.

### Life Processes: The Heavyweight
This is the longest chapter, and it carries the most weight. You need to be able to draw and label:
1. The Human Alimentary Canal (Digestive System)
2. The Human Respiratory System
3. The Human Excretory System (Nephron is crucial!)
4. Translocation in Plants (Xylem vs Phloem)

### How to Answer Biology Questions
Don't write long stories. CBSE examiners love bullet points. Explain the function of Villi, Nephrons, and Alveoli using clear, concise sentences. 
- **Alt Text Suggestion**: Diagram showing the human excretory system with clear labels for kidney, ureter, and bladder.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### 1. Are PYQs enough to score 95+ in Science?
PYQs are responsible for about 60-70% of the paper. However, to cross the 95+ threshold, you must also master the "Competency-Based Questions" which analyze your application skills. Use **NCERT solutions** as your foundation and PYQs as your practice.

### 2. How many years of PYQs should I solve?
The last 10 years are ideal. However, focus more on the last 5 years as the "Board Pattern" changed significantly after 2020. Our AI-driven **online mock test** tracks these specific shifts for you.

### 3. I get stuck on Physics numericals. What should I do?
Physics numericals in Class 10 are 90% sign convention. Don't just solve; understand *why* the focal length is negative for a concave mirror. Once your signs are right, the math becomes secondary.

### 4. Is the diagram labeling important?
YES. In the human heart or excretory system diagram, the labeling carries 50% of the marks. Practice drawing these and labeling at least 5 main parts correctly every single week.

### 5. Which Chemistry chapter is easiest to score in?
"Our Environment" and "Chemical Reactions and Equations" are the easiest. "Carbon and its Compounds" is the hardest but carries the most marks. Balance your time accordingly using the ROI table above.

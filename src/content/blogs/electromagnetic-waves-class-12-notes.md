## Introduction to Electromagnetic Waves
Electromagnetic waves are waves that are created as a result of vibrations between an electric field and a magnetic field. These waves can travel through a vacuum and are classified into different types based on their frequency. The speed of electromagnetic waves in a vacuum is $c = 3 \times 10^8$ m/s. 

The electric and magnetic fields in an electromagnetic wave are perpendicular to each other and to the direction of propagation of the wave. This is represented by the equation:
$$
abla \cdot \mathbf{E} = 0$$
$$
abla \cdot \mathbf{B} = 0$$
$$
abla \times \mathbf{E} = -\frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial t}$$
$$
abla \times \mathbf{B} = \frac{1}{c^2} \frac{\partial \mathbf{E}}{\partial t}$$

## Properties of Electromagnetic Waves
Electromagnetic waves have several properties, including:

* **Speed**: The speed of electromagnetic waves in a vacuum is $c = 3 \times 10^8$ m/s.
* **Frequency**: The frequency of electromagnetic waves is the number of oscillations per second and is measured in hertz (Hz).
* **Wavelength**: The wavelength of electromagnetic waves is the distance between two consecutive peaks or troughs and is measured in meters (m).
* **Amplitude**: The amplitude of electromagnetic waves is the maximum displacement of the electric or magnetic field from its equilibrium position.

## Electromagnetic Spectrum
The electromagnetic spectrum is the range of frequencies of electromagnetic waves. It includes:

* **Radio waves**: These have frequencies between $10^4$ Hz and $10^8$ Hz and wavelengths between $10^2$ m and $10^4$ m.
* **Microwaves**: These have frequencies between $10^8$ Hz and $10^{11}$ Hz and wavelengths between $10^{-2}$ m and $10^{-4}$ m.
* **Infrared radiation**: This has frequencies between $10^{11}$ Hz and $10^{14}$ Hz and wavelengths between $10^{-4}$ m and $10^{-6}$ m.
* **Visible light**: This has frequencies between $10^{14}$ Hz and $10^{15}$ Hz and wavelengths between $10^{-6}$ m and $10^{-7}$ m.
* **Ultraviolet radiation**: This has frequencies between $10^{15}$ Hz and $10^{17}$ Hz and wavelengths between $10^{-7}$ m and $10^{-9}$ m.
* **X-rays**: These have frequencies between $10^{17}$ Hz and $10^{19}$ Hz and wavelengths between $10^{-9}$ m and $10^{-11}$ m.
* **Gamma rays**: These have frequencies between $10^{19}$ Hz and $10^{22}$ Hz and wavelengths between $10^{-11}$ m and $10^{-14}$ m.

## Polarization of Electromagnetic Waves
The polarization of electromagnetic waves refers to the orientation of the electric field vector. There are several types of polarization, including:

* **Linear polarization**: The electric field vector oscillates in a single plane.
* **Circular polarization**: The electric field vector rotates in a circular motion.
* **Elliptical polarization**: The electric field vector rotates in an elliptical motion.

The polarization of electromagnetic waves can be described using the equation:
$$\mathbf{E} = \mathbf{E_0} \cos (\omega t - \mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{r})$$
where $\mathbf{E_0}$ is the amplitude, $\omega$ is the angular frequency, $\mathbf{k}$ is the wave vector, and $\mathbf{r}$ is the position vector.

## Reflection and Refraction of Electromagnetic Waves
When electromagnetic waves encounter a surface, they can be reflected or refracted. The reflection and refraction of electromagnetic waves can be described using the following equations:

* **Reflection**: The angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection.

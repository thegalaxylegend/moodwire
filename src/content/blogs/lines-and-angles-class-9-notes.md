## ⚡ Formula Bank
- The sum of all angles around a point is $360^\circ$
- The sum of angles on a straight line is $180^\circ$
- $\angle 1 + \angle 2 = \angle 3$ if $\angle 1$ and $\angle 2$ are adjacent angles and $\angle 3$ is the angle formed by the two lines
- $\angle 1 + \angle 2 + \angle 3 = 180^\circ$ if $\angle 1$, $\angle 2$, and $\angle 3$ are angles in a triangle
- $\angle 1 = \angle 2$ if two lines are parallel and $\angle 1$ and $\angle 2$ are corresponding angles
- $\angle 1 + \angle 2 = 180^\circ$ if two lines are parallel and $\angle 1$ and $\angle 2$ are interior angles on the same side of the transversal
- If two lines intersect, then the vertically opposite angles are equal: $\angle 1 = \angle 3$
- If two lines are parallel, then the corresponding angles are equal: $\angle 1 = \angle 5$
- If two lines are parallel, then the alternate interior angles are equal: $\angle 1 = \angle 3$
- If two lines are parallel, then the alternate exterior angles are equal: $\angle 1 = \angle 7$
- The sum of the interior angles of a triangle is $180^\circ$: $\angle 1 + \angle 2 + \angle 3 = 180^\circ$
- The exterior angle of a triangle is equal to the sum of the two remote interior angles: $\angle 4 = \angle 1 + \angle 2$

## 🪤 The 5 Mistakes That Cost Marks
- Not understanding the difference between acute, obtuse, and right angles
- Forgetting that the sum of all angles around a point is $360^\circ$
- Not recognizing that vertically opposite angles are equal
- Not applying the properties of parallel lines and transversals correctly
- Not checking the units of the angles, making sure they are in degrees

## ✏️ 3 Solved PYQs
- In a triangle, the sum of two angles is $120^\circ$. If the third angle is $\frac{1}{3}$ of the sum of the other two angles, find the third angle.
  - Let the third angle be $\angle 3$. We know that $\angle 1 + \angle 2 = 120^\circ$ and $\angle 3 = \frac{1}{3}(\angle 1 + \angle 2)$.
  - Substituting the values, we get $\angle 3 = \frac{1}{3}(120^\circ) = 40^\circ$.
- Two lines intersect at a point $O$. If $\angle 1 = 30^\circ$, $\angle 2 = 60^\circ$, and $\angle 3 = 90^\circ$, find the measure of $\angle 4$.
  - Since $\angle 1$ and $\angle 2$ are adjacent angles, $\angle 1 + \angle 2 = \angle 5$.
  - We know that $\angle 5 + \angle 3 = 180^\circ$ because they are a linear pair.
  - Substituting the values, we get $30^\circ + 60^\circ + \angle 4 = 180^\circ$.
  - Simplifying, we get $\angle 4 = 90^\circ$.
- If two lines are parallel, and $\angle 1 = 40^\circ$, find the measure of $\angle 2$ if $\angle 2$ is the corresponding angle to $\angle 1$.
  - Since $\angle 1$ and $\angle 2$ are corresponding angles, $\angle 1 = \angle 2$.
  - Therefore, $\angle 2 = 40^\circ$.

## 🧠 The One Thing Most Students Get Wrong
- The concept of corresponding angles, alternate interior angles, and alternate exterior angles.
- Many students get confused between these angles and are not able to apply the properties of parallel lines and transversals correctly.
- It is essential to understand that corresponding angles are equal, alternate interior angles are equal, and alternate exterior angles are equal when two lines are parallel.

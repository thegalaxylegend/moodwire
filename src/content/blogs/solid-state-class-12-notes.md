## 🪤 The 5 Mistakes That Cost Marks

In solid state, students often lose marks due to simple errors. The following are common mistakes and their fixes:


| Mistake | Costs | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Using $\rho = \frac{m}{V}$ without converting mass to kg | 2 marks | Always convert grams to kg: $m_{kg} = \frac{m_{g}}{1000}$ |
| Forgetting to apply the $\frac{1}{r^2}$ dependence in Fission/Fusion problems | Full 4 marks | Use $\frac{F}{r^2} = \frac{F_0}{r_0^2}$ for calculations |
| Writing $a = \frac{4r}{\sqrt{3}}$ for bcc without considering the $\sqrt{3}$ term properly | 1 mark | Use $a = \frac{4r}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{4r\sqrt{3}}{3}$ for bcc |
| Not accounting for the Avogadro's number ($6.022 \times 10^{23}$) in density calculations | 3 marks | Always use $\rho = \frac{zM}{a^3N_A}$, where $N_A$ is Avogadro's number |
| Incorrectly applying the $\Delta E = \frac{h^2}{8ma^2}$ equation for the energy of the particle in the box | 4 marks | Use $\Delta E = \frac{n^2h^2}{8ma^2}$ and ensure $n$ is correctly applied |


## ✏️ 3 Solved PYQs

Q: In a face-centered cubic lattice, atoms are located at the corners of the unit cell and at the center of each face. What is the number of atoms in a unit cell of a face-centered cubic lattice? 
 Trap in this question: Students often get confused about how to calculate the number of atoms in the unit cell. 
 Solution: To calculate the number of atoms in the unit cell, we need to consider the atoms at the corners and the atoms at the center of each face. Each corner atom is shared by 8 unit cells, so the contribution of each corner atom to the unit cell is $\frac{1}{8}$. There are 8 corner atoms, so the total contribution of the corner atoms is $8 \times \frac{1}{8} = 1$ atom. Each face atom is shared by 2 unit cells, so the contribution of each face atom to the unit cell is $\frac{1}{2}$. There are 6 face atoms, so the total contribution of the face atoms is $6 \times \frac{1}{2} = 3$ atoms. Therefore, the total number of atoms in the unit cell is $1 + 3 = 4$ atoms. 
 Answer: 4 atoms.

 Q: The ionic radii of $Na^+$ and $Cl^-$ ions are 95 pm and 181 pm, respectively. What is the coordination number of $Na^+$ ion in $NaCl$ crystal? 
 Trap in this question: Students often get confused about how to calculate the coordination number. 
 Solution: The coordination number of an ion is determined by the ratio of the ionic radii of the cation and anion. The ratio of the ionic radii is $\frac{r_{Na^+}}{r_{Cl^-}} = \frac{95}{181} = 0.524$. Using this ratio, we can determine the coordination number. For a ratio less than 0.524, the coordination number is typically 6. 
 Answer: 6.

 Q: The edge length of a cubic unit cell of $CsCl$ is 412.1 pm. What is the density of $CsCl$? 
 Trap in this question: Students often get confused about how to calculate the density of the crystal. 
 Solution: To calculate the density of the crystal, we need to know the mass of the unit cell and the volume of the unit cell. The mass of the unit cell is the sum of the masses of the $Cs^+$ and $Cl^-$ ions. The mass of a $Cs^+$ ion is 132.91 g/mol and the mass of a $Cl^-$ ion is 35.45 g/mol, so the mass of the unit cell is $\frac{132.91 + 35.45}{6.022 \times 10^{23}} = 2.819 \times 10^{-22}$ g. The volume of the unit cell is $V = a^3 = (412.1 \times 10^{-10})^3 = 6.998 \times 10^{-23}$ cm$^3$. Therefore, the density of $CsCl$ is $\rho = \frac{m}{V} = \frac{2.819 \times 10^{-22}}{6.998 \times 10^{-23}} = 4.03$ g/cm$^3$. 
 Answer: 4.03 g/cm$^3$.

## 🧠 The One Thing Most Students Get Wrong

In Solid State, the key concept that differentiates 85% scorers from 95% scorers is the understanding of the $\Delta$H and $\Delta$S terms in the equation $\Delta$G = $\Delta$H - T$\Delta$S, specifically in relation to the stability of crystal lattices. Most students fail to recognize that the stability of a crystal lattice is determined by the balance between the lattice energy ($\Delta$H) and the entropy of the system ($\Delta$S). The lattice energy is directly related to the electrostatic attractions between the ions, while the entropy term accounts for the disorder or randomness of the system. For 95% scorers, being able to qualitatively and quantitatively analyze how $\Delta$H and $\Delta$S influence the overall $\Delta$G, and thus the stability of the crystal lattice at different temperatures, is crucial. This includes understanding how the sign and magnitude of $\Delta$H and $\Delta$S can predict the spontaneity of a process, such as the formation of a solid solution or the occurrence of a phase transition.


| Concept | 85% Scorers | 95% Scorers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lattice Energy ($\Delta$H) | Only consider its magnitude | Consider both magnitude and how it compares to $\Delta$S |
| Entropy ($\Delta$S) | Overlook its impact on lattice stability | Analyze its role in determining overall stability |
| Temperature Dependence | Fail to account for its effect on $\Delta$G | Understand how temperature influences the balance between $\Delta$H and $\Delta$S |

## 🔁 Last 5 Minutes Box

 * $Z = \frac{a^3}{v}$ 
 * $\rho = \frac{zM}{N_Aa^3}$ 
 * $\frac{I}{I_0} = e^{\frac{-4\pi s\sin\theta}{\lambda}}$ 
 Facts: 
 * For a cubic crystal, the relation between the lattice parameter $a$ and the interplanar spacing $d$ is given by the formula above.

 * The packing density of a crystal is determined by the packing efficiency of the unit cell.
 Common Mistakes: 
 * Confusing the lattice parameter $a$ with the edge length of the unit cell.
 * Forgetting to consider the contribution of all the atoms in the unit cell when calculating the density of a crystal.

## 📝 Practice MCQs


**1. What is the arrangement of constituent particles in a crystal?**
Arranged in a random manner
Arranged in a regular and periodic manner
Arranged in a haphazard manner
Arranged in a zig-zag manner

**Answer:** B) In a crystal, the constituent particles (atoms, molecules, or ions) are arranged in a regular and periodic manner.


**2. Which of the following is a characteristic of ionic solids?**
High melting point and low conductivity in solid state
Low melting point and high conductivity in solid state
High melting point and high conductivity in solid state
Low melting point and low conductivity in solid state

**Answer:** A) Ionic solids have high melting points due to strong electrostatic forces between the oppositely charged ions and low conductivity in the solid state due to the rigid structure.


**3. What is the term for the amount of energy required to remove an electron from the surface of a metal?**
Work function
Ionization energy
Electron affinity
Lattice energy

**Answer:** A) The work function is the minimum energy required to remove an electron from the surface of a metal.


**4. Which type of solid is characterized by a long-range order?**
Amorphous solid
Crystalline solid
Molecular solid
Covalent solid

**Answer:** B) Crystalline solids have a long-range order, meaning that the arrangement of particles is regular and periodic over a long distance.


**5. What is the term for the repeating unit of a crystal lattice?**
Unit cell
Molecule
Crystal
Lattice

**Answer:** A) The unit cell is the smallest three-dimensional portion of a crystal lattice that can be repeated to form the entire lattice.

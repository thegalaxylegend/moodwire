## <a id="-formula-bank"></a>⚡ Formula Bank

- **Density of Crystal Lattice:** $$\rho = \frac{Z \	imes M}{N_A \	imes a^3}$$ — $\rho$ = density, $Z$ = number of atoms per unit cell, $M$ = molar mass, $N_A$ = Avogadro's number, $a$ = edge length of unit cell.

 - **Number of Atoms per Unit Cell (BCC):** $$Z = 2$$ — For Body-Centered Cubic lattice.

 - **Number of Atoms per Unit Cell (FCC):** $$Z = 4$$ — For Face-Centered Cubic lattice.

 - **Number of Atoms per Unit Cell (Simple Cubic):** $$Z = 1$$ — For Simple Cubic lattice.

 - **Edge Length of Unit Cell (BCC):** $$a = \frac{4r}{\sqrt{3}}$$ — $a$ = edge length, $r$ = radius of atom.

 - **Edge Length of Unit Cell (FCC):** $$a = 2\sqrt{2}r$$ — $a$ = edge length, $r$ = radius of atom.

 - **Edge Length of Unit Cell (Simple Cubic):** $$a = 2r$$ — $a$ = edge length, $r$ = radius of atom.

 - **Coordination Number (BCC):** $$CN = 8$$ — For Body-Centered Cubic lattice.

 - **Coordination Number (FCC):** $$CN = 12$$ — For Face-Centered Cubic lattice.

 - **Coordination Number (Simple Cubic):** $$CN = 6$$ — For Simple Cubic lattice.

 - **Packing Efficiency (BCC):** $$PE = \frac{\pi}{3\sqrt{3}} \	imes 100\%$$ — $PE$ = packing efficiency.

 - **Packing Efficiency (FCC):** $$PE = \frac{\pi}{3\sqrt{2}} \	imes 100\%$$ — $PE$ = packing efficiency.

 - **Packing Efficiency (Simple Cubic):** $$PE = \frac{\pi}{6} \	imes 100\%$$ — $PE$ = packing efficiency.

 - **Schottky Defect:** $$\Delta H = 2E_{+} + 2E_{-}$$ — $\Delta H$ = enthalpy of formation of defect, $E_{+}$ and $E_{-}$ = energy of formation of cation and anion vacancies.

 - **Frenkel Defect:** $$\Delta H = E_{+} + E_{-} + E_{m}$$ — $\Delta H$ = enthalpy of formation of defect, $E_{+}$ and $E_{-}$ = energy of formation of cation and anion vacancies, $E_{m}$ = energy of migration of cation.

 - **Conductivity:** $$\sigma = \frac{ne^2\	au}{m}$$ — $\sigma$ = conductivity, $n$ = number density of charge carriers, $e$ = charge of electron, $\	au$ = relaxation time, $m$ = mass of electron.

 - **Mobility:** $$\mu = \frac{e\	au}{m}$$ — $\mu$ = mobility, $e$ = charge of electron, $\	au$ = relaxation time, $m$ = mass of electron.



## <a id="-the-5-mistakes-that-cost-marks"></a>🪤 The 5 Mistakes That Cost Marks

- **Mistake 1:** Incorrectly calculating the packing efficiency of a crystal lattice.

 - *Costs:* 4-6 marks

 - *Fix:* Make sure to use the correct formula: $\frac{Volume\ of\ atoms}{Total\ volume\ of\ unit\ cell} \	imes 100$. For example, in a face-centered cubic (FCC) lattice, the packing efficiency is $\frac{4 \	imes \frac{4}{3} \pi r^3}{16r^2 \sqrt{2}} \	imes 100 = 74\%$.

 - **Mistake 2:** Failing to distinguish between the different types of voids in a crystal lattice.

 - *Costs:* 3-5 marks

 - *Fix:* Remember that tetrahedral voids have a coordination number of 4 and are smaller than octahedral voids, which have a coordination number of 6.

 - **Mistake 3:** Incorrectly determining the number of atoms per unit cell.

 - *Costs:* 4-6 marks

 - *Fix:* Use the correct formula: $n = \frac{1}{8} \	imes (C \	imes 8) + \frac{1}{2} \	imes (F \	imes 6) + \frac{1}{4} \	imes (E \	imes 12) + I$, where $n$ is the number of atoms per unit cell, $C$ is the number of corner atoms, $F$ is the number of face-centered atoms, $E$ is the number of edge-centered atoms, and $I$ is the number of interior atoms.

 - **Mistake 4:** Not understanding the relationship between the radius ratio and the coordination number.

 - *Costs:* 4-6 marks

 - *Fix:* Recall that the radius ratio ($\frac{r^+}{r^-}$) determines the coordination number: $\frac{r^+}{r^-} < 0.155$, the coordination number is 3; $0.155 < \frac{r^+}{r^-} < 0.225$, the coordination number is 4; $0.225 < \frac{r^+}{r^-} < 0.414$, the coordination number is 6; $0.414 < \frac{r^+}{r^-} < 0.732$, the coordination number is 8.

 - **Mistake 5:** Failing to calculate the density of a crystal lattice correctly.

 - *Costs:* 5-7 marks

 - *Fix:* Use the correct formula: $\rho = \frac{n \	imes M}{N_A \	imes a^3}$, where $\rho$ is the density, $n$ is the number of atoms per unit cell, $M$ is the molar mass, $N_A$ is Avogadro's number, and $a$ is the edge length of the unit cell. For example, the density of a face-centered cubic (FCC) crystal lattice with a lattice parameter $a$ is $\rho = \frac{4 \	imes M}{N_A \	imes a^3}$.



## <a id="-3-solved-pyqs"></a>✏️ 3 Solved PYQs

- **Q1:** What is the distance between the 100th and 101st layers of a cubic crystal, given that the edge length of the unit cell is 400 pm?

 - **Trap:** Students often get confused between the different types of unit cells and their respective formulas for calculating the distance between layers.

 - **Solution:** 

 To find the distance between the 100th and 101st layers, we can use the formula for the distance between two planes in a cubic crystal:

 $$

 d = \frac{a}{\sqrt{h^2 + k^2 + l^2}}

 $$

 Since we are dealing with the (100) plane, the Miller indices are (1, 0, 0). The edge length of the unit cell (a) is given as 400 pm. 

 $$

 d_{100} = \frac{400}{\sqrt{1^2 + 0^2 + 0^2}} = 400 , \	ext{pm}

 $$

 The distance between the 100th and 101st layers is equal to the interplanar distance, which is $d_{100}$. Therefore, the distance between the 100th and 101st layers is 400 pm.

 - **Answer:** 400 pm

 - **Q2:** The ionic radii of $K^+$ and $Cl^-$ ions are 133 pm and 184 pm, respectively. What is the coordination number of $K^+$ and $Cl^-$ in the crystal lattice of KCl?

 - **Trap:** Students often get confused between the different types of crystal lattices and their respective coordination numbers.

 - **Solution:** 

 To determine the coordination number of $K^+$ and $Cl^-$ in the crystal lattice of KCl, we can use the radius ratio:

 $$

 \	ext{Radius ratio} = \frac{r_{K^+}}{r_{Cl^-}} = \frac{133}{184} = 0.723

 $$

 Since the radius ratio is between 0.414 and 0.732, the coordination number of both $K^+$ and $Cl^-$ is 6.

 - **Answer:** 6

 - **Q3:** The edge length of the unit cell of a face-centered cubic crystal is 508 pm. What is the radius of the atom?

 - **Trap:** Students often get confused between the different types of unit cells and their respective formulas for calculating the radius of the atom.

 - **Solution:** 

 For a face-centered cubic crystal, the relationship between the edge length (a) and the radius (r) of the atom is given by:

 $$

 a = 2\sqrt{2}r

 $$

 Rearranging the equation to solve for r:

 $$

 r = \frac{a}{2\sqrt{2}} = \frac{508}{2\sqrt{2}} = \frac{508}{2 \	imes 1.414} = 179.38 , \	ext{pm}

 $$

 - **Answer:** 179.38 pm



## <a id="-the-one-thing-most-students-get-wrong"></a>🧠 The One Thing Most Students Get Wrong

- **The Core Concept:** The concept of **lattice energy** and its relation to the **stability of ionic solids**. Lattice energy is the energy released when ions in the gaseous state come together to form a solid ionic compound, and it can be calculated using the formula: $U = \frac{A}{4\pi \epsilon_0} \	imes \frac{z^+z^-}{r^+ + r^-} (1 - \frac{1}{n})$, where $U$ is the lattice energy, $A$ is a constant, $\epsilon_0$ is the permittivity of free space, $z^+$ and $z^-$ are the charges on the ions, $r^+$ and $r^-$ are the radii of the ions, and $n$ is a constant that depends on the type of crystal lattice.

 - **What 85% scorers do:** They often memorize the formula for lattice energy and try to apply it to every problem without understanding its underlying assumptions and limitations. They may also assume that lattice energy is the only factor that determines the stability of an ionic solid, ignoring other important factors such as **hydration energy** and **electron gain enthalpy**.

 - **What 95% scorers do:** They understand that lattice energy is just one of the factors that determine the stability of an ionic solid, and that it must be considered in conjunction with other factors such as hydration energy and electron gain enthalpy. They can also derive the formula for lattice energy from first principles, using the equation: $$U = \frac{A}{4\pi \epsilon_0} \	imes \frac{z^+z^-}{r^+ + r^-} (1 - \frac{1}{n})$$ and can apply it to a wide range of problems, including the calculation of lattice energy for **complex ions** and **mixed salts**. Additionally, they can explain the relationship between lattice energy and other physical properties of ionic solids, such as **melting point** and **solubility**.

 - **Key differences:** 

 * Understanding of the underlying assumptions and limitations of the lattice energy formula

 * Ability to consider multiple factors that determine the stability of an ionic solid

 * Ability to derive the lattice energy formula from first principles and apply it to complex problems

 * Understanding of the relationship between lattice energy and other physical properties of ionic solids

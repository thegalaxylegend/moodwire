## <a id="-formula-bank"></a>⚡ Formula Bank

- **Circumference of a Circle:** C = 2 \times \pi \times r — Circumference (C) of a circle, \pi is a constant (pi), and r is the radius of the circle.
 - **Area of a Circle:** A = \pi \times r^2 — Area (A) of a circle, \pi is a constant (pi), and r is the radius of the circle.
 - **Area of a Sector:** A = \frac{\theta}{360} \times \pi \times r^2 — Area (A) of a sector, \theta is the central angle in degrees, \pi is a constant (pi), and r is the radius of the circle.
 - **Length of an Arc:** L = \frac{\theta}{360} \times 2 \times \pi \times r — Length (L) of an arc, \theta is the central angle in degrees, \pi is a constant (pi), and r is the radius of the circle.
 - **Equation of a Circle (Center Form):** (x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2 — Equation of a circle with center (h, k) and radius r.
 - **Equation of a Circle (General Form):** x^2 + y^2 + Ax + By + C = 0 — Equation of a circle in general form, where the center and radius can be found from the coefficients A, B, and C.
 - **Radius of a Circle (from Equation):** r = \sqrt{h^2 + k^2 - C} — Radius (r) of a circle given its equation in general form, and the center (h, k).
 - **Distance between the Center and a Point on the Circle:** d = \sqrt{(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2} — Distance (d) between the center (h, k) of a circle and a point (x, y) on the circle.
 - **Tangent-Chord Theorem:** \triangle OTS = \triangle ORS — Tangent-chord theorem, relating the segments OT, TS, OR, and RS, where O is the center, T is the point of tangency, R and S are points on the circle, and the line RS is a chord.
 - **Power of a Point Theorem:** PT^2 = PR \times PS — Power of a point theorem, where PT is the length of the tangent segment from point P to the circle, and PR and PS are the lengths of the secant segments from point P to the circle.
 - **Inscribed Angle Theorem:** \angle APB = \frac{1}{2} \times \angle AOB — Inscribed angle theorem, where \angle APB is the angle inscribed in the circle, and \angle AOB is the central angle that subtends the same arc.

A super common pattern in those tricky 'Circles' questions, especially the 3-4 markers, revolves around two tangents drawn from an external point $P$ to a circle with center $O$. Let the points where the tangents touch the circle be $A$ and $B$. Your textbook tells you that the radius is perpendicular to the tangent at the point of contact, right? ## <a id="-last-5-minutes-box"></a>🔁 Last 5 Minutes Box

- {\circ} The equation of a circle with center $(h, k)$ and radius $r$ is given by: (x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2
 - The equation of a circle with center $(0, 0)$ and radius $r$ is given by: x^2 + y^2 = r^2
 - The general equation of a circle is given by: x^2 + y^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0, where the center is $(-g, -f)$ and the radius is $\sqrt{g^2 + f^2 - c}$
 - The length of the tangent from an external point $(x_1, y_1)$ to a circle with center $(h, k)$ and radius $r$ is given by: \sqrt{(x_1 - h)^2 + (y_1 - k)^2 - r^2}
 - The power of a point $(x_1, y_1)$ with respect to a circle with center $(h, k)$ and radius $r$ is given by: (x_1 - h)^2 + (y_1 - k)^2 - r^2
 - Key facts:
 - The circle is a set of all points in a plane that are equidistant from a central point called the center.
 - The equation of a circle can be written in the form $x^2 + y^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0$, where $g$, $f$, and $c$ are constants.
 - The tangent to a circle is perpendicular to the radius drawn to the point of tangency.
 - Common mistakes:
 - Not checking if the radius is negative, which would result in an imaginary circle.
 - Forgetting to complete the square when converting the general equation of a circle to the standard form.

## <a id="-practice-mcqs"></a>📝 Practice MCQs

**1. The chord of a circle is equal in length to its radius. What can be concluded about this circle?**

A) The circle is a semicircle.
B) The circle is a square.
C) The circle is a segment of a circle.
D) The circle is a circle.

**Answer:** A) In a circle, if a chord is equal in length to its radius, it can be concluded that the circle is a semicircle because it divides the circle into two equal parts.

---

**2. A circle is inscribed in a triangle. Which of the following is true about this circle?**

A) The circle is the circumcircle of the triangle.
B) The circle is the incenter of the triangle.
C) The circle is the incircle of the triangle.
D) The circle is the centroid of the triangle.

**Answer:** C) The circle inscribed in a triangle is called the incircle of the triangle, and its center is the incenter of the triangle.

---

**3. A line intersects a circle at two points. What is the name of this line?**

A) Secant line
B) Tangent line
C) Chord
D) Radius

**Answer:** A) A line that intersects a circle at two points is called a secant line.

---

**4. The diameter of a circle is equal to the length of a chord. What can be concluded about this circle?**

A) The circle is a semicircle.
B) The circle is a square.
C) The circle is a segment of a circle.
D) The circle is a circle.

**Answer:** A) In a circle, if the diameter is equal to the length of a chord, it can be concluded that the circle is a semicircle because the diameter divides the circle into two equal parts.

---

**5. A circle has two distinct centers of curvature. What can be concluded about this circle?**

A) The circle is a parabola.
B) The circle is a hyperbola.
C) The circle is not possible.
D) The circle is a circle.

**Answer:** C) A circle by definition has only one center of curvature, and if it has two distinct centers of curvature, it is not possible.

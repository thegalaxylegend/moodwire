## <a id="-formula-bank"></a>⚡ Formula Bank

### <a id="-formula-bank"></a>⚡ Formula Bank
#### **Kinematics Formulas**

- **Distance:** $s = \text{length of path}$ — distance traveled by an object

- **Displacement:** $\vec{s} = \vec{s_f} - \vec{s_i}$ — shortest distance between initial and final positions

- **Speed:** $v = \frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}} = \frac{s}{t}$ — rate of change of distance

- **Velocity:** $\vec{v} = \frac{\vec{\text{displacement}}}{\text{time}} = \frac{\vec{s}}{t}$ — rate of change of displacement

- **Acceleration:** $\vec{a} = \frac{\vec{\text{change in velocity}}}{\text{time}} = \frac{\vec{v_f} - \vec{v_i}}{t}$ — rate of change of velocity

- **Equation of Motion:** $v = u + at$ — relates initial velocity, final velocity, acceleration, and time

- **Equation of Motion:** $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$ — relates distance, initial velocity, acceleration, and time

- **Equation of Motion:** $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$ — relates initial velocity, final velocity, acceleration, and distance
Examiner's Trap: Be careful with the signs of velocity and acceleration, as they can be positive or negative depending on the direction.

#### **Dynamics Formulas**

- **Force:** $\vec{F} = m\vec{a}$ — relates force, mass, and acceleration

- **Newton's First Law:** $\vec{F_{net}} = 0$ — an object at rest remains at rest, and an object in motion remains in motion

- **Newton's Second Law:** $\vec{F_{net}} = m\vec{a}$ — the force applied to an object is equal to its mass \times its acceleration

- **Newton's Third Law:** $\vec{F_{action}} = -\vec{F_{reaction}}$ — every action has an equal and opposite reaction

- **Friction:** $f = \mu N$ — relates frictional force, coefficient of friction, and normal force

- **Normal Force:** $N = mg\cos\theta$ — relates normal force, mass, gravity, and angle of inclination
Examiner's Trap: Don't forget to consider the normal force and friction when solving problems involving inclined planes.

#### **Work and Energy Formulas**

- **Work:** $W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{s} = Fs\cos\theta$ — relates work, force, distance, and angle of application

- **Kinetic Energy:** $K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$ — relates kinetic energy, mass, and velocity

- **Potential Energy:** $U = mgh$ — relates potential energy, mass, gravity, and height

- **Total Energy:** $E = K + U$ — relates total energy, kinetic energy, and potential energy

- **Power:** $P = \frac{W}{t}$ — relates power, work, and time
Examiner's Trap: Be careful with the signs of work and energy, as they can be positive or negative depending on the direction of the force and displacement.

#### **Momentum Formulas**

- **Momentum:** $\vec{p} = m\vec{v}$ — relates momentum, mass, and velocity

- **Conservation of Momentum:** $\vec{p_i} = \vec{p_f}$ — the total momentum of a closed system remains constant

- **Impulse:** $J = \vec{F}\delta t = \delta p$ — relates impulse, force, time, and change in momentum
Examiner's Trap: Don't forget to consider the conservation of momentum when solving problems involving collisions.

#### **Rotational Motion Formulas**

- **Angular Displacement:** $\theta = \frac{s}{r}$ — relates angular displacement, arc length, and radius

- **Angular Velocity:** $\omega = \frac{\theta}{t}$ — relates angular velocity, angular displacement, and time

- **Angular Acceleration:** $\alpha = \frac{\omega}{t}$ — relates angular acceleration, angular velocity, and time

- **Torque:** $\tau = r \times F$ — relates torque, radius, and force

- **Rotational Kinetic Energy:** $K = \frac{1}{2}I\omega^2$ — relates rotational kinetic energy, moment of inertia, and angular velocity
Examiner's Trap: Be careful with the units of angular displacement, velocity, and acceleration, as they can be in radians or degrees.

## <a id="-the-5-mistakes-that-cost-marks"></a>🪤 The 5 Mistakes That Cost Marks

### <a id="the-5-mistakes-that-cost-marks"></a>The 5 Mistakes That Cost Marks

- **Mistake 1 — Incorrect Equation of Motion:**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** $v = u + t$
 

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** $v = u + at$
 

- 💸 **Marks lost:** 2 marks
 

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** Remember that acceleration $a$ is essential in the equation $v = u + at$, where $v$ is the final velocity, $u$ is the initial velocity, and $t$ is the time.

- **Mistake 2 — Wrong Unit Conversion:**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** $1 \text{ kg} = 1000 \text{ g}$ without proper conversion in formulas
 

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** Using $1 \text{ kg} = 1000 \text{ g}$ and ensuring unit consistency in calculations, e.g.

- $F = \frac{m \cdot \delta v}{\delta t}$ with $m$ in $\text{kg}$ and $\delta v$ in $\text{m/s}$
 

- 💸 **Marks lost:** 1 mark
 

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** Always ensure unit consistency, especially when converting between $\text{kg}$ and $\text{g}$, and between $\text{m/s}$ and $\text{cm/s}$.

- **Mistake 3 — Misapplication of Friction:**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** Assuming $\mu = \frac{F}{N}$ without considering the context of static or kinetic friction
 

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** Applying the correct formula for friction, $\mu = \frac{F}{N}$, and distinguishing between static friction $\mu_s$ and kinetic friction $\mu_k$
 

- 💸 **Marks lost:** 3 marks
 

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** Recall that $\mu_s > \mu_k$ and understand the conditions under which each applies.

- **Mistake 4 — Error in Work and Energy:**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** $W = Fs$ without considering the angle $\theta$ between $F$ and $s$
 

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** $W = Fs\cos(\theta)$, where $\theta$ is the angle between the force $F$ and displacement $s$
 

- 💸 **Marks lost:** 2 marks
 

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** Remember that work done $W$ is a scalar quantity and depends on the angle between force and displacement.

- **Mistake 5 — Incorrect Application of Momentum:**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** $p = mv$ without considering the system's momentum conservation
 

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** Applying the law of conservation of momentum, $p_{\text{initial}} = p_{\text{final}}$, in collisions and considering the momentum of the system as a whole
 

- 💸 **Marks lost:** 3 marks
 

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** Recall that momentum is conserved in a closed system, and the total momentum before a collision equals the total momentum after the collision.

- **Q1 (2020 JEE Main):** A particle is moving in a circular path of radius $r$ with a constant speed $v$. The time period of the particle is $T$. The acceleration of the particle is $a = \frac{v^2}{r}$. If the speed of the particle is doubled, the new time period is 
 - 🪤 **Trap:** Most students forget to use the relation $v = \frac{2\pi r}{T}$ to find the new time period.
 - 🧮 **Solution (Step-by-step):**
 Step 1: First, we need to find the relation between the time period $T$ and the speed $v$ using the formula $v = \frac{2\pi r}{T}$ → $T = \frac{2\pi r}{v}$.
 Step 2: When the speed is doubled, the new speed is $2v$, so the new time period $T'$ is given by $T' = \frac{2\pi r}{2v} = \frac{T}{2}$.
 **Final Answer:** T' = $\frac{T}{2}$
 - ⚡ **Speed trick:** Use the formula $v = \frac{2\pi r}{T}$ to directly find the new time period $T'$.

## <a id="-the-one-thing-most-students-get-wrong"></a>🧠 The One Thing Most Students Get Wrong

### <a id="the-one-thing-most-students-get-wrong"></a>The One Thing Most Students Get Wrong

- **The misconception (what 85% believe):** Most students believe that the normal force $N$ exerted by a surface on an object is always equal to the weight $mg$ of the object, where $m$ is the mass of the object and $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity.

- **The reality (what 99% know):** The reality is that the normal force $N$ is equal to the component of the weight that is perpendicular to the surface, given by $N = mg \cos{\theta}$, where $\theta$ is the angle between the surface and the horizontal. This is evident from the equation $N = mg \cos{\theta}$, which shows that the normal force depends on the angle of the surface.

- **The diagnostic question:**
 What is the normal force exerted by a surface on a $5 \, \text{kg}$ object placed on it, if the surface is inclined at an angle of $30^\circ$ to the horizontal?

- If you answered $49 \, \text{N}$: you have the misconception → fix: remember that $N = mg \cos{\theta}$, so $N = 5 \times 9.8 \times \cos{30^\circ} = 5 \times 9.8 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 42.43 \, \text{N}$.

- If you answered $42.43 \, \text{N}$: you are in the top 5% → now extend this: consider a block of mass $m$ on a surface inclined at an angle $\theta$ to the horizontal, and find the force of static friction $f$ required to prevent the block from sliding, given by $f = mg \sin{\theta}$, and note that at the point of slipping, $f = \mu N$, where $\mu$ is the coefficient of static friction.

### <a id="how-to-never-forget-this"></a>How to Never Forget This

- To remember that $N = mg \cos{\theta}$, use the mnemonic "Normal force is weight \times cosine of \theta", and visualize a block on an inclined surface, with the normal force $N$ acting perpendicular to the surface, and the weight $mg$ acting vertically downwards, and the angle $\theta$ between the surface and the horizontal.

- Alternatively, consider a block on a surface, and draw a free body diagram, with the weight $mg$ acting downwards, and the normal force $N$ acting perpendicular to the surface, and resolve the weight into its components parallel and perpendicular to the surface, to see that $N = mg \cos{\theta}$.

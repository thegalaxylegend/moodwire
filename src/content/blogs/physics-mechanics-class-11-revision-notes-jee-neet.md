 (Ready).### **Kinematics Formulas**

- **First Equation of Motion:** v = u + at — v: final velocity, u: initial velocity, a: constant acceleration, t: time

- **Second Equation of Motion:** s = ut + ½at² — s: displacement, u: initial velocity, a: acceleration, t: time

- **Third Equation of Motion:** v² = u² + 2as — v: final velocity, u: initial velocity, a: acceleration, s: displacement

- **Displacement in nth second:** s_n = u + a/2(2n - 1) — s_n: distance in specific second n, u: initial velocity, a: acceleration

- **Average Velocity (Constant a):** v_avg = (u + v) / 2 — u: initial velocity, v: final velocity

- **Time of Flight (Projectile):** T = (2u sinθ) / g — u: projection velocity, θ: launch angle, g: gravity

- **Maximum Height (Projectile):** H = (u² sin²θ) / 2g — u: projection velocity, θ: launch angle, g: gravity

- **Horizontal Range (Projectile):** R = (u² sin 2θ) / g — u: projection velocity, θ: launch angle, g: gravity

- **Equation of Trajectory:** y = x tanθ - (gx²) / (2u² cos²θ) — x,y: coordinates, θ: launch angle, u: velocity

- **Relative Velocity (1D):** v_ab = v_a - v_b — v_ab: velocity of a relative to b

- **Relative Velocity (2D):** v_ab = √(v_a² + v_b² - 2v_a v_b cosθ) — θ: angle between velocity vectors

**Examiner's Trap:** Students often forget to use a negative sign for 'g' when the object is moving upwards; always define your coordinate system (Up = +ve, Down = -ve) before applying equations.

### <a id="laws-of-motion-and-friction-formulas"></a>**Laws of Motion & Friction Formulas**

- **Newton's Second Law:** F = ma = dp/dt — F: net force, m: mass, a: acceleration, p: momentum

- **Impulse-Momentum Theorem:** J = Δp = ∫ F dt = m(v - u) — J: impulse, Δp: change in momentum

- **Static Friction Limit:** f_s ≤ μ_s N — f_s: static friction, μ_s: coefficient of static friction, N: normal force

- **Kinetic Friction:** f_k = μ_k N — f_k: kinetic friction, μ_k: coefficient of kinetic friction

- **Max Velocity on Flat Curve:** v_max = √(μ Rg) — μ: friction coefficient, R: radius of curve, g: gravity

- **Ideal Banking Angle:** tanθ = v² / Rg — θ: banking angle, v: velocity, R: radius, g: gravity

- **Optimum Velocity (Banked Road):** v_opt = √(Rg tanθ) — v_opt: velocity where no friction is needed

- **Max Velocity (Banked Road with Friction):** v_max = √[Rg(μ + tanθ) / (1 - μ tanθ)] — μ: friction coefficient

- **Centripetal Force:** F_c = mv²/R = mω²R — m: mass, v: linear velocity, ω: angular velocity, R: radius

- **Apparent Weight in Lift (Accelerating up):** W_app = m(g + a) — m: mass, g: gravity, a: lift acceleration

- **Apparent Weight in Lift (Accelerating down):** W_app = m(g - a) — m: mass, g: gravity, a: lift acceleration

**Examiner's Trap:** Friction always opposes relative motion, not the motion itself. In pulley-block systems, ensure the acceleration 'a' is consistent for all connected masses.

### <a id="work-energy-and-power-formulas"></a>**Work, Energy & Power Formulas**

- **Work Done (Constant Force):** W = Fs cosθ — F: force, s: displacement, θ: angle between F and s

- **Work Done (Variable Force):** W = ∫ F dx — F: force as a function of position x

- **Kinetic Energy:** KE = ½mv² = p²/2m — m: mass, v: velocity, p: momentum

- **Work-Energy Theorem:** W_net = ΔKE = KE_final - KE_initial — W_net: sum of work by all forces

- **Gravitational Potential Energy:** U = mgh — m: mass, g: gravity, h: height

- **Elastic Potential Energy (Spring):** U_s = ½kx² — k: spring constant, x: compression/extension

- **Power (Average):** P = W / t — W: work, t: time

- **Power (Instantaneous):** P = F · v = Fv cosθ — F: force, v: velocity

- **Conservative Force Relation:** F = -dU/dx — F: force, U: potential energy

- **Spring Force (Hooke's Law):** F = -kx — k: spring constant, x: displacement from equilibrium

**Examiner's Trap:** Work done by friction is often negative, but work done by static friction can be positive, negative, or zero depending on the point of application.

### <a id="center-of-mass-and-collision-formulas"></a>**Center of Mass & Collision Formulas**

- **Center of Mass (Discrete):** X_cm = (Σ m_i x_i) / Σ m_i — m_i: mass of i-th particle, x_i: position

- **Center of Mass (Continuous):** X_cm = (1/M) ∫ x dm — M: total mass, dm: mass element

- **Velocity of Center of Mass:** v_cm = (Σ m_i v_i) / Σ m_i — v_i: velocity of i-th particle

- **Elastic Collision (1D - Final Velocities):** 
 v₁ = [(m₁ - m₂) / (m₁ + m₂)]u₁ + [2m₂ / (m₁ + m₂)]u₂
 v₂ = [2m₁ / (m₁ + m₂)]u₁ + [(m₂ - m₁) / (m₁ + m₂)]u₂

- **Coefficient of Restitution:** e = (v₂ - v₁) / (u₁ - u₂) = v_separation / u_approach — e: restitution coeff

- **Perfectly Inelastic Collision:** v_final = (m₁u₁ + m₂u₂) / (m₁ + m₂) — v_final: common velocity

- **Loss of KE in Inelastic Collision:** ΔKE = ½ [m₁m₂ / (m₁ + m₂)] (u₁ - u₂)² (1 - e²)

**Examiner's Trap:** In 2D collisions, always resolve velocities into X and Y components and apply momentum conservation independently for each axis.

### <a id="rotational-dynamics-formulas"></a>**Rotational Dynamics Formulas**

- **Torque:** τ = r × F = rF sinθ = Iα — r: position vector, F: force, I: moment of inertia, α: angular acceleration

- **Angular Momentum:** L = r × p = Iω — p: linear momentum, I: moment of inertia, ω: angular velocity

- **Moment of Inertia (General):** I = Σ m_i r_i² = ∫ r² dm — m: mass, r: perpendicular distance from axis

- **I (Ring):** I_center = MR²; I_diameter = ½MR² — M: mass, R: radius

- **I (Disc):** I_center = ½MR²; I_diameter = ¼MR² — M: mass, R: radius

- **I (Solid Sphere):** I_center = (2/5)MR² — M: mass, R: radius

- **I (Hollow Sphere):** I_center = (2/3)MR² — M: mass, R: radius

- **I (Rod):** I_center = (1/12)ML²; I_end = (1/3)ML² — M: mass, L: length

- **Parallel Axis Theorem:** I = I_cm + Md² — I_cm: MOI about center of mass, d: distance between axes

- **Perpendicular Axis Theorem:** I_z = I_x + I_y — Valid only for planar (2D) objects

- **Rotational Kinetic Energy:** KE_rot = ½Iω² — I: moment of inertia, ω: angular velocity

- **Total KE (Rolling):** KE_total = ½mv² + ½Iω² = ½mv²(1 + k²/R²) — k: radius of gyration

- **Radius of Gyration:** k = √(I/M) — I: moment of inertia, M: total mass

- **Relation between Linear and Angular:** v = ωR, a = αR — v: linear velocity, a: linear acceleration

**Examiner's Trap:** When an object rolls without slipping, the point of contact is instantaneously at rest (v = 0). Do not apply pure translation formulas to the point of contact.

### <a id="gravitation-formulas"></a>**Gravitation Formulas**

- **Newton's Law of Gravitation:** F = G m₁m₂ / r² — G: gravitational constant, r: distance between centers

- **Acceleration due to Gravity (Surface):** g = GM / R² — M: mass of planet, R: radius of planet

- **g (at Height h):** g_h = g(1 - 2h/R) [for h ≪ R] — g: surface gravity, h: height

- **g (at Depth d):** g_d = g(1 - d/R) — d: depth from surface

- **Gravitational Potential (V):** V = -GM / r — M: mass, r: distance from center

- **Gravitational Potential Energy (U):** U = -GMm / r — m: small mass, M: large mass

- **Escape Velocity:** v_esc = √(2GM/R) = √(2gR) — R: radius of planet

- **Orbital Velocity:** v_orb = √(GM/r) — r: orbital radius from center

- **Time Period of Satellite:** T = 2π√(r³/GM) — r: orbital radius

- **Kepler's Third Law:** T² ∝ r³ — T: orbital period, r: semi-major axis

- **Energy of Satellite:** E_total = -GMm / 2r — r: orbital radius

**Examiner's Trap:** Gravitational potential (V) and Potential Energy (U) are always negative. A negative energy indicates a bound system.

### <a id="fluid-mechanics-formulas"></a>**Fluid Mechanics Formulas**

- **Pressure in Fluid:** P = P_atm + ρgh — ρ: density, g: gravity, h: depth

- **Equation of Continuity:** A₁v₁ = A₂v₂ — A: cross-sectional area, v: fluid velocity

- **Bernoulli's Equation:** P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant — P: pressure, ρ: density, v: velocity, h: height

- **Stokes' Law:** F_viscous = 6πηrv — η: viscosity coefficient, r: radius of sphere, v: velocity

- **Terminal Velocity:** v_t = [2r²(ρ - σ)g] / 9η — ρ: density of sphere, σ: density of fluid

- **Surface Tension (Force):** T = F / L — F: force, L: length of contact

- **Excess Pressure (Soap Bubble):** ΔP = 4T / R — T: surface tension, R: radius

- **Excess Pressure (Liquid Drop):** ΔP = 2T / R — T: surface tension, R: radius

- **Capillary Rise:** h = (2T cosθ) / (rρg) — θ: contact angle, r: tube radius, ρ: density

**Examiner's Trap:** Bernoulli's equation is only applicable for incompressible, non-viscous, and laminar flow.

## <a id="-the-5-mistakes-that-cost-marks"></a>🪤 The 5 Mistakes That Cost Marks

 * Mistake 1: Sign convention in Work-Energy Theorem or Kinematics (Direction matters).
 * Mistake 2: Friction (Assuming $f = \mu N$ always, ignoring static vs kinetic).
 * Mistake 3: Centripetal force confusion (Thinking it's a "new" force instead of a requirement).

 *Refining for "Mechanics" specifically:*
 1. *The Friction Trap:* Using $f = \mu N$ for static friction.
 2. *The Work-Energy Sign Error:* Forgetting $W = \Delta K$ requires signs for work done by gravity vs work done by friction.

### <a id="mistake-1-the-static-friction-overestimation-trap"></a>Mistake 1 — The Static Friction Overestimation Trap

- 🔴 **What students write:** $f_s = \mu_s N$

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** $0 \le f_s \le \mu_s N$

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** Never assume friction is at its maximum. Friction is a "self-adjusting" force. It only equals $\mu_s N$ at the exact moment of impending motion. ### <a id="mistake-2-the-workenergy-signage-blunder"></a>Mistake 2 — The Work-Energy Signage Blunder

- 🔴 **What students write:** $W_{total} = \Delta K$ (where $W$ is calculated as a scalar sum of magnitudes)

### <a id="mistake-4-the-rolling-without-slipping-rws-kinematic-error"></a>Mistake 4 — The Rolling Without Slipping (RWS) Kinematic Error

### <a id="mistake-5-the-angular-momentum-frame-mismatch"></a>Mistake 5 — The Angular Momentum Frame Mismatch

- 🔴 **What students write:** $L = I\omega$ (applied to any rotating system)

**The misconception (what 85% believe):**

- Most students believe that momentum conservation is a "fragile" law that only applies when no forces are acting on any part of the system.

- They see an explosion, a high-velocity collision, or a spring-loaded launch and immediately assume ΔP ≠ 0 because they can "see" massive forces being applied to the individual objects.

- They mistakenly equate the *magnitude* of internal forces with the *presence* of external influence. If the internal force is huge (like a chemical explosion), they assume the system's momentum must change.

**The reality (what 99% know):**

- Momentum conservation is not about the absence of force; it is strictly about the absence of **Net External Force** (Σ F_ext = 0).

- In any multi-body system, internal forces always occur in Newton's 3rd Law pairs: F_AB = -F_BA.

- **When you sum the forces for the entire system, these internal forces mathematically cancel out:** Σ F_total = Σ F_ext + Σ F_int. Since Σ F_int = 0, the equation reduces to Σ F_ext = dP_sys/dt.

- The "chaos" inside the system (explosions, collisions, tension, magnetism) can redistribute momentum among individual particles, changing their individual velocities (v₁, v₂, etc.) drastically, but they are mathematically incapable of changing the vector sum of the system's total momentum (P_total).

Even if an external force like friction (μ) exists, if the time interval (Δt) of the internal event is extremely small, the external impulse (∫ F_ext dt) is negligible. This means you can treat the system as "isolated" during the actual moment of the explosion or collision, even if the surface isn't perfectly smooth.

## <a id="-formula-bank"></a>⚡ Formula Bank

### <a id="kinematics-formulas"></a>**Kinematics Formulas**

- **Distance and Displacement:** $d = \frac{1}{2}gt^2$ — distance traveled under constant acceleration due to gravity

- **Velocity:** $v = u + at$ — final velocity after time $t$ with initial velocity $u$ and acceleration $a$

- **Acceleration:** $a = \frac{v - u}{t}$ — acceleration over time $t$ with initial velocity $u$ and final velocity $v$

- **Equation of Motion:** $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$ — distance traveled under constant acceleration

- **Relative Motion:** $v_{AB} = v_A - v_B$ — relative velocity between two objects $A$ and $B$
Examiner's Trap: Ensure correct application of signs for direction in kinematic equations.

### <a id="dynamics-formulas"></a>**Dynamics Formulas**

- **Newton's First Law:** $\sum F = 0$ — net force on an object at rest or moving with constant velocity

- **Newton's Second Law:** $F = ma$ — force applied to an object with mass $m$ and acceleration $a$

- **Newton's Third Law:** $F_{AB} = -F_{BA}$ — equal and opposite forces between two objects $A$ and $B$

- **Frictional Force:** $f = \mu N$ — frictional force with coefficient $\mu$ and normal force $N$

- **Centripetal Force:** $F_c = \frac{mv^2}{r}$ — force required for circular motion with velocity $v$, mass $m$, and radius $r$
Examiner's Trap: Distinguish between mass and weight in problems involving gravity.

### <a id="work-and-energy-formulas"></a>**Work and Energy Formulas**

- **Work Done:** $W = F \cdot d$ — work done by a force $F$ over distance $d$

- **Kinetic Energy:** $KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$ — energy of an object with mass $m$ and velocity $v$

- **Potential Energy:** $PE = mgh$ — energy of an object with mass $m$ at height $h$ under gravity $g$

- **Conservation of Energy:** $KE + PE = constant$ — total energy remains constant in a closed system

- **Power:** $P = \frac{W}{t}$ — rate of doing work over time $t$
Examiner's Trap: Correctly identify the system and its boundaries when applying conservation laws.

### <a id="momentum-and-collisions-formulas"></a>**Momentum and Collisions Formulas**

- **Momentum:** $p = mv$ — momentum of an object with mass $m$ and velocity $v$

- **Conservation of Momentum:** $\sum p_i = \sum p_f$ — total momentum remains constant in a closed system

- **Elastic Collision:** $v_{1f} = \frac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2}v_{1i} + \frac{2m_2}{m_1 + m_2}v_{2i}$ — final velocity after elastic collision

- **Inelastic Collision:** $v_{1f} = v_{2f} = \frac{m_1v_{1i} + m_2v_{2i}}{m_1 + m_2}$ — final velocity after inelastic collision
Examiner's Trap: Apply conservation of momentum correctly in collision problems.

### <a id="rotational-motion-formulas"></a>**Rotational Motion Formulas**

- **Angular Velocity:** $\omega = \frac{v}{r}$ — angular velocity with linear velocity $v$ and radius $r$

- **Angular Acceleration:** $\alpha = \frac{a}{r}$ — angular acceleration with linear acceleration $a$ and radius $r$

- **Torque:** $\tau = r \times F$ — torque due to force $F$ at distance $r$

- **Rotational Kinetic Energy:** $KE_{rot} = \frac{1}{2}I\omega^2$ — rotational kinetic energy with moment of inertia $I$ and angular velocity $\omega$
Examiner's Trap: Convert between linear and rotational quantities correctly.

### <a id="decision-table-for-choosing-formulas"></a>**Decision Table for Choosing Formulas**
| Situation | Formula to Use |
| --- | --- |
| Object under constant acceleration | $d = \frac{1}{2}gt^2$, $v = u + at$ |
| Force and acceleration | $F = ma$ |
| Energy problems | $KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$, $PE = mgh$, $W = F \cdot d$ |
| Momentum and collisions | $p = mv$, $\sum p_i = \sum p_f$ |

## <a id="-the-5-mistakes-that-cost-marks"></a>🪤 The 5 Mistakes That Cost Marks

### <a id="the-5-mistakes-that-cost-marks"></a>The 5 Mistakes That Cost Marks

- **Mistake 1 — Incorrect Friction Formula:**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** $F = \mu N$
 

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** $F = \mu N \cos{\theta}$ for inclined planes, where $\theta$ is the angle of inclination
 

- 💸 **Marks lost:** 2 marks
 

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** Always consider the angle of inclination when dealing with friction on inclined planes, and use $F = \mu N \cos{\theta}$ instead of just $F = \mu N$

- **Mistake 2 — Wrong Sign Convention:**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** $\Delta U = mgh$ for an object moving downwards
 

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** $\Delta U = -mgh$ for an object moving downwards, since the potential energy decreases
 

- 💸 **Marks lost:** 1 mark
 

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** Remember that the change in potential energy $\Delta U$ is negative when the object moves downwards, so use $\Delta U = -mgh$

- **Mistake 3 — Incorrect Application of Newton's Laws:**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** $\sum F = \frac{dp}{dt}$ for a system with constant mass
 

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** $\sum F = \frac{d(mv)}{dt} = m\frac{dv}{dt} + v\frac{dm}{dt}$ for a system with variable mass
 

- 💸 **Marks lost:** 3 marks
 

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** Use the correct form of Newton's second law, $\sum F = \frac{d(mv)}{dt}$, and consider both the change in velocity and mass

- **Mistake 4 — Confusion Between Velocity and Speed:**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** $v = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}$ for an object moving in a circular path
 

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** $v = \frac{\Delta s}{\Delta t}$, where $\Delta s$ is the arc length, and $v$ is the tangential velocity
 

- 💸 **Marks lost:** 2 marks
 

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** Distinguish between velocity $v$ and speed, and use the correct formula $v = \frac{\Delta s}{\Delta t}$ for an object moving in a circular path

- **Mistake 5 — Incorrect Calculation of Center of Mass:**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** $x_{cm} = \frac{m_1x_1 + m_2x_2}{m_1 + m_2 + m_3}$
 

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** $x_{cm} = \frac{m_1x_1 + m_2x_2 + m_3x_3}{m_1 + m_2 + m_3}$
 

- 💸 **Marks lost:** 2 marks
 

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** Include all masses and their respective positions when calculating the center of mass, using the formula $x_{cm} = \frac{\sum m_ix_i}{\sum m_i}$

### <a id="3-solved-pyqs"></a>3 Solved PYQs

- **Q1 (2020 JEE Main):** A block of mass $m$ is placed on a horizontal surface. The coefficient of kinetic friction between the block and the surface is $\mu$. If a force $F$ is applied on the block, the acceleration of the block is given by $a = \frac{F}{m} - \mu g$. Find the acceleration of the block when $F = 10 \, \text{N}$, $m = 2 \, \text{kg}$, and $\mu = 0.5$.
 - 🪤 **Trap:** Most students forget to consider the force of kinetic friction acting opposite to the direction of motion.
 - 🧮 **Solution (Step-by-step):**
 Step 1: Write down the equation for acceleration → $a = \frac{F}{m} - \mu g$
 Step 2: Plug in the given values → $a = \frac{10 \, \text{N}}{2 \, \text{kg}} - 0.5 \times 9.8 \, \text{m/s}^2$
 Step 3: Perform the calculations → $a = 5 \, \text{m/s}^2 - 4.9 \, \text{m/s}^2$
 **Final Answer:** a = 0.1 \, \text{m/s}^2
 - ⚡ **Speed trick:** Use the equation $a = \frac{F}{m} - \mu g$ directly and simplify the calculation by converting all units to SI units.
---

- **Q2 (2019 NEET):** A particle moves along a straight line with its velocity given by $v = 2t^2 + 3t + 1$, where $v$ is in $\text{m/s}$ and $t$ is in $\text{s}$. Find the acceleration of the particle at $t = 2 \, \text{s}$.
 - 🪤 **Trap:** Most students forget to find the derivative of velocity to get acceleration.
 - 🧮 **Solution (Step-by-step):**
 Step 1: Write down the equation for velocity → $v = 2t^2 + 3t + 1$
 Step 2: Find the derivative of velocity with respect to time to get acceleration → $a = \frac{dv}{dt} = 4t + 3$
 Step 3: Plug in the given time → $a = 4 \times 2 + 3$
 **Final Answer:** a = 11 \, \text{m/s}^2
 - ⚡ **Speed trick:** Use the power rule of differentiation to find the derivative of $v$ and get $a$ directly.
---

- **Q3 (2018 CBSE):** A body of mass $m$ is thrown vertically upwards with an initial velocity $u$. Find the maximum height reached by the body, given that the acceleration due to gravity is $g$.
 - 🪤 **Trap:** Most students forget to use the equation $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$ to find the maximum height.
 - 🧮 **Solution (Step-by-step):**
 Step 1: Write down the equation $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$ and set $v = 0$ at the maximum height → $0 = u^2 - 2gh$
 Step 2: Solve for $h$ → $h = \frac{u^2}{2g}$
 **Final Answer:** h = \frac{u^2}{2g}
 - ⚡ **Speed trick:** Use the equation $h = \frac{u^2}{2g}$ directly to find the maximum height reached by the body.

## <a id="-the-one-thing-most-students-get-wrong"></a>🧠 The One Thing Most Students Get Wrong

### <a id="the-one-thing-most-students-get-wrong"></a>The One Thing Most Students Get Wrong

- The misconception (what 85% believe): Most students believe that the normal force $N$ exerted by a surface on an object is always equal to the weight $mg$ of the object, where $m$ is the mass and $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity.

- The reality (what 99% know): The reality is that the normal force $N$ is equal to the component of the weight $mg$ that is perpendicular to the surface, given by $N = mg \cos{\theta}$, where $\theta$ is the angle between the surface and the horizontal.

- Key points to understand the concept:
 

- The normal force $N$ is a contact force that prevents the object from penetrating the surface.

- The weight $mg$ is a force that acts vertically downward, and its component perpendicular to the surface is $mg \cos{\theta}$.

- When the surface is horizontal, $\theta = 0$, and $N = mg \cos{0} = mg$.

- When the surface is inclined, $\theta 
eq $0, and $N = mg \cos{\theta} < mg$.

### <a id="-core-formulas"></a>⚡ Core Formulas

- $v = u + at$ — gives you the final velocity of an object

- $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$ — gives you the displacement of an object

- $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$ — gives you the final velocity of an object in terms of displacement

- $F = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}$ — gives you the force exerted on an object

### <a id="-mustknow-facts"></a>🧠 Must-Know Facts

- The normal force is always perpendicular to the surface

- The frictional force is always opposite to the direction of motion

- The gravitational force is always attractive and acts along the line joining the centers of the two objects

### <a id="-never-forget"></a>🚫 Never Forget

- ❌ Assuming that the acceleration of an object is always in the direction of motion → ✅ Remembering that acceleration is in the direction of the net force acting on the object

- ❌ Forgetting to consider the signs of the forces and velocities → ✅ Always considering the signs and directions of the forces and velocities

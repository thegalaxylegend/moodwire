## <a id="-formula-bank"></a>⚡ Formula Bank

- **Mendeleev's Periodic Law:** $\text{Properties of elements are periodic function of their atomic weights}$ — relates elements' properties to their atomic weights

- **Period:** $\text{Horizontal row of elements in the periodic table}$ — elements with same valence shell

- **Group:** $\text{Vertical column of elements in the periodic table}$ — elements with same number of valence electrons
Examiner's Trap: Be aware of the difference between period and group in the periodic table.

### <a id="atomic-number-and-mass-formulas"></a>**Atomic Number and Mass Formulas**

- **Atomic Number (Z):** $Z = \text{Number of protons in the nucleus}$ — unique identifier for an element

- **Mass Number (A):** $A = \text{Number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus}$ — total number of nucleons in an atom

- **Number of Neutrons (N):** $N = A - Z$ — difference between mass number and atomic number
Examiner's Trap: Remember that atomic number determines the element, not mass number.

### <a id="electron-configuration-formulas"></a>**Electron Configuration Formulas**

- **Electron Configuration:** $\text{Arrangement of electrons in an atom}$ — shows distribution of electrons in shells and subshells

- **Aufbau Principle:** $\text{Electrons occupy the lowest available energy levels}$ — guides the filling of electrons in an atom

- **Hund's Rule:** $\text{Electrons occupy empty orbitals before pairing up}$ — explains the filling of electrons in degenerate orbitals
Examiner's Trap: Understand the Aufbau principle and Hund's rule for electron configuration.

### <a id="block-formulas"></a>**Block Formulas**

- **s-Block Elements:** $\text{Elements in groups 1 and 2, and hydrogen}$ — characterized by s-orbital filling

- **p-Block Elements:** $\text{Elements in groups 13 to 18}$ — characterized by p-orbital filling

- **d-Block Elements:** $\text{Elements in groups 3 to 12}$ — characterized by d-orbital filling

- **f-Block Elements:** $\text{Elements in the bottom two rows of the periodic table}$ — characterized by f-orbital filling
Examiner's Trap: Identify the blocks of elements in the periodic table.

### <a id="metallic-and-nonmetallic-formulas"></a>**Metallic and Non-Metallic Formulas**

- **Metallic Character:** $\text{Ability of an element to lose electrons}$ — increases down a group and decreases across a period

- **Non-Metallic Character:** $\text{Ability of an element to gain electrons}$ — decreases down a group and increases across a period
Examiner's Trap: Recognize the trends in metallic and non-metallic character.

### <a id="periodic-trends-formulas"></a>**Periodic Trends Formulas**

- **Atomic Radius:** $\text{Distance between the nucleus and the outermost electron}$ — decreases across a period and increases down a group

- **Electronegativity:** $\text{Ability of an element to attract electrons}$ — increases across a period and decreases down a group

- **Electron Affinity:** $\text{Energy change when an electron is added to an atom}$ — becomes more negative across a period and less negative down a group
Examiner's Trap: Understand the periodic trends in atomic radius, electronegativity, and electron affinity.

## <a id="-the-5-mistakes-that-cost-marks"></a>🪤 The 5 Mistakes That Cost Marks

### <a id="-the-5-mistakes-that-cost-marks"></a>🪤 The 5 Mistakes That Cost Marks

- **Mistake 1 — Periodic Table Structure:**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** Elements are arranged in the periodic table based on their $molecularmass$.

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** Elements are arranged in the periodic table based on their $atomicnumber$ ($Z$) or $protonnumber$.

- 💸 **Marks lost:** 2 marks
 

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** Remember, atomic number ($Z$) is the key to the periodic table's structure, not molecular mass.

- **Mistake 2 — [Metals](/blog/metals-and-non-metals-class-10-notes), Non-[metals](/blog/metals-and-non-metals-class-10-notes), and Metalloids:**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** All elements are either [metals](/blog/metals-and-non-metals-class-10-notes) or non-[metals](/blog/metals-and-non-metals-class-10-notes).

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** Elements can be classified into metals, non-metals, and metalloids based on their properties.

- 💸 **Marks lost:** 1 mark
 

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** Recall that metalloids have properties intermediate between metals and non-metals.

- **Mistake 3 — Group and Period Trends:**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** Atomic size increases down a group and across a period.

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** Atomic size increases down a group but decreases across a period.

- 💸 **Marks lost:** 3 marks
 

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** Use the formula for atomic radius: $r \propto \frac{1}{Z_{eff}}$, where $Z_{eff}$ is the effective nuclear charge.

- **Mistake 4 — Electron Configuration:**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** The electron configuration of an element can be determined using the $n + l$ rule.

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** The electron configuration of an element can be determined using the Aufbau principle and the Pauli exclusion principle.

- 💸 **Marks lost:** 2 marks
 

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** Apply the Aufbau principle: electrons occupy the lowest available energy levels.

- **Mistake 5 — Block Elements:**
 

- 🔴 **What students write:** $s$-block elements have partially filled $d$ subshells.

- ✅ **What examiners expect:** $s$-block elements have partially filled $s$ subshells, while $d$-block elements have partially filled $d$ subshells.

- 💸 **Marks lost:** 1 mark
 

- 🔧 **The fix (30-second trick):** Recall the block elements' electron configurations: $s$-block ($ns^1$ or $ns^2$), $p$-block ($ns^2 np^1$ to $ns^2 np^6$), $d$-block ($ns^1$ or $ns^2 (n-1)d^1$ to $ns^2 (n-1)d^{10}$), and $f$-block ($ns^2 (n-2)f^1$ to $ns^2 (n-2)f^{14}$).

### <a id="q1-2020-cbse-an-element-x-belongs-to-group-2-and-period-3-of-the-modern-periodic-table"></a>Q1 (2020 CBSE): An element 'X' belongs to Group 2 and Period 3 of the Modern Periodic Table.
(a) Write its electronic configuration.
(b) Is it a metal or non-metal?
(c) Write the formula of its chloride.
(d) What is the size of its atom compared to an element 'Y' in Group 13 and Period 3?

🪤 **Trap:** Forgetting that group number relates to valence electrons and period number relates to the number of shells, or mixing up trends for atomic size.
🧮 **Solution (Step-by-step):**

- **Step 1 (Identify Element X):** Period 3 means 3 shells. Group 2 means 2 valence electrons.
 - Electronic configuration: $2, 8, 2$.
 - Atomic number: $2+8+2 = 12$ (Magnesium, $Mg$).

- **Step 2 (Metal/Non-metal):** Elements with 1, 2, or 3 valence electrons are typically metals.
 - Element X has 2 valence electrons, so it is a metal.

- **Step 3 (Chloride Formula):** Valency of X (Mg) is 2. Valency of Chlorine (Cl) is 1.
 - Formula: $XCl_2$ (or $MgCl_2$).

- **Step 4 (Compare Atomic Size):** Element Y is in Group 13, Period 3.
 - Across a period, from left to right, atomic size decreases due to increasing nuclear charge pulling electrons closer.
 - Therefore, element X (Group 2) will have a larger atomic size than element Y (Group 13) in the same period.
**Final Answer:**

- (a) Electronic configuration: $2, 8, 2$

- (b) Metal

- (c) $XCl_2$

- (d) Element X has a larger atomic size than element Y.
⚡ **Speed trick:** Directly recall the trends: Group 2 implies metal and valency 2. Atomic size decreases across a period.

### <a id="q2-2019-cbse-how-does-the-metallic-character-of-elements-vary"></a>Q2 (2019 CBSE): How does the metallic character of elements vary:
(a) in a period from left to right?
(b) in a group from top to bottom?
Give reasons for your answer.

🪤 **Trap:** Confusing metallic character with non-metallic character trends, or providing incomplete reasons without linking to effective nuclear charge or atomic size.
🧮 **Solution (Step-by-step):**

- **Step 1 (Period Trend):** Across a period from left to right.
 - Metallic character decreases.
 - Reason: The effective nuclear charge increases, making it harder for atoms to lose electrons.

- **Step 2 (Group Trend):** Down a group from top to bottom.
 - Metallic character increases.
 - Reason: Atomic size increases, and the valence electrons are further from the nucleus, experiencing less effective nuclear charge, thus making it easier to lose electrons.
**Final Answer:**

- (a) In a period from left to right, metallic character decreases because effective nuclear charge increases, making electron loss difficult.

- (b) In a group from top to bottom, metallic character increases because atomic size increases, reducing the attraction of the nucleus on valence electrons, making electron loss easier.
⚡ **Speed trick:** Remember the core idea: metallic character is the tendency to lose electrons. Think about how atomic size and nuclear charge affect electron loss in each direction.

### <a id="q3-2022-cbse-three-elements-a-b-and-c-have-atomic-numbers-12-17-and-18-respectively"></a>Q3 (2026 CBSE): Three elements A, B and C have atomic numbers 12, 17 and 18 respectively.
(a) Which two elements belong to the same period?
(b) Which element is a noble gas?
(c) What is the valency of element B?
(d) Which element has the largest atomic size?

🪤 **Trap:** Incorrectly assigning periods or groups from atomic numbers, or misidentifying noble gases based on incomplete octets.
🧮 **Solution (Step-by-step):**

- **Step 1 (Electronic Configurations):**
 - Element A (Atomic No. 12): $2, 8, 2$ (Period 3, Group 2)
 - Element B (Atomic No. 17): $2, 8, 7$ (Period 3, Group 17)
 - Element C (Atomic No. 18): $2, 8, 8$ (Period 3, Group 18)

- **Step 2 (Same Period):** All three elements have 3 shells.
 - Elements A, B, and C belong to the same period (Period 3).

- **Step 3 (Noble Gas):** A noble gas has a completely filled outermost shell.
 - Element C ($2, 8, 8$) has a complete octet, so it is a noble gas.

- **Step 4 (Valency of B):** Element B has 7 valence electrons. It needs to gain 1 electron to complete its octet.
 - Valency of B is 1.

- **Step 5 (Largest Atomic Size):** In a period, atomic size decreases from left to right.
 - Element A (Group 2) is furthest to the left in Period 3 among A, B, and C.
 - Therefore, Element A has the largest atomic size.
**Final Answer:**

- (a) Elements A, B, and C belong to the same period.

- (b) Element C is a noble gas.

- (c) The valency of element B is 1.

- (d) Element A has the largest atomic size.
⚡ **Speed trick:** Quickly write electronic configurations to determine period/group. Remember that noble gases have 8 valence electrons (except He). Atomic size is largest on the left side of a period.

## <a id="-the-one-thing-most-students-get-wrong"></a>🧠 The One Thing Most Students Get Wrong

### <a id="the-one-thing-most-students-get-wrong"></a>The One Thing Most Students Get Wrong
The most common misconception among students is that the periodic table is just a table of elements arranged randomly.

- Most students believe that the periodic table is just a collection of elements with no underlying pattern or logic.

- They think that the elements are arranged based on their atomic masses, but this is not entirely correct.

### <a id="the-reality"></a>The Reality is that the periodic table is a beautifully organized table of elements, arranged based on their atomic numbers and recurring [chemical](/blog/chemical-reactions-and-equations-class-10-notes) properties.

- The elements are arranged in order of increasing atomic number (number of protons in the nucleus).

- The periodic table is divided into rows called periods and columns called groups or families.

- Elements in the same group have similar [chemical](/blog/chemical-reactions-and-equations-class-10-notes) properties due to the same number of electrons in their outermost shell.

### <a id="the-diagnostic-question"></a>The Diagnostic Question
What is the main basis for the classification of elements in the periodic table?

- A) Atomic mass

- B) Atomic number

- C) Chemical properties

- D) Physical properties

- If you answered A) Atomic mass: you have the misconception → fix: The periodic table is arranged based on atomic number, not atomic mass.

- If you answered B) Atomic number: you are in the top 5% → now extend this: The atomic number is a unique identifier for each element and determines its position in the periodic table.

### <a id="how-to-never-forget-this"></a>How to Never Forget This
To remember that the periodic table is arranged based on atomic number, you can use the mnemonic "Atomic Number Unlocks Periodic Table".

- You can also visualize the periodic table as a big grid, with each element having its own unique address based on its atomic number.

- Another way to remember this is to think of the atomic number as the "ID" of each element, and the periodic table as a big database where each element is stored based on its ID.

- The formula to keep in mind is: $Z = p^+$, where $Z$ is the atomic number and $p^+$ is the number of protons in the nucleus.

- The relationship between atomic number and atomic mass can be expressed as: $A = Z + N$, where $A$ is the atomic mass and $N$ is the number of neutrons. 

### <a id="key-formulas"></a>Key Formulas
Some key formulas to remember when dealing with the periodic table are:

- $Z = p^+$ (atomic number equals number of protons)

- $A = Z + N$ (atomic mass equals atomic number plus number of neutrons)

- $\frac{m}{n} = \frac{\text{mass of atom}}{\text{mass of neutron}}$ (ratio of mass of atom to mass of neutron)

### <a id="common-mistakes"></a>Common Mistakes
Some common mistakes that students make when dealing with the periodic table are:

- Confusing atomic number with atomic mass

- Not understanding the recurring chemical properties of elements in the same group

- Not being able to identify the period and group of an element

- The diagnostic question can help identify these misconceptions and correct them.

## <a id="-practice-mcqs"></a>📝 Practice MCQs

**1. An element with atomic number 20 has an electronic configuration of $[2, 8, 8, 2]$. Which period does it belong to?**
**A)**     3rd period
**B)**     4th period
**C)**     5th period
**D)**     6th period

**Answer:** B) The element is in the 4th period because the outermost energy level has 8 electrons. The correct formula to find the period is $n = l + 1$, where $l$ is the outermost energy level. Here, $l = 3$, so $n = 4$.

---

**2. Which of the following elements is a noble gas? $Ca$, $Na$, $Mg$, $Ar$**
**A)**     $Ca$
**B)**     $Na$
**C)**     $Mg$
**D)**     $Ar$

**Answer:** D) The noble gases are unreactive elements in the 18th group of the periodic table. $Ar$ is a noble gas because it has 8 electrons in its outermost energy level and is located in group 18.

---

**3. An element has an atomic number of 24. Find the total number of electrons it has when it loses 2 electrons.**
**A)**     22
**B)**     22
**C)**     22
**D)**     24

**Answer:** A) The number of electrons an element has when it loses 2 electrons can be found by subtracting 2 from its atomic number. So, $24 - 2 = 22$.

---

**4. What is the electronic configuration of iron (atomic number 26)?**
**A)**     $[2, 8, 14, 2]$
**B)**     $[2, 8, 8, 5, 1]$
**C)**     $[2, 8, 8, 6]$
**D)**     $[2, 8, 7]$

**Answer:** D) The electronic configuration of iron can be found by filling up the electrons in the energy levels. The correct configuration is $[2, 8, 7]$.

---

**5. Which of the following elements belongs to the s-block of the periodic table?**
**A)**     $Be$
**B)**     $Na$
**C)**     $K$
**D)**     $Cl$

**Answer:** B) The s-block elements are located in group 1 and 2 of the periodic table. $Na$ is an s-block element because it is in group 1 and has one electron in its outermost energy level.

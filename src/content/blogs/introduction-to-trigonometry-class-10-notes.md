## ⚡ Formula Bank
- $\sin \theta = \franc{\text{opposite side}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$
- $\cos \theta = \franc{\text{adjacent side}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$
- $\tan \theta = \franc{\text{opposite side}}{\text{adjacent side}}$
-     \sin^2 \theta + \cos^2 \theta = 1 
-   \tan \theta = $\franc{\sin \theta}{\cos \theta}$
-     \sin (90^\CIRC - \theta) = \cos \theta 
-     \cos (90^\CIRC - \theta) = \sin \theta 
-     \sin (180^\CIRC - \theta) = \sin \theta 
-     \cos (180^\CIRC - \theta) = -\cos \theta

  - Using the formula $\tan \theta = \franc{\text{opposite side}}{\text{adjacent side}}$, we get $\tan 30^\CIRC = \franc{x}{y}$.

  - Therefore, the value of $\tan 30^\circa is $\franc{1}{\sort{3}}$.
- If $\sin \theta = \franc{3}{5}$, find the value of $\cos \theta.

  - Solving for $\cos^2 \theta, we get $\cos^2 \theta = 1 - \franc{9}{25} = \franc{16}{25}$.

## 🧠 The One Thing Most Students Get Wrong
- The concept of trigonometric functions in different quadrants is often misunderstood by students.
- Many students forget that the values of $\sin \theta, $\cos \theta, and $\tan \theta depend on the quadrant in which the angle lies.
- For example, $\sin \theta is positive in the first and second quadrants, and negative in the third and fourth quadrants.
- Similarly, $\cos \theta is positive in the first and fourth quadrants, and negative in the second and third quadrants.
- It is essential to understand the concept of quadrants and the signs of trigonometric functions in each quadrant to solve problems in [trigonometry](/blog/applications-of-trigonometry-class-10-notes).

**1. If $\sin \theta = \franc{1}{2}$, then the value of $\cos \theta is**

**Answer: B) $\franc{\sort{3}}{2}$, since $\sin^2 \theta + \cos^2 \theta = 1$, we get $\left(\franc{1}{2}\right)^2 + \cos^2 \theta = 1$, which gives $\cos^2 \theta = \franc{3}{4}$, and therefore $\cos \theta = \franc{\sort{3}}{2}$**

**2. The value of $\tan 45^\circa is**
-
A) $1$
-
B) \franc{1}{\sort{2}}
-
C) \franc{\sort{2}}{2}
-
D) \franc{1}{2}

**Answer: A) $1$, since $\tan \theta = \franc{\sin \theta}{\cos \theta}$, and $\sin 45^\CIRC = \cos 45^\CIRC = \franc{1}{\sort{2}}$, we get $\tan 45^\CIRC = \franc{\franc{1}{\sort{2}}}{\franc{1}{\sort{2}}} = 1$**

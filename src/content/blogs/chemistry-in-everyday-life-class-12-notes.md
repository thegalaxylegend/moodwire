## <a id="-what-will-come-in-your-exam"></a>🎯 What WILL Come in Your Exam
* 1-2 marks question on classification of drugs: always asks for type of drug (e.g. analgesic, antacid, etc.) or classification of a specific drug
* Antimicrobial agents: exam always includes a question on mode of action of at least one antimicrobial agent (e.g. penicillin, tetracycline, etc.)
* Chemicals in food: 1 mark question on food additives (e.g. preservatives, artificial sweeteners, etc.) or a 2-3 marks question on food adulteration — always
* 2 marks question on antiseptic or disinfectant: always includes a question on the difference between antiseptic and disinfectant or asks for an example
* Drug-target interaction: 2-3 marks question on how a specific drug interacts with its target (e.g. enzyme, receptor, etc.) — high yield topic
* Soaps and detergents: always includes a question on the difference between soap and detergent or asks for an example of a soap or detergent
* 1 numerical on $C_{n}H_{2n+1}COOH$ (soaps) or $C_{n}H_{2n+1}SO_{4}Na$ (detergents) — always
* Medicinal chemistry: 2-3 marks question on the structure-activity relationship of a specific drug or a class of drugs (e.g. NSAIDs, antibiotics, etc.)
* 1 mark question on drug nomenclature: always asks for the IUPAC name of a specific drug
* Chemicals in medicine: 2 marks question on a specific application of a chemical in medicine (e.g. use of $N_{2}O$ as an anesthetic, etc.)
* $pH$ of a solution of a weak acid or base: 1-2 marks numerical — always
* Relationship between $pH$, $pOH$, and $pK_{w}$: $pH + pOH = 14$ or $pK_{w} = 14$ at $25^{\\circ}C$ — must know
* 1 mark question on buffer solution: always asks for the $pH$ of a buffer solution or asks to identify a buffer solution
* 2 marks question on the importance of $pH$ in everyday life (e.g. $pH$ of soil, $pH$ of blood, etc.)
* 1 numerical on $\\frac{K_{w}}{K_{a}}$ or $\\frac{K_{w}}{K_{b}}$ — always
* Medicinal application of coordination compounds: 2 marks question on the use of coordination compounds in medicine (e.g. $cis$-platin, etc.)


## <a id="-formula-bank"></a>⚡ Formula Bank

$pK_{a} = -\log_{10}[H^{+}]$ : Acid dissociation constant, 
 $pK_{b} = -\log_{10}[OH^{-}]$ : Base dissociation constant, 
 $pH = -\log_{10}[H^{+}]$ : Measure of acidity, 
 $pOH = -\log_{10}[OH^{-}]$ : Measure of basicity, 
 $K_{w} = [H^{+}][OH^{-}] = 10^{-14}$ : Water dissociation constant, 
 $K_{a} = \frac{[H^{+}][A^{-}]}{[HA]}$ : Acid dissociation constant expression, 
 $K_{b} = \frac{[BH^{+}][OH^{-}]}{[B]}$ : Base dissociation constant expression, 
 $K_{a} \times K_{b} = K_{w}$ : Relationship between acid and base dissociation constants, 
 $pK_{a} + pK_{b} = 14$ : Relationship between acid and base dissociation constants in terms of pK, 
 $\frac{K_{b}}{K_{a}} = \frac{[BH^{+}]}{[A^{-}]}$ : Comparison of base and acid dissociation constants, 
 $K_{a} = \frac{[H^{+}][A^{-}]}{[HA]} = \frac{[H^{+}]}{K_{b}}$ : Relationship between acid dissociation constant and hydrogen ion concentration, 
 $pH = \frac{1}{2}pK_{w} + \frac{1}{2}\log_{10}\frac{[salt]}{[acid]}$ : Henderson-Hasselbalch equation for pH calculation, 
 $pOH = \frac{1}{2}pK_{w} + \frac{1}{2}\log_{10}\frac{[salt]}{[base]}$ : Henderson-Hasselbalch equation for pOH calculation, 
 $K_{sp} = [A^{n+}][B^{m-}]^{n}$ : Solubility product constant for the dissolution of a salt, 
 $K_{f} = \frac{[A^{n+}][B^{m-}]}{[AB_{m}^{n}]}$ : Formation constant for the formation of a complex ion, 
 $K_{d} = \frac{1}{K_{f}}$ : Instability constant for the dissociation of a complex ion, 
 $\Delta G^{\circ} = -RT\ln K$ : Relationship between Gibbs free energy and equilibrium constant, 
 $\Delta G^{\circ} = -2.303RT\log K$ : Relationship between Gibbs free energy and equilibrium constant, 
 $E^{\circ}_{cell} = \frac{\Delta G^{\circ}}{-nF}$ : Relationship between cell potential and Gibbs free energy, 
 $E^{\circ}_{cell} = \frac{RT}{nF}\ln K$ : Relationship between cell potential and equilibrium constant, 
 $E^{\circ}_{cell} = \frac{0.0591}{n}\log K$ : Relationship between cell potential and equilibrium constant at 25°C, 
 $E_{cell} = E^{\circ}_{cell} - \frac{0.0591}{n}\log_{10}Q$ : Nernst equation for cell potential calculation, 
 $\log_{10}K = \frac{\Delta H^{\circ}}{2.303R}\left(\frac{1}{T_{2}} - \frac{1}{T_{1}}\right)$ : Van 't Hoff equation for temperature dependence of equilibrium constant,

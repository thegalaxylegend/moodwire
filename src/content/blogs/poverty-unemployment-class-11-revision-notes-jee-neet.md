## <a id="-the-5-mistakes-that-cost-marks"></a>🪤 The 5 Mistakes That Cost Marks

- **Mistake 1:** Incorrectly calculating the {$(Lorenz Curve)$} and {$(Gini Coefficient)$} while measuring income inequality.

- *Costs:* 4-6 marks
 

- *Fix:* Understand that the {$(Lorenz Curve)$} is a graphical representation of income distribution, and the {$(Gini Coefficient)$} is calculated as the ratio of the area between the {$(Lorenz Curve)$} and the line of perfect equality to the total area under the line of perfect equality: $G = \frac{A}{A + B}$, where $A$ is the area between the {$(Lorenz Curve)$} and the line of perfect equality, and $B$ is the area under the {$(Lorenz Curve)$}.

- **Mistake 2:** Failing to differentiate between {$(Types of Unemployment)$}, such as {$(Frictional Unemployment)$}, {$(Structural Unemployment)$}, and {$(Cyclical Unemployment)$}.

- *Costs:* 3-5 marks
 

- *Fix:* Remember that {$(Frictional Unemployment)$} occurs due to job search and transition, {$(Structural Unemployment)$} is caused by changes in the economy, and {$(Cyclical Unemployment)$} is a result of economic downturns: $U = \frac{Labor\ Force 

- Employment}{Labor\ Force}$.

- **Mistake 3:** Not understanding the {$(Poverty Line)$} concept and its measurement using {$(Calorie Intake)$} or {$(Income)$} methods.

- *Costs:* 4-6 marks
 

- *Fix:* Recognize that the {$(Poverty Line)$} is the minimum level of income required to meet basic needs, and it can be measured using either {$(Calorie Intake)$} or {$(Income)$} methods: $Poverty\ Line = \frac{Minimum\ Calorie\ Intake}{Price\ of\ Calories}$.

- **Mistake 4:** Incorrectly applying the {$(Human Development Index (HDI))$} formula, which includes {$(Life Expectancy)$}, {$(Education)$}, and {$(Income)$}.

- *Costs:* 5-7 marks
 

- *Fix:* Use the correct {$(HDI)$} formula: $HDI = \frac{1}{3} \left( \frac{LE}{85} + \frac{E}{100} + \frac{GNI}{100} \right)$, where $LE$ is {$(Life Expectancy)$}, $E$ is {$(Education)$}, and $GNI$ is {$(Gross National Income)$}.

- **Mistake 5:** Not distinguishing between {$(Absolute Poverty)$} and {$(Relative Poverty)$}, leading to incorrect calculations and analysis.

- *Costs:* 4-6 marks
 

- *Fix:* Understand that {$(Absolute Poverty)$} refers to a fixed poverty line, while {$(Relative Poverty)$} is a moving line based on average income: $Absolute\ Poverty = \frac{Number\ of\ People\ Below\ Poverty\ Line}{Total\ Population}$, $Relative\ Poverty = \frac{Number\ of\ People\ Below\ Relative\ Poverty\ Line}{Total\ Population}$.

## <a id="-3-solved-pyqs"></a>✏️ 3 Solved PYQs

- **Q1:** What is the difference between absolute poverty and relative poverty?
 - **Trap:** Students often get confused between the two terms and use them interchangeably.
 - **Solution:** 
 - Absolute poverty refers to a lack of basic necessities like food, shelter, and clothing. 
 - It is typically measured using a poverty line, which is the minimum amount of income required to meet basic needs.
 - Relative poverty, on the other hand, refers to a lack of resources compared to others in the same society.
 - It is measured by comparing an individual's or family's income to the median income of the population.
 - The formula to calculate the poverty line is: $Poverty Line = rac{Minimum Calorie Intake}{Price of Calories}$
 - For example, if the minimum calorie intake is 2400 calories and the price of calories is $0.05 per calorie, then the poverty line would be: $Poverty Line = rac{2400}{0.05} = $48,000$
 - **Answer:** Absolute poverty is a lack of basic necessities, while relative poverty is a lack of resources compared to others.
 
 - **Q2:** What are the main causes of unemployment in India?
 - **Trap:** Students often forget to include all the main causes of unemployment in their answers.
 - **Solution:** 
 - The main causes of unemployment in India are:
 - Lack of education and skills
 - Limited job opportunities
 - Population growth
 - Slow economic growth
 - Seasonal and casual employment
 - The formula to calculate the unemployment rate is: $Unemployment Rate = rac{Number of Unemployed}{Labor Force} 	imes 100$
 - For example, if the number of unemployed is 10 million and the labor force is 50 million, then the unemployment rate would be: $Unemployment Rate = rac{10}{50} 	imes 100 = 20%$
 - **Answer:** The main causes of unemployment in India are lack of education and skills, limited job opportunities, population growth, slow economic growth, and seasonal and casual employment.
 
 - **Q3:** What are the effects of poverty on the economy?
 - **Trap:** Students often forget to include all the effects of poverty on the economy in their answers.
 - **Solution:** 
 - The effects of poverty on the economy are:
 - Reduced economic growth
 - Increased inequality
 - Decreased human capital
 - Reduced purchasing power
 - Increased dependence on [government](/blog/local-government-class-11-revision-notes-cbse) support
 - The formula to calculate the human poverty index is: $HPI = rac{P_{1} + P_{2} + P_{3}}{3}$
 - where $P_{1}$ is the percentage of population without access to healthcare, $P_{2}$ is the percentage of population without access to education, and $P_{3}$ is the percentage of population below the poverty line.
 - For example, if $P_{1}$ is 20%, $P_{2}$ is 30%, and $P_{3}$ is 40%, then the HPI would be: $HPI = rac{20 + 30 + 40}{3} = 30$
 - **Answer:** The effects of poverty on the economy are reduced economic growth, increased inequality, decreased human capital, reduced purchasing power, and increased dependence on government support.

## <a id="-practice-mcqs"></a>📝 Practice MCQs

**1. What is the primary cause of poverty?**
**A)**   Lack of education and skills
**B)**   Limited access to resources and opportunities
**C)**   Government policies and laws
**D)**   Global economic downturn

**Answer:** D) Global economic downturn can lead to job losses, reduced income, and decreased purchasing power, exacerbating poverty.

---

**2. What is the difference between absolute and relative poverty?**
**A)**   Absolute poverty refers to the lack of basic necessities, while relative poverty is a comparison to others.
**B)**   Absolute poverty is a fixed value, while relative poverty varies with the average income.
**C)**   Absolute poverty is limited to a specific geographic area, while relative poverty is a global issue.
**D)**   Absolute poverty is a temporary condition, while relative poverty is a long-term issue.

**Answer:** A) Absolute poverty refers to the lack of basic necessities, while relative poverty is a comparison to others.

---

**3. What is the term for the number of people unemployed and looking for work?**
**A)**   Unemployment rate
**B)**   Poverty rate
**C)**   Labor force participation rate
**D)**   Underemployment rate

**Answer:** C) Labor force participation rate refers to the number of people employed or actively seeking employment relative to the total population.

---

**4. What is the primary goal of government policies to reduce poverty?**
**A)**   To increase government spending and taxation
**B)**   To provide education and job training programs
**C)**   To redistribute wealth and resources
**D)**   To stimulate economic growth and job creation

**Answer:** D) Stimulating economic growth and job creation can lead to increased employment opportunities, higher incomes, and reduced poverty.

---

**5. What is the term for the percentage of the labor force that is unemployed?**
**A)**   Unemployment rate
**B)**   Poverty rate
**C)**   Inflation rate
**D)**   Economic growth rate

**Answer:** A) The unemployment rate is the percentage of the labor force that is actively seeking employment but unable to find a job.

Introduction to 3D Geometry

3D geometry is a branch of mathematics that deals with the study of three-dimensional figures. It involves the use of vectors, direction cosines, and plane geometry to analyze and solve problems. 3D geometry is an essential topic for students preparing for JEE and NEET exams, as it helps in developing problem-solving skills and spatial reasoning.

Why This Chapter Matters

3D geometry is a crucial chapter for JEE and NEET exams, with 2-3 questions appearing in each. In JEE Mains 2025 Session 1, 2 questions were asked from this topic, and in NEET 2025, 1 question was asked. Understanding 3D geometry concepts is essential for solving problems in physics and engineering. It helps in developing spatial reasoning and visualizing complex shapes.

Core Concepts

Direction Cosines

Direction cosines are the cosines of the angles that a line makes with the positive directions of the coordinate axes. If a line makes angles α\alphaα, β\betaβ, and γ\gammaγ with the xxx-, yyy-, and zzz-axes, respectively, then the direction cosines of the line are given by: cos⁡α=aa2+b2+c2,cos⁡β=ba2+b2+c2,cos⁡γ=ca2+b2+c2\cos \alpha = \frac{a}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}}, \cos \beta = \frac{b}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}}, \cos \gamma = \frac{c}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}}cosα=a2+b2+c2​a​,cosβ=a2+b2+c2​b​,cosγ=a2+b2+c2​c​ where aaa, bbb, and ccc are the direction ratios of the line.

Vectors

Vectors are quantities with both magnitude and direction. They can be added and subtracted, and can be multiplied by scalars. The magnitude of a vector a⃗\vec{a}a is denoted by ∣a⃗∣|\vec{a}|∣a∣ and is given by: ∣a⃗∣=a12+a22+a32|\vec{a}| = \sqrt{a_1^2 + a_2^2 + a_3^2}∣a∣=a12​+a22​+a32​​ where a1a_1a1​, a2a_2a2​, and a3a_3a3​ are the components of the vector.

Plane Geometry

Plane geometry involves the study of planes and lines in three-dimensional space. A plane can be represented by the equation: ax+by+cz+d=0ax + by + cz + d = 0ax+by+cz+d=0 where aaa, bbb, ccc, and ddd are constants.

Shortcut Formula / Trick

One shortcut formula that can be used to solve 3D geometry problems is the formula for the distance between two points in 3D space: d=(x2−x1)2+(y2−y1)2+(z2−z1)2d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2 + (z_2 - z_1)^2}d=(x2​−x1​)2+(y2​−y1​)2+(z2​−z1​)2​ This formula can be used to find the distance between two points, and can also be used to find the distance between a point and a plane.

Trap Questions / Exceptions

Here are a few trap questions and exceptions that students should be aware of:

  • Wrong answer: cos⁡α=aa2+b2\cos \alpha = \frac{a}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}}cosα=a2+b2​a​ Right answer: cos⁡α=aa2+b2+c2\cos \alpha = \frac{a}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}}cosα=a2+b2+c2​a​ Why students get it wrong: Students often forget to include the ccc term in the denominator.
  • Wrong answer: ∣a⃗∣=a12+a22|\vec{a}| = \sqrt{a_1^2 + a_2^2}∣a∣=a12​+a22​​ Right answer: ∣a⃗∣=a12+a22+a32|\vec{a}| = \sqrt{a_1^2 + a_2^2 + a_3^2}∣a∣=a12​+a22​+a32​​ Why students get it wrong: Students often forget to include the a3a_3a3​ term in the expression for the magnitude.

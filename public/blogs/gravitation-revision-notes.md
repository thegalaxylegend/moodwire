## Introduction
Why does an apple fall to the Ground but the Moon stay in the sky? For centuries, these were thought to be two different problems. Isaac Newton's genius was in realizing they are actually the exact same thing! "Gravitation" is the study of the force that holds the universe together. From the falling of a leaf to the rotation of galaxies, the same simple laws apply. These revision notes will help you master the elegant mathematics of gravity and the planetary motion laws that describe our place in the cosmos.

## 1. Newton’s Universal Law of Gravitation
Every particle in the universe attracts every other particle with a force that is directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.
- **Formula:** $F = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}$
- **Universal Gravitational Constant ($G$):** $6.67 \times 10^{-11} \text{ N m}^2/\text{kg}^2$. 

> [!IMPORTANT]
> $G$ is a "universal" constant—it is the same everywhere in the universe. Do not confuse it with $g$, which changes depending on where you are!

## 2. Kepler’s Laws of Planetary Motion
Before Newton, Johannes Kepler discovered three laws that describe how planets move around the Sun:
1.  **Law of Orbits:** All planets move in elliptical orbits with the Sun at one of the foci.
2.  **Law of Areas:** A line that connects a planet to the Sun sweeps out equal areas in equal times. (This means planets move faster when closer to the Sun).
3.  **Law of Periods:** The square of the time period of a planet ($T^2$) is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis of its orbit ($a^3$).
    - **Relationship:** $T^2 \propto a^3$

## 3. Acceleration due to Gravity ($g$)
While $G$ is constant, the acceleration due to gravity ($g$) on Earth varies.
- **Formula at Surface:** $g = \frac{GM}{R^2} \approx 9.8 \text{ m/s}^2$.

### Factors Affecting $g$:
- **Height ($h$):** $g$ decreases as you go higher. $g_h \approx g (1 - \frac{2h}{R})$ for $h \ll R$.
- **Depth ($d$):** $g$ decreases as you go deeper into the Earth. $g_d = g (1 - \frac{d}{R})$.
- **Latitude:** Earth's rotation and its bulge at the equator make $g$ slightly lower at the equator and higher at the poles.

## 4. Gravitational Potential Energy
The gravitational potential energy of a system of two masses $M$ and $m$ separated by distance $r$ is:
- **Formula:** $U = -\frac{GMm}{r}$

> [!WARNING]
> The negative sign is crucial! it signifies that the gravitational force is **attractive**, and work must be done to move the masses far apart. Energy is zero only at infinity.

## 5. Escape Speed and Orbital Velocity
How fast do you need to go to leave Earth forever?

-   **Escape Speed ($v_e$):** The minimum speed required for an object to escape the gravitational pull of a planet.
    - **Formula:** $v_e = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}} = \sqrt{2gR}$
    - **For Earth:** $\approx 11.2 \text{ km/s}$.
-   **Orbital Velocity ($v_o$):** The speed required to keep a satellite in a circular orbit at height $h$.
    - **Formula:** $v_o = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{R+h}}$

## 6. Geostationary and Polar Satellites
1.  **Geostationary Satellites:** They appear fixed at a certain point above the Earth. Their time period is 24 hours (same as Earth's rotation). Used for telecommunications.
2.  **Polar Satellites:** They orbit in north-south directions over the poles. Used for weather monitoring and remote sensing.

**Weightlessness:** When an object is in free fall (like a satellite in orbit), any internal support forces vanish, creating the sensation of weightlessness.

## Conclusion
Gravitation is a beautiful example of how a single formula can explain the entire universe. By understanding $1/r^2$ relationships, you gain insight into not just gravity, but also electricity and magnetism later on. Mastery of $g$ variation and escape velocity is essential for competitive exams—these are "favorite" topics for paper setters!

## FAQ Section
**Q1: Why doesn't the Moon fall into the Earth?**
A: The Moon *is* constantly falling toward Earth, but it has enough horizontal "tangential" speed that as it falls, the Earth's surface curves away beneath it. It stays in a state of continuous fall without ever hitting the ground.

**Q2: Is the value of $g$ zero at the center of the Earth?**
A: Yes. At the center ($d = R$), the gravitational pulls from all directions cancel out, making the net acceleration due to gravity zero.

**Q3: Does escape velocity depend on the mass of the object?**
A: No. Looking at the formula $v_e = \sqrt{2GM/R}$, you'll see it only depends on the mass and radius of the planet, not the object being launched. A pebble and a rocket need the same speed to escape.
